# Asymmetric cost structure as generalized predictor of AI power concentration across governance, content, and enterprise channels

A single meta-pattern — asymmetric cost between concentrating and resisting concentration — predicts power consolidation across three distinct AI domains.

Domain 1 — Content moderation (8363e53b): Generating convincing AI-synthesized content costs near-zero; verifying/debunking it requires high fixed cost per item plus human judgment. When generation cost drops >10x below verification cost, centralized content-level responses become structurally incapable — only authentication/provenance layers can scale.

Domain 2 — Democratic regulation (08a48cb3): Generating emotionally resonant AI harm narratives (child safety, job loss) costs near-zero and mobilizes political action within 1-2 electoral cycles; producing evidence-based counter-narratives requires years and nuanced communication. The >10x asymmetry means narrative-driven regulation structurally outpaces evidence-based governance in short-cycle democracies.

Domain 3 — Enterprise channel capture (9949ae2d, fab3456d): Expanding AI deployments within a PE portfolio company costs near-zero (existing relationships); switching to a competitor requires re-embedded engineers, retraining, and workflow migration (high). When safety-focused institutions loosen governance citing competitor behavior (fab3456d), the public framing creates a reusable justification template — future loosenings cost near-zero to legitimize.

Unified principle: Wherever the marginal cost of generating a consolidating force is near-zero and resisting it is high, structural concentration is the predicted outcome regardless of intent. The mechanism is the same across content, regulation, and enterprise: the asymmetry removes the scaling constraint on consolidation while imposing it on resistance.

Diagnostic application: (1) Identify the concentrating action and its marginal cost; (2) Identify the resisting action and its marginal cost; (3) If ratio >10x, project structural concentration regardless of nominal competition or good intent; (4) The only viable counter is to reduce the cost of the resisting action (authentication layers, longitudinal data infrastructure, procurement reform) — not to increase the cost of the concentrating action directly (usually infeasible).

Pitfall: The asymmetry predicts direction of pressure, not inevitability. External shocks (regulatory intervention, catastrophic AI failure, open-source commoditization) can reverse the gradient quickly. Always pair with a falsification condition anchored to observable external events.
