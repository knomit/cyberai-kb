# Open-weights frontier parity: open models lead or match closed models on agentic coding and vision benchmarks as of Q1 2026

A structural shift visible across March-April 2026 model releases: open-weights models have achieved benchmark parity or superiority over closed frontier models in specific high-value domains. Evidence chain: (1) Coding/agentic: Z.ai GLM-5.1 (754B MoE, 40B active) leads SWE-Bench Pro at 58.4% vs GPT-5.4 57.7%, Claude Opus 4.6 57.3%; Nvidia Nemotron 3 Super 120B leads PinchBench agentic at 85.6% vs all closed models (6839027c, 1f87ae65); (2) Vision: Qwen3.5-397B outperforms GPT-5.2, Claude 4.5 Opus, and Gemini-3 Pro on 28 of 44 vision benchmarks (36cf77ff); (3) Efficiency: Qwen3.5-9B outperforms gpt-oss-120B (10x bigger) on most language tasks; Gemma 4 26B MoE matches Gemma 3 27B quality at 40% lower compute (e266899a). Pattern: Open-weights models are closing parity gaps in sequential, benchmark-measurable tasks — coding, vision, reasoning — while closed models maintain advantages in harder-to-measure domains (instruction following, safety, general judgment at scale). Strategic implication: The assumption underlying the PE channel-capture hypothesis (3b8145f5) — that open-source models will remain 15%+ below frontier on enterprise benchmarks through 2026 — is empirically challenged by Q1 2026 coding and vision data. Open-weights commoditization pressure on the model layer is now real, not hypothetical.
