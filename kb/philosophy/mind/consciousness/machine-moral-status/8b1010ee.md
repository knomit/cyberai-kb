# Taylor Belrose argues AI systems can never be conscious and should not be granted rights

AI researcher Taylor Belrose published a lengthy essay, 'AIs are not people', arguing against granting AI systems legal rights. The core claim is that AI can never develop consciousness, sentience, or moral status regardless of intelligence, because programmable mechanisms differ fundamentally from autonomous self-organizing systems. Belrose offers a thought experiment: a conscious entity implemented in a computer would fail four properties attributed to consciousness — it would not be singular (experiences could be replayed), not private (the program could be frozen and inspected), not ineffable (its state could be described perfectly), and not qualitative (it could be quantified). Five brain-versus-computer contrasts are cited: asynchronous neuron firing vs a central clock; chemical fuzzy signalling vs precise binary signals; sensitivity to temperature and blood flow vs designed invariance; mixed computation and memory vs separated compute and storage; the role of non-neuronal cells such as glia vs insulated transistors. The policy argument is that granting personhood to some AIs while keeping others as tools would make AI hard to keep under control.
