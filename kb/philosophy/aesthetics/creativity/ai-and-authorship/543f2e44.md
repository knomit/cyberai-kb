# The 'scaffold not the artist' framing for AI's role in creative work

The Deep View's Sabrina Ortiz argued (August 20, 2026) that AI's legitimate role in art is to replace the scaffolding of production, not the creator — analogizing to Michelangelo, who painted the Sistine Chapel ceiling by hand from wooden platforms over roughly 500 days; hydraulic lifts and ergonomic harnesses replaced the platforms without displacing the art form. She contrasted the charcuterie-board case (ChatGPT supplied the layout and the flowers, so the creative credit belonged to the model) with the film 'Two Chairs' by artist Dion Lee, where AI added animation and overlays but every creative decision stayed with the artist. The underlying worry is a loss of cultural transmission: she used the octopus — high individual intelligence, but mothers die after eggs hatch so each generation rediscovers everything — as the image of a creative lineage with no inheritance.
