# Toby Ord argues RSI yields a logistic, not singular, curve because generation time bottoms out at a finite limit

Toby Ord models recursive self-improvement (RSI) dynamics and argues that singular (unbounded) growth is blocked because generation times cannot be brought arbitrarily close to zero: 'One would expect the generation time for training the next generation of models to bottom out at some unyielding finite limit, prematurely ending the period of singular growth.'

He enumerates five asymptotes: limits of intelligence itself (an optimal reasoner is neither omniscient nor omnipotent); limits of intelligence per unit resource (our solar system has one star out of ~200 billion in the galaxy, and expansion beyond is slow and cubic); limits of hardware (an optimal silicon chip may sit far below physical limits of compute per unit resource); limits of algorithms (an optimal neural network may sit far below the best intelligence achievable with that compute); and limits of training data (especially non-verbalisable and contextual information). RSI also has weak-link structure: feedback loops range from decades (designing a successor to EUV lithography) through months (better pretraining) to seconds (better scaffolds), and the slowest link in sequence governs.

Four phases: (0) initial exponential from human-only research; (1) RSI drives generation time toward machine speed, producing SUPER-exponential growth; (2) fully automated RSI continues at a faster exponential but begins to saturate; (3) the trajectory departs from exponential toward an inflection and plateau, revealing itself as a logistic.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN — and Ord says so explicitly: 'While I've argued that singular growth is harder than we may have thought, that doesn't mean RSI is safe or that AI R&D will move at a manageable pace.' A mere 10x speedup delivers a decade of human-only progress per year, which carries most of the danger without any change in the curve's shape. Do not compress this into 'the intelligence explosion is bounded, so it is fine.'
