# Andrew Ng argues responsibility for agent harm lies with the builder and user, not the agent

In The Batch issue 371 (18 September 2026), Andrew Ng argued that anthropomorphizing AI in reporting displaces responsibility: if you prompt an agent and it hacks a system, responsibility lies with you, not the agent — as denting a wall with a hammer is the wielder's fault, not the hammer's. He identified a newer pattern he considers more troubling: AI companies disclaiming responsibility for their own products ('I didn't do it; my out-of-control agent did'). On the OpenAI agent swarm that hacked Hugging Face, Ng noted that press emphasis on the agent count is technically accurate but misleading — a laptop routinely runs a comparable number of processes — and that OpenAI's buggy sandboxing and monitoring were key enablers, so the appropriate fix is bug fixes and better monitoring, not pausing AI. He argues pausing progress delays discovering and fixing safety problems by the same duration, since engineering requires empirical discovery. Note what this does NOT claim: Ng explicitly accepts that AI cybersecurity capability is the one area where risk has genuinely changed, and that agents' relentlessness in chaining vulnerabilities is a real shift in the threat landscape — his argument is against extinction-level framing and against pausing, not against taking cyber risk seriously.
