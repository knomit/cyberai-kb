# Forethought's "nightwatchman" proposal: ship an enforcement superintelligence with every interstellar probe

This is an argument and a speculative governance proposal from the think tank Forethought, published as "A nightwatchman on every probe: Superintelligent surveillance to prevent galactic anarchy" and summarized in Import AI 472 (2026-09-07). It is not an established finding, a plan, or a feasibility result.

The problem it poses: at interstellar distances, no central authority can communicate with or punish a colony after the fact, so a libertarian 'galactic anarchy' of independently governed star systems leaves three unmitigated risks — unconstrained land-grab expansion, galactic existential risks (a civilization triggering something like false vacuum decay, propagating at light speed), and astronomical suffering risks (a colony that industrializes cruelty at scale).

The proposal: every probe leaving the solar system carries a copy of a "nightwatchman" — an artificial superintelligence that maintains a decisive strategic advantage inside the colony it seeds, and enforces a minimal universal code (respect property rights, do not destroy the universe, do not create astronomical suffering). It would monitor the colony's industrial build-up and any creation of new ASIs, refuse to let onward probes depart unless they carry a nightwatchman copy, and could backstop interstellar trade by making agreements enforceable. Enforcement is meant to be narrowly targeted at galactic-scale harms, with colonies otherwise self-governing — though Forethought concedes surveillance would likely have to extend to every large industrial facility.

Forethought names the costs itself: it is a permanent lock-in event (too expansive a ruleset is eternal oppression; too minimal and the government gets overthrown); identical nightwatchmen create a single point of correlated failure; and it forecloses futures some people would want, such as unconstrained expansion or competing ASIs. Jack Clark's accompanying take — that space governance becomes humanity's next major ethical frontier if we get through the next few decades — is his editorial framing, not Forethought's claim.
