# AI's binding constraint is migrating from compute to electricity and public consent — and regulation is now forcing the vertical-integration step

A cluster of mid-2026 facts supports a claim stronger than 'data centers are unpopular': the limiting input on AI scaling is shifting from silicon to POWER, and from power to the political consent required to site it.

**The consent constraint is now quantified and binding.** Gallup (May 2026): ~7 in 10 Americans oppose data center construction in their area, nearly 50% strongly. Separately, 55% believe AI will do more harm than good. This has stopped being sentiment and become law: on July 14, 2026 New York became the first US state to impose a moratorium — up to one year — on hyperscale AI data centers (>50 MW), citing power costs, water supply, and noise. Public opinion at these levels is not a PR problem for a facility that needs local zoning approval; it is a veto.

**The critical detail is not the ban — it's the clause.** Hochul's order requires data centers to EITHER PRODUCE THEIR OWN ENERGY OR PAY A PREMIUM for grid power, and states they will never be exempt from local zoning. This is regulation explicitly pricing self-supply below grid access. It closes a loop with the vertical-integration thesis (kb/business/ai-infrastructure/predictions/energy-integration/808a8664.md): the prediction that labs move from CONTRACTING for power (PPAs) to OWNING generation was argued from interconnect queue lengths and one instance (Musk's ~$1B APR Energy acquisition, >1 GW). New York supplies a second, independent forcing function — a regulatory one. Where the interconnect argument said contracting is too SLOW, the New York clause says contracting is too EXPENSIVE and politically exposed. Two independent mechanisms pushing the same direction is why that hypothesis was raised to 0.5 in this review.

**The pattern repeats internationally with different content.** China's July 15, 2026 ban on companion chatbots is nominally AI safety but is substantially demographic policy — the population shrank a fourth consecutive year, and regulators warned in September 2025 that AI could transform 'traditional views on employment, fertility, and education.' The common structure across NY and Beijing: **AI is being regulated as a SOCIETAL-IMPACT technology (land, water, electricity, birth rates), not as an information technology.** That is a different regulatory tradition with different instruments — zoning boards, utility commissions, family policy — and it operates independently of frontier-model governance debates.

**The tension this creates.** US frontier-model oversight remains ad hoc and federally reluctant ('there will not be an FDA for AI' — Krishnan), while binding, enforceable AI constraints are being written by governors and utility regulators over physical externalities. The action is at the substrate, not the model.

**Counter-pressure, and epistemic status.** Meta is actively contesting the consent frame (pitching its 5GW Richland Parish site as a community win), and the edge/robotics direction (NVIDIA Jetson Thor) routes some inference around data centers entirely. Confidence 0.65: the New York moratorium is ONE state, is time-limited to a year, and is an executive order rather than statute — it may prove an outlier rather than a template. The Gallup and 55%-harm figures come via newsletter summary rather than direct examination of the polls' wording and methodology.
