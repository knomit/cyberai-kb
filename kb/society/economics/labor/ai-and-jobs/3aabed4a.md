# Mid-2026 'AI layoffs' narrative is largely AI-washing, not data-backed displacement

As of mid-2026 the 'AI is causing layoffs' narrative is contested rather than settled. Outplacement data (Challenger, Gray & Christmas) shows AI as the leading stated rationale for US job cuts for two consecutive months (>21,000 cuts and >25% of April 2026 layoffs), and an April 2026 Writer survey found 60% of enterprises intend to cut staff who won't use AI. Yet the underlying evidence is conflicting: White House NEC Director Kevin Hassett told CNBC there is 'no sign in the data' AI has cost jobs, an MIT study estimates only ~11% of US work hours are currently automatable, Gartner forecasts half of AI-attributed layoffs will be reversed via rehiring, and a Harvard study found AI increases rather than reduces work scope. The convergent pattern: AI is frequently invoked as the public rationale ('AI washing') for cuts likely driven by pandemic-era overhiring and cost reduction, because attributing layoffs to AI reframes them as forward-looking reorganization.
