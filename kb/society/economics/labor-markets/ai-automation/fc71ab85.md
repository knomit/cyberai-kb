# Goldman Sachs (Aug 2026): AI hiring headwinds are real but confined to a narrow set of industries

A Goldman Sachs report published Wednesday, August 19, 2026 found that AI is beginning to press labor markets in major economies, with industries more exposed to AI automation showing slowed job growth over the past four years. Job growth in information and communication services has slowed across all major economies since 2022, covering white-collar work such as call centers, software publishing, management consulting and advertising. Call center employment is down 39% relative to the historical average in the US, 33% in Canada, and 27% in Germany. Entry-level workers in AI-exposed occupations are hit hardest. AI adoption rates rose 15–20% in developed markets (led by France, the US, the Netherlands and the UK) and 10–15% in emerging markets. Authors Sarah Dong (macro research analyst) and Joseph Briggs (economist) concluded that AI-related hiring headwinds are clearly visible in employment data but impacts are limited to a narrow set of industries and workers.
