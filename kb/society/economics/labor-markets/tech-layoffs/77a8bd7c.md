# US firms drove 82% of ~157,000 global tech layoffs in H1 2026, about half tied to AI restructuring

A report from UK research firm TradingPlatforms found that about 82% of the 156,975 tech jobs eliminated globally in the first half of 2026 came from US companies, with nearly half of those cuts tied to US firms restructuring around AI and automation (led by Oracle, Meta, Block, Cisco and PayPal). The analysis drew on TrueUp.io, Layoffs.fyi, TechCrunch and state WARN filings. Cloud/SaaS companies recorded the most layoffs globally; e-commerce/marketplace firms (Amazon, eBay) were second.

Specific 2026 cuts named: Microsoft ~4,800 jobs on July 6 (1,600 in Xbox); Oracle ~21,000 over the prior year; Meta ~8,000; Cloudflare ~1,100. Ford rehired 350+ veteran engineers after its AI-powered quality systems could not perform on their own.

SCOPE NOTE: the precise figure (156,975) is H1 2026; secondary coverage restated it as "nearly 160,000 tech layoffs in 2026" without the half-year qualifier — do not read it as a full-year total.

COUNTERVAILING EVIDENCE, which the headline number alone hides: some estimates find companies investing in AI GREW headcount by as much as 10%, with entry-level hiring rising 12%, and other research finds AI increases the hours and scope of work rather than narrowing it. The layoff share is therefore not by itself evidence that AI is net-reducing tech employment.
