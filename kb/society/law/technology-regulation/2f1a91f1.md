# ChatGPT for Teens reads as a compliance response to converging US child-safety enforcement, against low classroom AI literacy

OpenAI launched ChatGPT for Teens on Aug 18, 2026 with automatic enrollment for self-reported or system-estimated ages 13-17 and default protections (blocking romantic/sexualized roleplay, discouraging emotional dependence, not implying feelings or consciousness) plus parent controls including eating-disorder notifications. AI risk-management attorney Lily Li ties the timing to three concurrent pressures: the $942M New Mexico ruling ordering Meta to pay and overhaul child protections, California SB-243 regulating companion chatbots including minor safety protocols, and the FTC inquiry into AI chatbots as companions. The efficacy gap is measurable: a Common Sense Media survey (published Aug 17, 2026) found 70% of teens use AI for schoolwork but only 30% have had a teacher discuss safe AI use, and Common Sense's Robbie Torney noted parental controls historically fail most families because people do not understand or adopt them. Li also flagged California AB 1043, which will require operating systems to send age signals, arguing OS-level signals are a more reliable age indicator than model-side estimation — i.e. the enrollment mechanism ChatGPT for Teens currently depends on is the weakest link. WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: Common Sense Media had not tested ChatGPT for Teens at time of comment; no claim is made here about the product's actual safety performance.
