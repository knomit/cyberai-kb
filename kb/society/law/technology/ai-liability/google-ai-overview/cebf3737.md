# Munich court held Google liable for defamatory AI Overview output

The Regional Court of Munich ruled that Google can be held liable for defamatory statements generated by its AI Overview feature. When users searched German publisher Verlagshaus24 or subsidiary GeraMond plus the autocomplete-suggested word 'scam', AI Overview asserted the companies were known for dubious/fraudulent business practices (subscription traps, poor service) — claims the court found false, resulting from AI confusing them with other companies. The court held that AI Overviews were 'independent, new, and substantive statements' that Google presented as its own speech, so Google bore liability, and rejected Google's argument that users can verify AI output. It issued an immediate temporary injunction to stop disseminating the statements, ordered Google to pay 80% of litigation costs, and threatened fines up to $285,000 for violations. Google plans to appeal to Germany's Federal Court of Justice. One analysis found inaccuracies in roughly 10% of Google AI Overview results.
