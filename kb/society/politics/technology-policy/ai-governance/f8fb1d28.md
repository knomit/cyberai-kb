# Demis Hassabis proposed a FINRA-style independent standards body for frontier AI

On 2026-07-14, Google DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis published a ~1,500-word essay titled 'A Framework for Frontier AI and the Dawning of a New Age', moving past general calls for AI safety to a concrete governance proposal. His four recommendations: (1) create an independent standards body — a US-led, federally overseen public-private organization run by leading technical experts, modeled on FINRA, to develop, update and enforce frontier model evaluations; (2) require mandatory pre-deployment evaluations for cybersecurity, biological threats, deception, agentic capabilities, and national security issues; (3) raise the bar for frontier labs — publish technical documentation, invest substantially in AI safety, vet key hires, maintain strong cybersecurity, and address vulnerabilities systematically; (4) build an adaptable international framework with global cooperation, independent auditors, and the retained ability to coordinate a slowdown in frontier development via pre-deployment approvals if risks become severe. Hassabis argued the US is well positioned to take the first step given its economic and technical standing — notable given the three biggest frontier labs (Google, OpenAI, Anthropic) are headquartered there. He said funding 'would need to be substantial and likely mostly come from industry' to attract world-class technical talent and provide compute for large-scale testing. Chris Canal, CEO of AI safety evaluator Equistamp, responded that such a body 'should publish predictions of what the next generation of models will score before release, and be graded publicly on its accuracy. A testing regime that can't predict is [only] measuring the past.'
