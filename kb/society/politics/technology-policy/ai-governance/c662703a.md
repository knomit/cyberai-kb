# When the regulated party authors the rule, the rule protects its position regardless of the sincerity of the safety case

MECHANISM: when the parties who would be bound by a rule are also the ones drafting and advocating it, the rule that emerges is one compatible with their existing position — and this holds independently of whether the stated justification is sincere, because the selection pressure acts on which proposals get made, not on the motives behind them.

Two independent lines of argument in the September 2026 AI-pacing debate converge on this shape. One argues from the incumbent's economics: the specific rules frontier labs propose also protect existing investments, preserve price premiums and defer competitive spending, and a lab cannot economically restrain itself unilaterally because the first to slow loses position — which makes advocating enforced regulation structurally rational for an incumbent whatever its intentions. The other argues from competition policy: allowing a small number of dominant firms to set global AI rules risks entrenching them, on the historical pattern of monopolies limiting competition under safety pretexts, and the proposed remedy is diverse international rule-making with transparency, mandatory testing and independent assurance.

LOAD-BEARING QUALIFIERS, carried from both sources and not to be dropped: (1) the claim is about incentive alignment, NOT an allegation of bad faith — many advocates are undoubtedly well-intentioned, and the argument does not depend on them not being; (2) the competition-policy line is a position piece from an interested party, a competitor to the firms it criticizes, and is to be weighed as an argument rather than a neutral assessment. Neither source establishes that any proposed rule is in fact bad, only that its provenance is not evidence that it is good.

CONSUMER CONSEQUENCE: assess a self-proposed rule on whether its content would bind the proposer against its own interest, not on the proposer's stated rationale — and read the framing of a proposal as more predictive than its specific content, since the framing is the template that governs the next proposal.
