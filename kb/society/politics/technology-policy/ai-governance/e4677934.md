# An underspecified proposal draws broad endorsement precisely because each party fills the term with its own meaning — the agreement is verbal, not substantive

MECHANISM: when a proposal's central term names a direction without specifying a magnitude or a binding condition, parties with incompatible goals can each endorse it while meaning something different, so breadth of endorsement measures the term's emptiness rather than the existence of agreement.

The September 2026 frontier-pacing episode instantiates this twice over. On the analytic side, several distinct camps — motivated respectively by model interpretability, worker interests, economic growth, geopolitical strategy, and resistance to new regulation — all adopted the same word while never converging on an actual speed. On the observed side, the endorsement pattern scrambled normal coalitions: commercial rivals aligned with each other, figures from opposite political poles appeared together to press lawmakers, and two governments pushed back independently — while equity markets in chip, capital-equipment and investor names fell, pricing the risk of an actual slowdown that the verbal consensus had not in fact agreed to.

LOAD-BEARING QUALIFIERS carried from the members: coalition announcements should NOT be read as agreement on any concrete limit; a prior attempt to bound training compute failed, which is evidence of how hard controlling innovation speed is in practice rather than evidence that anyone has now succeeded; and the observed material is about the reception and fallout of the proposal, not about the content of the framework proposed.

TEST FOR A CONSUMER, following the corpus's settlement rule: restate the claim's variable and the confirming observation's variable side by side. 'Many parties endorsed pacing' settles that a word was adopted; it does not settle that any party will slow down. Read the framing a party gives its endorsement as more predictive than the endorsement itself, since the framing is the template that governs what it does next.
