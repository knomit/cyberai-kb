# Anthropic, Google and OpenAI discussed an AI industry standards body before the September 2026 pacing fight

CNN reported on Monday September 14, 2026 that Anthropic, Google and OpenAI had discussed creating an AI industry standards body even before the September 2026 pacing controversy, motivated in part by Demis Hassabis's July 2026 essay laying out an ambitious AI safety plan. This matters for reading the pacing debate: the standards-body idea was not improvised in response to the Amodei essay or the market reaction. It does not establish that any lab has actually slowed its own development.

UPDATE (The Information, reported Sept 25, 2026): the three companies are reportedly pushing forward with the plan, hoping to launch by end of 2026 or early 2027. The group's potential name is the 'Standards Authority for Frontier AI.' Candidates considered for CEO include Sriram Krishnan (top AI policy adviser in the Trump administration) and Arati Prabhakar (former director of the US Office of Science and Technology Policy under Biden); the group is also considering people for chair and scientific-adviser roles. The body would aim to support third-party model testing, set qualifications for model/lab auditors, and help define labs' voluntary safety commitments; it remains unclear whether it would conduct testing itself, a role currently held by the US Center for AI Standards and Innovation (housed within NIST) -- there is reported concern within the group about whether that agency has adequate resources. A related executive order that would have established a government AI regulatory body was reportedly halted by opposition from Nvidia's Jensen Huang and Meta's Mark Zuckerberg.
