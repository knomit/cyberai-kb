# Third-party AI evaluation without standards, disclosure channels and enforcement degrades into a checkbox exercise

Miranda Bogen, director of the governance lab at the Center for Democracy and Technology, told The Deep View that outside testers 'can't change companies' behavior without a complementary suite of tools.' She named four complements that must accompany third-party evaluation: measurement standards, channels to communicate failed safety checks to relevant external stakeholders, a mandatory obligation to fix deficiencies, and a clear allocation of responsibilities. Without all four, evaluations end up as a checkbox exercise. Bogen cited the financial industry and aviation as contexts that repeatedly demonstrated the limits of third-party assessment. The implication: an evaluation regime with no leverage over a failing lab — no requirement to fix the model or withhold release — is a symbolic good-faith measure, not risk mitigation. This does NOT mean third-party evaluation is worthless; Bogen calls it a critical piece of the puzzle, just not a sufficient one.
