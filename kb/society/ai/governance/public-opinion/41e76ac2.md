# CSAIP poll of ~56,000 Americans: AI job-transition policies poll far better than UBI or a sovereign wealth fund

The Center for Shared AI Prosperity (CSAIP) polled about 56,000 Americans on 79 distinct AI-related economic policy ideas, publishing ahead of the U.S. midterms and covered in Import AI 472 (2026-09-07). Headline result: 61 of the 79 economic policies retained net approval even after respondents were shown strong opposition arguments, and the dozen top ideas all held an approval margin of at least +40 points against opposition framing.

Top three by net margin: expand apprenticeships (+66), require severance for workers whose jobs are automated away (+63), and sector-based job training (+60). Bottom three: a U.S. sovereign wealth fund (-51), a tax on distributed profits (-33), and universal basic income (-33). The pattern CSAIP reports is that straightforwardly redistributive measures — moving resources from corporations and the wealthiest to workers and families — and measures that specifically ascribe responsibility to AI companies for disruption they cause poll strongly, including taxing AI profits to fund Social Security and Medicare; novel macro-structural instruments poll badly.

Foreseeable misreading: this measures stated support under survey framings, not policy effectiveness and not legislative feasibility. CSAIP states the caveat itself: a popular policy is not the same as an effective policy, and the polling shows where the public is already open to an idea versus where a bold idea would need strong organizing to gain traction.
