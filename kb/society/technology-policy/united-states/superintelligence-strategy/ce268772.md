# RAND recommends a 'Freedom of Action' US superintelligence strategy built on four investments and five uncertainties

RAND published a perspective arguing that because the path of the AI takeoff is unknown, the United States should pursue a 'Freedom of Action' strategy whose stated purpose is 'to build and preserve the ability to secure U.S. geopolitical advantage and ensure humanity's survival with agency through the transition to Superintelligence'. All FOUR ingredients are required, not any one: (1) build a human-AI ecosystem — invest in AI safety, tools preserving human agency, benefit-sharing, societal preparation, and shaping ecosystem incentives; (2) build an AI-security architecture — tools to see the frontier at home and abroad including compute visibility, verification technology for agreements, and regulatory expertise; (3) adapt legacy national-security institutions for the AI era; (4) invest in the response capacity of citizens, firms and governments — decision-maker information, diffusion of positive uses, break-glass crisis plans, and AI literacy.

RAND frames seven archetypal strategies in three families: Coexistence (Dominance, Co-Development, Preparedness), Denial (Moratorium, Deterrence, Continuity of Society), and Acceleration. Which one is right depends on five uncertainties: danger proximity, coexistence feasibility, restraint feasibility, whether decisive strategic advantage is achievable, and suppression feasibility.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: Freedom of Action is not a recommendation to pick one of the seven archetypes now — it is a recommendation to spend money preserving OPTIONALITY across them, which is a real and substantial cost, not a way of deferring cost.
