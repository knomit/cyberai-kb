# Agent failures carry higher stakes than chatbot or ad-targeting failures

Miranda Bogen, director of the governance lab at the Center for Democracy and Technology, told The Deep View for its 2026-09-09 piece on Meta's Muse that consumer agents require 'an enormous amount of private data to function' and that the consequences of their failure exceed those of prior consumer-data harms: 'With minimal input from users, these systems could take actions that damage someone's reputation, leak sensitive medical information, or even deplete bank accounts. Given the stakes, the assurances companies are offering around reliability and privacy seem concerningly inadequate.' The structural point generalizes beyond Meta: a system that takes autonomous action on connected accounts has a materially different risk profile from one that only produces text or targets ads, so privacy assurances calibrated to the latter do not transfer. Context specific to Meta's credibility here: in August 2026 Meta paid $18 billion to settle a 29-state lawsuit alleging it knowingly designed Instagram and Facebook to addict children, on top of the Cambridge Analytica history.
