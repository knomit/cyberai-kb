# 'Racing to Ruin' models when rival AI firms can coordinate a slowdown

Researchers at MIT and Columbia ('Racing to Ruin', arXiv) modeled R&D competition between AI-firm duopolists 'in the shadow of disaster', where scaling raises the hazard of a catastrophic event. Their game-theoretic analysis finds the two key variables for a stable slowdown are transparency about technology development and the ability to model rivals as trustworthy rational actors. With low trust every equilibrium 'races to ruin' (disaster with probability one); with high trust the probability that two rational firms race forever vanishes. Transparency is 'double-edged' and can destroy then restore early-stopping equilibria.
