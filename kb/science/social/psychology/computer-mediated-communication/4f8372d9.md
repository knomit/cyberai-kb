# In text messaging, the period carries negative social tone independent of wording, and a 2025 study extends the effect with denser punctuation

Two studies nine years apart establish that in text messaging, terminal punctuation functions as a social-tone signal rather than a neutral orthographic convention — readers extract affect from the punctuation itself, holding the words constant.

2016, Binghamton University: text messages ENDING with a period were rated by readers as LESS SINCERE than otherwise identical messages without one.

2025 follow-up: placing a period AFTER EVERY WORD (e.g. 'What. Do. You. Need.') raised readers' perceived MEANNESS rating of the message. The 2025 record explicitly frames itself as extending the earlier terminal-punctuation and sincerity finding.

THE JOINT CLAIM, stated no wider than the sources support: more period-marking is read as more socially negative, across two different studies and two different rating dimensions.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN — the distinction matters and collapsing it would overstate the result. SINCERITY and MEANNESS are DIFFERENT dependent variables measured in different studies; these are not two points on one calibrated scale, and nothing here establishes an intermediate dose-response curve between 'one terminal period' and 'a period after every word'. Both findings are about READERS' PERCEPTIONS of messages, not about what senders actually intended or felt — a sender who punctuates properly is not thereby insincere. Neither record states the language or population beyond the 2016 study's Binghamton University origin, so generalization beyond English-language texting is not established here.
