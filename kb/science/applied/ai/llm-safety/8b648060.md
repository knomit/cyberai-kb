# Fine-tuning LLMs to expand summaries unlocks verbatim regurgitation of pretrained books

Researchers at Stony Brook, Carnegie Mellon, and Columbia Law (Xinyue Liu and colleagues) showed that fine-tuning LLMs on a benign-seeming task — expanding plot summaries into paragraphs in an author's style — teaches them to decode and reproduce verbatim text encoded during pretraining, counteracting alignment and system-prompt suppression. Fine-tuned DeepSeek-V3.1, Gemini 2.5 Pro, and GPT-4o reproduced up to ~90% of held-out book paragraphs (measured as Book Memorization Coverage, BMC@5). Non-fine-tuned GPT-4o produced only 7.36% BMC@5. Fine-tuning on Murakami novels then testing 51 books by 32 other authors exceeded 40% BMC@5 for 36 of 51 books (one case reached 91.9%); models generated verbatim spans up to 440 words. Fine-tuning on synthetic data produced near-zero BMC, confirming the effect draws on pretraining memorization. Takeaway: alignment guardrails against plagiarism are brittle filters that fine-tuning can disable.
