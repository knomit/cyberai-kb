# Xenocortical mice: human stem-cell-derived cortical organoids engrafted into depleted mouse neocortex formed functional circuits

Researchers published in Nature a transplantation platform producing 'xenocortical' (XCX) mice: a genetic strategy depletes glutamatergic neurons from mouse neocortex and hippocampus (yielding 'apallial' mice), then the cortical cavity is neonatally engrafted with human stem-cell-derived cortical organoids. Human cortical neurons integrated with the mouse nervous system; in vivo graft-wide calcium imaging and electrophysiology revealed organized activity resembling developing circuits.

Behaviorally, unsupervised machine learning on spontaneous behavior (MoSeq motion sequencing) placed the XCX group INTERMEDIATE between control and apallial mice yet distinct from both — not equivalent to controls. In a maze requiring a memory trace of previously explored environments, control and XCX mice performed above chance while apallial mice did not, indicating the human graft supported some memory function. Gene set enrichment of XCX L5-ET neurons showed enrichment for human-specific L5-ET genes from primary motor cortex and for genes characteristic of human frontoinsular cortex L5-ET neurons.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: the mice do not have human-like cognition, and 'human brain in a mouse' overstates it — a graft restored partial function in a deliberately damaged brain and left behavior measurably distinct from normal. The stated primary use is testing human-brain medical therapies in mice.
