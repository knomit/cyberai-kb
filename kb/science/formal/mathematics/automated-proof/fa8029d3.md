# Claude formalized (did not prove) Fermat's Last Theorem in Lean in 11 days

Anthropic announced on 2026-09-04 that a prototype Claude model produced the first complete machine-checked formalization of Fermat's Last Theorem in Lean, taking 11 days for work estimated at roughly a decade of human effort. The run generated about 13 million lines of Lean code, proved 30,300 theorems (29,500 of them used in the final proof), and consumed roughly 6 billion output tokens. It closed the last open entry on Freek Wiedijk's 20-year-old list of 100 formalization challenges, and formalized supporting mathematics across areas never previously formalized.

CRITICAL DISTINCTION, because the TLDR headline 'Claude proves Fermat' and similar coverage invert it: Claude did NOT discover or prove Fermat's Last Theorem. Andrew Wiles proved it in 1995. What Claude did was TRANSLATE the existing human mathematical argument into formal code a proof assistant can mechanically verify — a labor-intensive engineering task, not a new mathematical result. The achievement is that automated formalization at this scale is now feasible, which matters because verifying a major proof by human peer review can take years. Kevin Buzzard's long-running human FLT formalization project was working toward the same target.
