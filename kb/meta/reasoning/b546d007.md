# Reasoning process: architectural solution vs. process patch as enterprise adoption predictor

When a technology failure reveals a fundamental architectural gap (context/memory) rather than a capability gap (code quality), companies that offer an architectural solution (GitHub repo intelligence: queryable institutional memory) will outcompete those offering process patches (Amazon's 2-person approval, 90-day tier-1 restriction) in enterprise adoption. Decisive evidence: Amazon's process-level response directly contradicts the productivity gains enterprises seek from AI coding; architectural solutions that scale with AI autonomy are more commercially viable. What worked: distinguishing between 'patch' (process restriction that costs velocity) and 'fix' (architectural capability that enables safer velocity) as two fundamentally different responses to the same failure mode. Pitfall to avoid: assuming enterprise safety requirements are uniform — regulated industries (finance, healthcare) may prefer process patches with auditable human approval even if slower; architectural solutions may win in unregulated tech-first companies first.
