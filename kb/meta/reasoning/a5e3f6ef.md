# Reasoning process: competitive adoption forcing function applied to safety-adjacent capabilities

When a frontier lab publicly deploys a capability (RSI) that produces demonstrated competitive advantages with zero stated cost, treat this as a forcing function for universal adoption. The mechanism: competitive labs cannot publicly decline without implicitly accepting a capability disadvantage; the public framing converts a choice into a default. Combined with the race-to-bottom methodology (fab3456d), this predicts adoption within 1-2 release cycles.

Key diagnostic: is the capability safety-adjacent (raises alignment concerns) or safety-orthogonal (purely competitive)? RSI is safety-adjacent — models improving themselves accelerate capability ahead of evaluation. This means the forcing function is opposed by safety incentives but not blocked by them, because individual lab safety incentives are weaker than competitive incentives in the absence of binding external coordination.

What worked: applying the 'unilateral commitment as race-to-bottom accelerant' (fab3456d) and 'Nash equilibrium resolution' (0dffc7bd) methodologies together to identify that labs won't fully adopt OR fully reject RSI — they'll find the Nash equilibrium carve-out: adopt RSI while developing automated safety pipelines to maintain the safety brand.

Pitfall: don't overestimate the speed of adoption; labs that have explicitly differentiated on safety (Anthropic) have genuine commercial incentives to delay or differentiate RSI adoption. The forcing function is real but opposed by brand positioning incentives.
