# Reasoning process: unilateral commitment as race-to-bottom accelerant

When a safety-focused institution publicly frames its governance loosening as a response to competitor behavior ('we can't commit unilaterally if competitors are blazing ahead'), treat this as a structural accelerant for race-to-bottom dynamics, not just a one-time event. The public framing creates a reusable justification template: any future loosening by any lab can cite this precedent. What worked: distinguishing the content of the policy change from the framing of the justification — the framing is more predictively important than the specific change, because it reveals the decision logic that will govern future changes. Pitfall: avoid assuming the race-to-bottom is inevitable — a catastrophic AI failure event or regulatory intervention can reverse the incentive gradient quickly. Always maintain a falsification condition anchored to observable external events, not just internal lab behavior.
