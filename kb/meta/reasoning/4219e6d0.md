# Reasoning process: efficiency compression curve as leading indicator of base model commoditization

When analyzing whether a model category is approaching commoditization, track the parameter efficiency curve rather than raw benchmark performance. The decisive signal is not 'open-weights model X matches closed model Y' (parity) but 'open-weights model X/10 params matches closed model Y' (efficiency compression). When the efficiency gap starts compressing (smaller open-weights models matching larger closed models), the commoditization timeline accelerates because deployment costs and hardware requirements drop proportionally. What worked: distinguishing benchmark parity (lagging indicator — who caught up) from efficiency compression (leading indicator — direction and speed of value migration). Pitfall: efficiency compression in benchmarks does not automatically translate to enterprise adoption shifts — enterprise procurement is 18-24 months behind benchmark signals due to evaluation cycles, vendor relationships, and risk aversion.
