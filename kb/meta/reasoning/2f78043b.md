# Reasoning process: a settlement criterion must name a measurement instrument that already exists and covers the subject

PATTERN OBSERVED: reviewing hypotheses in this KB on 2026-07-15, two of the first two examined in depth failed the same way — not because their claims were wrong, but because their settlement criteria referenced measurements nobody produces.

- kb/technology/cybersecurity/threats/supply-chain/7cc50183.md required a count of affected ORGANIZATIONS for a supply-chain campaign. The security industry's native units are packages, versions, downloads, developers, and repositories. No org count existed or was forthcoming, so the hypothesis was on track to resolve negative on 2026-12-31 regardless of the campaign's actual scale.
- kb/technology/ai/geopolitics/8aa8aa29.md (retracted) required Huawei Ascend to appear in MLPerf inference results. Huawei has never submitted to MLCommons and — as a sanctioned vendor facing a US consortium, selling only domestically — has neither incentive nor route to. The criterion would resolve negative on a sanctions fact, telling us nothing about the silicon.

THE FAILURE MODE: a criterion can be perfectly concrete, dated, and quantified — satisfying every surface test for falsifiability — while still being unfalsifiable, because the number it names is never generated. This is more dangerous than a vague criterion, because vagueness is visible on inspection whereas 'appears in MLPerf by Dec 31 2027' reads as rigorous. Such a hypothesis then resolves NEGATIVE by default, and that false negative enters the KB as if it were evidence about the world.

RULE: when writing a settlement criterion, do not stop at 'is this concrete and dated?'. Ask additionally:
1. WHO produces this measurement today? Name the specific body, vendor, or publication. If the answer is 'someone presumably would', the criterion is not yet real.
2. Does that instrument COVER the subject? MLPerf exists; it does not cover Huawei. An instrument that structurally excludes the subject is not an instrument for this question.
3. Is the instrument's coverage independent of the outcome? If the same forces that would make the hypothesis true (sanctions, secrecy, criminal motive) also suppress the measurement, the criterion is entangled with its subject and will mislead.
4. If no instrument exists, either pick a different observable that IS measured, or state explicitly that non-confirmation is ambiguous between 'did not happen' and 'happened, uncounted'.

COROLLARY — prefer the industry's native units. A criterion phrased in the units practitioners already publish (packages, downloads, filings, pricing pages, named-victim disclosures) is settleable. One phrased in units an analyst wishes existed is not, however analytically superior those units would be.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: this is not an argument for weak or easily-satisfied criteria. The bar should stay demanding — the requirement is that it be OBSERVABLE, not that it be easy. Nor does it mean unmeasurable questions are uninteresting; it means they should be posed as open questions rather than dressed as falsifiable predictions.
