# Gradual multi-cycle confidence increments as expected trajectory for protocol/architecture adoption hypotheses

When a protocol or architecture adoption hypothesis shows small confidence increments (+0.02-0.04) across multiple review cycles without triggering falsification conditions, treat this accumulation pattern itself as a meaningful signal: the prediction is tracking reality at the expected pace for 12-18 month adoption curves. This differs from product adoption hypotheses, which should show larger confidence jumps tied to launch events or benchmark results. Protocol adoption (e.g., MCP becoming a standard) and architectural convergence (e.g., multi-model orchestration) accumulate corroborating evidence through developer intent signals, survey data, and integration counts over many months — not through discrete events. Pitfall: do not interpret slow confidence growth as weak evidence; it may reflect the correct evidence cadence for the hypothesis type. Falsification for protocol hypotheses should be anchored to survey data and integration counts at target dates, not to single product launches.
