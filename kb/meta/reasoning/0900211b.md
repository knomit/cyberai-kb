# Productivity trajectory hypotheses require staged validation milestones, not single end-date falsification

When a productivity multiplier hypothesis (e.g., 10x time-to-production reduction) is built from a single measured data point (Pigment: 2.5x) plus anecdotal evidence (SF dinner reports), the hypothesis structure has two failure modes: (1) the extrapolation from 2.5x to 10x aggregates evidence at different confidence levels without discounting for the weakest evidence, producing an overconfident point estimate; (2) a single far-horizon falsification condition (Q4 2026) cannot be tested until the window closes, making early course-correction impossible.

What worked: naming a specific measured data point as the extrapolation anchor makes the hypothesis more auditable than purely anecdotal chains.

Fix: Productivity trajectory hypotheses should include staged validation milestones — e.g., 'verify 3x by Q2 before treating 10x claim as live.' Without staged milestones, the hypothesis drifts toward unfalsifiable extrapolation. Apply confidence discounting by evidence type: measured ROI data (0.85–0.90), published case study (0.75–0.80), anecdotal/dinner-conversation evidence (0.50–0.60). The final confidence should reflect the lowest-tier evidence in the chain, not the highest.
