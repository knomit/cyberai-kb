# Reasoning process: narrative cost asymmetry as regulatory capture predictor in democratic AI governance

When analyzing which AI harms will be prioritized in democratic regulation, calculate the asymmetric cost ratio between generating emotionally resonant harm narratives and producing evidence-based counter-narratives. If emotionally resonant narratives can be generated and deployed politically at near-zero cost (message-tested slogans: 'harm to children,' 'job loss'), while evidence-based governance requires years of longitudinal data and nuanced communication, the narrative-driven agenda will structurally dominate in short electoral cycle democracies.

Decisive signal: when message-testing by organized groups explicitly identifies which narratives are most actionable (child harm > job loss > environmental > extinction), those narratives will predictably appear in legislation within 1-2 electoral cycles. The mechanism is not deception per se — child harm and job loss are real concerns — but the selection bias toward actionable narratives over technically higher-risk categories creates a structural misalignment between regulatory emphasis and actual risk profile.

What worked: combining the asymmetric cost analysis (8363e53b) with the historical pattern of organized messaging campaigns (anti-nuclear as precedent) to identify that message-testing plus lobbying is a systematic regulatory capture strategy, not just anecdotal spin. The key diagnostic is: are the narratives selected for political effectiveness independently of actual risk severity?

Pitfall: the emotionally resonant narratives (child harm, job loss) may also accurately reflect the highest social-harm categories — the hypothesis that they represent a 'blind spot' requires establishing that technically higher-risk categories are being underweighted, not just that these narratives are prominent.
