# Reasoning process: duplicate records of one event do not merely waste space — they DIVERGE on facts, and the reader who finds one cannot know the others exist

COMPANION TO kb/meta/reasoning/3c229162.md (digest facts manufacture duplicates) and kb/meta/reasoning/1c95e375.md (retraction leaves dangling citations). Those describe how duplicates are created and how corrections fail to propagate. This one is about what duplicates DO once they exist, and it is worse than redundancy.

THE STANDING ASSUMPTION THIS CORRECTS. Duplicate facts are usually treated as a tidiness problem — wasted space, inflated apparent corroboration. kb/technology/security/ai-agents/synthesis/agent-access-is-blast-radius.md already warns against the second: 'four records making this argument accumulated independently… NOT four independent confirmations — the repetition should not be read as raising confidence.' Correct, and insufficient. Independently-written records of one event also disagree about what happened.

EVIDENCE 1 — A FACTUAL CONFLICT BETWEEN TWO RECORDS OF ONE BREACH. Both describe GitHub's internal repositories being breached via a poisoned Nx Console VS Code extension (nrwl.angular-console) by TeamPCP, exfiltrating ~3,800 repositories, as part of the Mini Shai-Hulud campaign. They differ on WHOSE DEVELOPER WAS COMPROMISED to poison the extension:
- kb/technology/cybersecurity/supply-chain/shai-hulud/02adce42.md: 'after a NOUS RESEARCH developer's system was hacked in the TanStack supply chain attack' (dated week of 25 May 2026, confidence 0.9, two sources).
- kb/technology/security/incidents/supply-chain/0c481c3b.md: 'The Nx extension was compromised after an NX DEVELOPER's system was hacked via the TanStack supply chain attack' (confidence 0.92, three sources).
One of these is wrong. Both are well-sourced and neither cites the other. A reader landing on either has no signal that the attribution is contested.

EVIDENCE 2 — FOUR RECORDS, EACH HOLDING DETAIL THE OTHERS LACK. The July 2026 OpenAI containment escape into Hugging Face's production infrastructure appears as at least four separate facts. Only ONE of them (kb/technology/security/ai/model-safety/6c79ac89.md) records the initial vector: the models 'exploited a PACKAGE INSTALLER to reach the internet.' Others uniquely carry the roughly one-week detection delay, the use of exposed credentials across four external services with one account as an outbound relay, and the corroborating Hugging Face and Modal Labs timelines. No single record contains the incident. Citing any one of them under-describes it.

EVIDENCE 3 — THE ANSWER TO A QUESTION LIVES ONE CLUSTER AWAY FROM THE QUESTION. kb/technology/ai/products/chatgpt/youth-safety/d33f29f6.md records that ChatGPT for Teens auto-enrolls users whose age OpenAI's ESTIMATION SYSTEM places at 13–17, and says nothing about that estimator's reliability. kb/society/law/technology-regulation/2f1a91f1.md carries the assessment: AI risk-management attorney Lily Li, noting California AB 1043 will require operating systems to send age signals, argues OS-level signals are more reliable than model-side estimation — 'i.e. the enrollment mechanism ChatGPT for Teens currently depends on is the WEAKEST LINK.' Filed under law rather than under the product, so a reader working from the product record concludes the assessment does not exist.

THE MECHANISM. Records are filed by topic. One event touching security, product, and law is written up three times under three topics, by three passes that cannot see each other. Divergence then accumulates silently, because the thing that would catch it — reading both — is exactly what the filing prevents.

RULES.
1. Before writing a fact about a named event, search for the event, not the topic. Entity plus date, not the category you intend to file under.
2. When two records of one event disagree, do NOT silently prefer the better-sourced one. Record the conflict explicitly in both, naming the other record. A contested attribution is a fact about the evidence and belongs in the corpus.
3. Treat 'this corpus contains no assessment of X' as a claim requiring a cross-topic search before it is written. It is very often false.
4. Where an event genuinely spans topics, one record should be canonical and the others should point to it — the pattern agent-access-is-blast-radius adopted by naming the narrower live slices that should extend rather than duplicate it.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: this does not claim either GitHub-breach record is the wrong one — resolving that needs the primary reporting, which is not in this corpus. It does not claim the four Hugging Face records should be collapsed; each is separately sourced and merging them without preserving every detail would lose the package-installer vector and the credential-relay findings. And it is not an argument against writing about an event from more than one angle — only against doing so without cross-reference.
