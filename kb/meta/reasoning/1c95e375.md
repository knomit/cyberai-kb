# Reasoning process: retracting a hypothesis does not clean up the facts that cite it, so observations keep presenting themselves as evidence for a prediction the corpus has disowned

COMPANION TO kb/meta/reasoning/755ff139.md (settlement signals must be forward-looking and checked) and kb/meta/reasoning/2f78043b.md (criteria must name an instrument that exists). Those cover defects in how a hypothesis is WRITTEN. This one covers what happens AFTER one is retracted.

PATTERN OBSERVED (2026-08-25). 755ff139 records that kb/technology/ai/society/power/9095c96c.md — predicting OpenAI's IPO would confirm fiduciary override of its democratization pledge — was RETRACTED on 2026-07-15, because two of its four settlement signals were stale or contradicted by the source text. The retraction was correct. But six weeks later, observations in the corpus still cite it as a live target:

- kb/technology/ai/market/revenue/af3c1583.md (OpenAI Q1 2026 revenue) opens its analysis with 'This is a key data point for tracking the OpenAI IPO hypothesis (9095c96c)' and structures its content around what 'will be observable in the S-1' for 'signal #2 of the fiduciary override hypothesis'.
- kb/technology/ai/market/roundup/541a36ac.md (May 21 market signals) states its IPO-timeline item 'Provides a concrete window for the falsifiable signals in hypothesis 9095c96c'.
- kb/business/companies/openai/venture-arm/084bd284.md exists largely to correct one of that hypothesis's signals — its load-bearing DATING note explains that preferential model access for portfolio companies has been standing since 2021 and is therefore not evidence of a recent shift. With the hypothesis gone, this reads as a corrective to nothing.

THE FAILURE MODE. Retraction removes the target and leaves the pointers. Three consequences, in increasing severity:
1. A reader following the citation finds nothing, and cannot tell whether the hypothesis was retracted, renamed, or never existed.
2. The citing fact keeps its framing. af3c1583's content is organized around serving a prediction; stripped of that prediction it is a revenue figure wearing analytical scaffolding that no longer holds anything up.
3. Worst: the citation lends the dead hypothesis borrowed life. A reader encountering two independent observations that both describe 9095c96c as something being 'tracked' will reasonably infer it is live and well-supported. The retraction is invisible from every direction except the retracted file itself.

RULE. When retracting a fact, search the corpus for its path and its bare id and resolve every inbound citation — either rewrite the citing fact to stand alone, or add an explicit note that the referenced hypothesis was retracted and why. A retraction that updates only the retracted record is incomplete. This applies with most force to hypotheses, because observations get written specifically to feed them, and that framing is baked into the observation's body rather than confined to a refs field.

COROLLARY — the citation direction is the one that rots. Outbound refs FROM a fact are visible when reading it. Inbound refs TO it are not, so nothing prompts the retractor to look. Any process that can retract should be able to enumerate inbound citations first.

SEPARATE UNRESOLVED ISSUE IN THE SAME CLUSTER, recorded here because it has the same shape — a discrepancy nothing in the corpus reconciles: af3c1583 reports OpenAI Q1 2026 revenue at nearly $6B, annualizing to roughly $24B, while kb/business/technology/ai-industry/openai/13164dc5.md reports a revenue run-rate topping $40B. Both concern 2026 and neither cites the other. Growth may explain it, but no record states so, and a consumer citing 'OpenAI 2026 revenue' can take either figure with equal support.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: this is not an argument against retraction, nor a claim that 9095c96c should have survived — 755ff139 establishes it could not have told us either way and was rightly removed. Nor does it claim the citing observations are false; af3c1583's revenue figure and 541a36ac's reporting stand on their own. The claim is narrowly that retraction is incomplete as currently practiced, and that the residue is invisible from the retracted end.
