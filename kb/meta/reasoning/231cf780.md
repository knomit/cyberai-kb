# Reasoning process: two methodology facts at equal confidence read Qualcomm oppositely from the same observation — universal adoption is symmetric evidence, and neither cites the margin data that would break the tie

This corpus holds TWO methodology facts, both at confidence 0.8, that classify the SAME exemplar in OPPOSITE directions. Anyone applying either lens to a new hardware supplier will get a confident answer from a premise that does not support it.

THE CONFLICT.
- kb/meta/reasoning/substrate-layer-dominance.md predicts durable platform dominance by asking whether a player controls a layer all competing tools above it must consume. Its first diagnostic variant is named the 'Non-replicable resource substrate (GITHUB/QUALCOMM pattern)'. Substrate owners are held to profit from proliferation of competition above them, independent of which competitor wins. Motif: substrate-supplier-captures-value.
- kb/meta/reasoning/6dfb5d1e.md holds that when multiple independent players converge on the same silicon platform, the competitive layer shifts UPWARD to software and design. Its decisive evidence is Google, Alibaba and Meta all using QUALCOMM Wear Elite while differentiating on display, battery and brand. Its stated pitfall is, verbatim, 'assuming that the dominant silicon supplier will capture the most value', with the Android-OEM-versus-Apple margin comparison as the warning case. Motif: commoditization-relocates-rivalry.

Same company, same 2026 wearables market, equal confidence, opposite predictions, opposite motifs. NEITHER FACT CITES THE OTHER.

THE UNDERLYING PROBLEM IS EVIDENTIAL, NOT CLERICAL. Both lenses rest on the same observation: everyone builds on Qualcomm. kb/technology/ai/infrastructure/hardware/fb87f369.md records that Snapdragon Wear Elite is the first chipset to support WearOS by Google, Android and Linux simultaneously, with Google, Motorola and Samsung as global partners, and Meta Ray-Bans already shipping millions of Qualcomm-powered units. Universal adoption of one supplier is SYMMETRIC EVIDENCE — it is exactly what a non-replicable chokepoint looks like, and exactly what a commoditized table-stakes component looks like. The observation cannot discriminate, so a reasoner who starts from either lens will find it confirmed.

WHAT WOULD DISCRIMINATE, AND IS ABSENT. Where the margin accrues. If the substrate owner holds pricing power and captures margin as volume grows, the substrate lens is right; if margin compresses toward cost while the differentiating layers above hold price, the commoditization lens is right. NEITHER FACT CITES QUALCOMM MARGIN, PRICING, OR SHARE-OF-BILL-OF-MATERIALS DATA. 6dfb5d1e gestures at the right test — the Android OEM versus Apple margin contrast — but supplies the analogy rather than Qualcomm's own numbers.

HOW TO USE THIS. Do not apply either lens to a supplier on adoption breadth alone. Ask first whether margin at the substrate layer is rising, flat, or compressing as adoption widens; only then pick the lens. If that data is unavailable, the correct output is an UNRESOLVED classification, not the lens that came to mind first.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN:
- It does NOT establish that either methodology is wrong. Both describe real and well-attested dynamics; the finding is that this exemplar does not distinguish them and that both were applied to it anyway.
- It does NOT resolve which is right about Qualcomm. No margin evidence is offered here either.
- It does NOT claim the two lenses always conflict. They address different questions — one predicts value capture, one predicts where rivalry relocates — and can both hold in a market where a chokepoint captures component margin while brand captures end-product margin. The defect is that neither fact states the condition under which its own conclusion follows.
- The GitHub half of substrate-layer-dominance's first variant is untouched by this; only the Qualcomm exemplar is contested.
