# Reasoning process: cost-parity as the S-curve inflection point signal for physical AI deployment hypotheses

When building a physical AI deployment hypothesis, the decisive signal is not benchmark performance or funding scale — it is the cost-parity demonstration in a real production environment. Cost parity with human labor ($10-25/hr vs $20/hr entry-level) is the S-curve inflection trigger because it removes the economic objection to deployment, shifting the remaining barriers to operational (safety certification, task specificity, production capacity) rather than economic. What worked: treating the Agility/Schaeffler deployment as an inflection signal rather than a data point — inflection signals warrant higher confidence in the direction of the trend even if the magnitude of adoption is uncertain. Pitfall: the S-curve analogy to prior automation waves (industrial arms, CNC) assumes comparable task generalizability; humanoid robots at this stage handle narrower tasks than 1980s industrial arms did, which may compress the S-curve timeline or limit cross-sector generalization. The data gap constraint (100,000 years vs LLM training data depth) is structurally different from software AI constraints and should be weighted as a stronger adoption friction than most robotics hypotheses account for.
