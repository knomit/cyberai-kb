# METHODOLOGY: Asymmetric cost analysis as content moderation failure predictor

Reasoning process: When analyzing content moderation or fact-checking capacity, calculate the asymmetric cost ratio between generating false content and verifying/debunking it. If generation cost drops below verification cost by >10x, centralized content-level responses become structurally incapable of scaling — and the only viable responses must operate at an authentication/provenance layer.

Key signals: (1) AI tools now generate convincing fakes in seconds at near-zero marginal cost; (2) Verification requires human judgment, source triangulation, and context — high fixed cost per item; (3) Viral spread amplifies the gap further — a fake reaches peak virality before debunking completes.

What worked: The cost asymmetry framing made the structural nature of the problem legible — this is not a moderation quality problem, it's a scaling impossibility. The authentication layer solution follows directly from the diagnosis.

Pitfalls: Don't confuse 'content moderation at the margin is useful' (true) with 'content moderation can solve the structural problem' (false). Marginal improvements don't change the structural outcome when asymmetry is >10x.
