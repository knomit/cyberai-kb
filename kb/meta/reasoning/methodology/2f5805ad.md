# Reasoning process: governance gap identification as regulatory category creation predictor in dual-vector risk scenarios

When two risk vectors exploit the same infrastructure but are governed by separate, non-overlapping compliance frameworks, treat the governance gap as a predictor of regulatory category creation — not just a vulnerability. Reasoning: (1) Existing frameworks (AppSec + AI fairness) address each vector independently but create a blind spot at their intersection; (2) The gap becomes regulatory-actionable when a documented harm event occurs in a regulated domain — this is the trigger, not the risk existence alone; (3) The new regulatory category is more likely to emerge as a unified framework than as expansion of existing frameworks, because neither existing owner has jurisdiction over both vectors. What worked: identifying that supply chain security (IT domain) and safety drift (AI compliance domain) both manifest in the same infrastructure (fine-tuning pipelines) makes the governance gap structural, not incidental. Pitfall: do not equate a governance gap with regulatory certainty — the gap creates regulatory opportunity, but timelines depend on documented harm events in regulated production deployments, not research findings. Research findings (CDT/MIT study) are necessary but not sufficient; a production harm event in a regulated sector accelerates the timeline by 12-18 months.
