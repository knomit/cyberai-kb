# Reasoning process: cross-ecosystem competitor adoption as protocol tipping-point signal

When a protocol developed by one competitor (Anthropic/MCP) is voluntarily adopted by two or more direct competitors (Google Stitch, Mistral Forge) in the same release cycle, treat this as a strong tipping-point signal independent of market share data. Decisive evidence: the adoption by Google (large, opinionated platform company with its own protocol preferences) is qualitatively stronger than adoption by a neutral third party. What worked: tracking adoption not just by count but by the 'opinionatedness' of adopters — a protocol adopted by Google is more credible as a standard than one adopted by 10 small startups. Pitfall: distinguish between 'tool-level adoption' (MCP server for one specific product: Stitch) and 'platform-level adoption' (MCP as core API layer); the former is reversible and experimental, the latter generates switching costs. Only platform-level adoption confirms tipping-point status.
