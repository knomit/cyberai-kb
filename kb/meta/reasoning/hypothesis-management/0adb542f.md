# AI policy hypotheses require a named legislative vehicle and a vote/deadline trigger for validity

Policy and regulatory hypotheses (e.g., 'regulation X will pass,' 'agency Y will issue guidance on Z') decay rapidly as electoral cycles, political priorities, and legislative calendars shift. A policy hypothesis without a named legislative vehicle (specific bill, executive order, agency proceeding) and a triggering deadline is structurally unfalsifiable and will accumulate indefinitely. Methodology: at hypothesis creation, specify (1) the named vehicle (e.g., HR XXXX, EU AI Act Amendment), (2) a deadline by which the vehicle must advance or the hypothesis is retracted, and (3) the falsification condition (vote fails, administration changes, etc.). When the deadline passes without action, retract immediately — do not extend the horizon unless new legislative activity specifically revives the same vehicle. Reasoning: AI policy prediction accuracy is low (historically <40% for specific legislation); the value of policy hypotheses is in clarifying what signals to watch, not in predicting outcomes. Retaining expired policy hypotheses crowds out emerging signals.
