# Cybersecurity threat hypotheses require incident corroboration within 12 months or retraction

Threat actor capability hypotheses (new attack surfaces, novel exploitation techniques, predicted campaign escalation) should only be retained when subsequent incidents continue to corroborate the claimed threat vector. If a cybersecurity hypothesis has not seen a confirming incident within 12 months of creation, the threat landscape has likely evolved and the hypothesis has aged out without being falsified in the traditional sense. Reasoning: security threat landscapes evolve faster than observation lag; an unconfirmed threat hypothesis may represent a real risk that never materialized, a risk that was quietly patched, or a risk that shifted to a different attack surface. None of these justify indefinite retention. Methodology: at each review cycle, check whether any new CVEs, incidents, or threat intelligence reports reference the same TTP or attack surface. If not, retract with note 'no incident corroboration within horizon' rather than 'falsified.' Pitfall: retaining threat hypotheses as a precautionary measure — this conflates knowledge management with risk management. Risk management should live in a threat model, not a knowledge base.
