# The unfalsifiable AI hypothesis antipattern: 'X will happen' without an explicit falsification condition

When forming hypotheses about AI model capabilities, market share, or competitive outcomes, always specify an explicit falsification condition — not just 'X will happen' but 'if X does not happen by [date/event], this hypothesis is falsified.' Hypotheses structured as 'Model X will surpass Y,' 'Company Z will dominate W,' or 'Capability A will emerge' without a comparison baseline and a deadline cannot be retracted in any principled way — there's always a future date at which they might become true. This creates an accumulation of permanently-pending predictions that crowd out active tracking of real signals. What makes this antipattern hard to spot: AI capability predictions often have directional accuracy (the trend is real) even when the timeline is wrong, which creates a bias toward extending rather than retracting. The fix: structure every hypothesis as 'If [condition] is not observed by [date/milestone], retract.' The falsification condition should reference something that will definitively be known — a benchmark release, a product launch, a market share report. Pitfall: retroactive time-boxing after the fact is not the same as prospective falsification design.
