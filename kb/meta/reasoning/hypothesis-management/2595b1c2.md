# Hypothesis time-boxing: AI product and hardware hypotheses require explicit expiration horizons

When forming hypotheses about AI product competition, feature adoption, or hardware capability timelines, assign an explicit expiration horizon at creation: 3–6 months for AI software products (model releases, product positioning), 12–18 months for infrastructure/hardware roadmap claims. A hypothesis that has not been corroborated or falsified within its horizon should be retracted automatically at the next review — not treated as a persistent open question. Reasoning: the AI product layer moves faster than any hypothesis accumulation cycle; open-ended product hypotheses silently age into noise. Diagnostic signal: if a hypothesis references a specific product (coding tool, agent platform) and new product developments have shipped in the same space without confirming the original prediction, the hypothesis has effectively been superseded by market movement, not just 'unresolved.' Pitfall: conflating 'no contradicting evidence' with 'still valid' — in fast-moving domains, absence of contradiction is weak evidence when the domain is generating new facts at high velocity.
