# Reasoning process: market bifurcation hypotheses require explicit mid-tier definition and dual-squeeze mechanism specification

When synthesizing AI market economics facts into a bifurcation hypothesis, the decisive analytical move is identifying the 'squeeze' mechanism specifically — not just noting that costs are falling and capabilities are rising, but identifying which competitive forces compress the middle from each direction simultaneously. What worked: using the Neurometric 75/25 task split as the structural definition of the bifurcation, not just pricing data. The pricing data (DeepSeek $3.48 vs Claude $25) is the measurement; the task complexity distribution is the structural explanation. Pitfall: do not confuse 'pricing will bifurcate' (descriptive) with 'mid-tier vendors will fail' (predictive) — the prediction requires specifying the mechanism by which customers will switch away from mid-tier, not just that prices will split. The most common error is underestimating enterprise procurement inertia, which can maintain mid-tier spending 2-3 years past the economic obsolescence point.
