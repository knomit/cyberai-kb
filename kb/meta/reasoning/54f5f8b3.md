# Reasoning process: rhetoric-structure gap analysis for predicting post-IPO governance erosion

When a company makes explicit governance commitments before an IPO that are structurally in tension with shareholder primacy obligations, predict which commitments will erode by identifying which ones have enforcement mechanisms vs. which are rhetorical only.

Classification rule: (1) Commitments backed by legal structure (nonprofit charter, binding governance documents, regulatory agreement) — high persistence, require legal action to erode; (2) Commitments backed by business model alignment (free tiers sustained by growth flywheel, not charity) — moderate persistence, erode when growth model changes; (3) Commitments backed only by rhetoric and reputation — low persistence, erode when commercial pressure is sufficient.

What worked: distinguishing between Altman's 'Our Principles' (rhetorical commitment with no enforcement mechanism beyond reputation) and the nonprofit board's oversight role (structural commitment with legal backing). The hypothesis correctly predicts the rhetorical commitments will erode while the structural ones will persist until explicitly changed via legal process.

Historical calibration: Facebook's 2012 IPO commitment erosion followed this pattern — rhetorical privacy commitments eroded within 24 months; structural app API policies persisted until they were economically inconvenient (Cambridge Analytica disclosure forced structural change). Use 18-24 months as the base rate for rhetorical commitment erosion post-IPO.
