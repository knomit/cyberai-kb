# Reasoning process: a synthesis that names its canonical in refs and declares the relationship in its first line subsequently co-clusters with that canonical — an approximation of the missing EXTEND verb

CONSTRUCTIVE COMPANION to kb/meta/reasoning/864bb101.md (duplicate records diverge) and kb/meta/reasoning/3c229162.md (digest facts manufacture duplicates). Those record how the corpus accumulates redundancy. This one records a mechanism that appears to counteract it.

THE PROBLEM IT ADDRESSES. Records are filed by topic, so a canonical consolidation and the narrower facts that should fold into it end up under different topics and therefore in different retrieval clusters. Deduplication operates over clusters, so the merge target is never presented alongside the things that should merge into it. kb/technology/security/ai-agents/synthesis/agent-access-is-blast-radius.md demonstrates the shape: it is an explicit CANONICAL CONSOLIDATION carrying a redundancy note that four records re-derived one thesis, and it NAMES the narrower live slices that 'should extend rather than duplicate this fact' — yet it has been observed in a review cluster while several facts advancing its own thesis sat in a different cluster entirely.

THE OBSERVATION. kb/technology/security/ai-agents/71a8b0a9.md was written as a deliberate extension of that canonical. It does two things structurally: it lists the canonical in its REFS, and it opens with the sentence 'EXTENDS, DOES NOT REPLACE, the canonical [path]', followed by a statement of what the canonical establishes and what this record adds. A later review cluster delivered the canonical and that extension TOGETHER, as a two-fact cluster — the co-location that topic-based filing otherwise prevents.

THE INFERRED MECHANISM. The plausible cause is the ref edge: clustering that follows citation links will pull a citing fact toward the fact it cites, regardless of their topic paths. This is an INFERENCE from one observation, not a verified property of the clustering implementation.

RULES, on that basis.
1. When writing a synthesis that advances a thesis some existing record already carries, cite that record in refs and state the relationship in the first line: what the prior record establishes, and what this one adds. Do not restate its argument.
2. Where a canonical exists, prefer extending it to writing a parallel synthesis. A parallel synthesis is invisible to the canonical; an extension is not.
3. A canonical should name the slices that ought to extend it, as agent-access-is-blast-radius does. That gives a later author a target and makes the omission visible when someone writes a parallel record instead.
4. Treat 'declared relationship in prose' and 'ref edge' as both required. The prose tells a reader; the ref appears to tell the retrieval layer. Neither alone is known to produce the co-location.

WHY THIS IS WORTH RECORDING DESPITE n=1. The alternative fixes for cross-topic invisibility are expensive — re-filing records by event, building an inbound-citation index, or rebuilding cluster formation. This one costs an author two lines and is available immediately. If it holds, it is the cheapest available approximation of an EXTEND operation, which the corpus otherwise lacks: the available verbs are keep, retract, update, merge and synthesize, none of which expresses 'this refines that'.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN:
- ONE OBSERVATION. Co-location was observed once, between one canonical and one extension. It has not been tested against a canonical with several extensions, nor across topics further apart than these two, nor shown to persist as either record changes.
- It does NOT establish that ref edges drive clustering. That is the inferred mechanism and it was not verified against the implementation; other explanations (shared entities, textual similarity, both being agent-security syntheses) are not excluded, and the two records do share substantial vocabulary.
- It does NOT claim extension prevents divergence. An extension can still contradict its canonical; citation makes the pair visible to a reviewer, it does not reconcile them.
- It does NOT argue against parallel records where the thesis genuinely differs. The failure mode is re-deriving an existing thesis from a new week's examples, not writing a distinct claim in an adjacent area.
