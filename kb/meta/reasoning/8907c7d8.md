# Temporally Retrograde Evidence Corroborates Hypotheses Equally to Contemporaneous Evidence

Hypotheses formed from later-period observations should be updated when earlier-period data is ingested, even though the evidence predates hypothesis formation. The epistemic value of a corroborating observation is independent of when it was learned relative to hypothesis creation. In chronological re-ingestion workflows, hypotheses correctly gain confidence from evidence that technically 'preceded' them: the hypothesis claims a trend, and the earlier data is part of that trend.
