# Argument that frontier labs' pacing proposals serve their own economics

An analysis titled "Frontier labs have a financial incentive to pace the frontier" argues that while many frontier-lab advocates for regulatory pacing are well-intentioned, the specific rules the labs propose also protect their existing investments, preserve price premiums, and defer billions in competitive spending — so the arguments should not be taken at face value. The piece's central mechanism claim is that labs cannot economically slow down unilaterally without government enforcement, because the first to slow loses position; this makes the call for regulation structurally rational for an incumbent regardless of sincerity. Note the argument is about incentive alignment, not an allegation of bad faith.
