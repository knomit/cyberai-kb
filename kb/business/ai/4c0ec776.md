# Mid-2026 enterprise AI defined by a cost-control backlash acting on both vendors and buyers

Two synthesis threads converge on one macro dynamic in mid-2026: runaway inference/token costs ('tokenmaxxing', e.g. Uber exhausting its annual AI budget in four months) created an enterprise cost-control backlash. This pressure acts on both sides of the market. On the vendor side, even the dominant frontier lab Anthropic (77% enterprise adoption per Ramp; revenue run-rate reportedly $9B→$47B) ships spend-alert and analytics features to help customers rein in the very spend that fuels its growth. On the buyer side, incumbents vertically integrate to escape third-party costs: Microsoft substitutes cheaper in-house MAI models for licensed OpenAI/Anthropic models in Office (while cutting 4,800 jobs), and DeepSeek designs its own inference chips. Cost governance and vertical integration are the two faces of the same margin squeeze.
