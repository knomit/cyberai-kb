# Ramp AI Index shows enterprises shifting from frontier to mid-tier models

Ramp's monthly AI Index, released Wednesday 2026-09-09 covering August 2026, reports that business AI adoption and spend are both decelerating, and that frontier-model usage is losing share to cheaper mid-tier models.

FIGURES, with their scoping intact:
- US business AI adoption reached 56.1% in August 2026, a month-over-month increase of less than half a percentage point.
- Per-employee AI spend AMONG THE TOP 1% OF ADOPTING FIRMS fell nearly 10%, from $7,976 to $7,205. This figure is scoped to that top-1% cohort and must not be quoted as an all-business average.
- The effective price per million tokens fell 41% to $0.68, down from a 2026 peak of $1.15 in March.
- Frontier models (Opus, Fable, Sol) drove 45% of token share, down from a 53% peak.

THE MECHANISM: volume is increasingly driven by mid-tier models like Anthropic's Claude Sonnet and OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Terra. Ramp chief economist Ara Kharazian reports that businesses are 'imposing company-wide defaults that reduce usage of frontier models, saying standard models are still highly performant and also more cost effective.' Falling spend here therefore reflects substitution toward cheaper tiers, not falling usage.

NOTABLY, the shift is NOT toward open-source or Chinese models, which only 6.4% of AI-spending businesses use — it is toward cheaper tiers from the same two vendors. Anthropic widened its lead over OpenAI over the month.

TWO CAVEATS MATTER AND BOTH ARE LOAD-BEARING. First, this measures only Ramp's customer base, which skews toward tech companies and startups — it is NOT a representative sample of US business. Second, Kharazian himself flagged a benign alternative explanation for the spend drop: summer vacation, with similar declines observed around November and December. The substitution reading above is therefore the reported interpretation, not a controlled finding.
