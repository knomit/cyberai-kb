# Hypothesis: A frontier lab will make outcome- or seat-based pricing its headline enterprise commercial model by Q4 2027, displacing per-token as the default

Prediction (forward-looking): The enterprise cost-control backlash identified in the mid-2026 synthesis will not be resolved by buyers getting better at governing token spend. It will be resolved by vendors changing the unit they sell. At least one of OpenAI, Anthropic, or Google will make a non-per-token unit (per-outcome, per-resolved-task, per-seat, or committed-capacity) the default or headline pricing for a major enterprise product line.

Settlement criterion (by 31 Dec 2027 = confirmed): a public pricing page, enterprise commercial announcement, or earnings/press statement from OpenAI, Anthropic, or Google presents a non-per-token unit as the primary advertised price for a flagship enterprise offering — not merely as an available alternative alongside per-token, and not limited to a pilot or single named customer. Falsified if all three still lead with per-token metering for enterprise flagships at that date.

Causal reasoning (goes beyond the synth fact): The synth fact establishes that Anthropic ships spend-alert and analytics tooling to help customers rein in the spend that fuels its own revenue. That is a reflexive position — a vendor cannot durably sell a metered unit its customers are actively trying to consume less of, and cannot durably compete on that unit against open-weight substitutes whose marginal price trends toward the cost of self-hosting. Spend-governance tooling is the intermediate move; repricing is the terminal one. Two independent signals point the same way: OpenAI's July 2026 enterprise guidance explicitly argues for measuring 'useful work per dollar' rather than token prices alone — a vendor reframing the buyer's unit of account is usually a precursor to reframing the invoice — and a majority of production tokens now route through open-weight models, which caps what per-token can be worth for commodity workloads.

Load-bearing evidence gap: whether frontier-lab enterprise revenue growth is still tracking token volume growth. If revenue per enterprise account is rising while that account's token consumption flattens, repricing is already underway and confidence should rise materially. If the two remain tightly coupled, the pricing model is not under real strain and confidence should fall. Disclosed revenue-per-account vs. volume figures are the single highest-value observation here.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: this does not predict per-token pricing disappears — it will very likely persist for API and developer tiers, and the claim is specifically about headline enterprise pricing. It does not predict frontier-lab revenue declines; outcome pricing is at least as plausibly a margin expansion. It does not claim open-weight models match frontier capability, only that they cap the price of commodity token volume. It also does not claim the cost backlash is universal — the underlying evidence is anecdotal (Uber's budget, Ramp adoption data) rather than a representative survey, which is why this sits below the source synthesis in confidence.
