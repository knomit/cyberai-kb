# Index Ventures partner reframes AI FOMO investing as a deliberate strategy

Jahanvi Sardana, partner at Index Ventures, told The Deep View for its 2026-09-14 piece that FOMO-driven AI investing is a defensible strategy rather than only a pathology. Her account of the mechanism: investors who missed early entry into Anthropic and OpenAI 'look at this pot of gold and then you retrofit everything else into that pot of gold, just hoping that you hit that home run'; the next wave was AI coding agents (Cursor, Code Rabbit, Lovable, Cognition); and investors are now hunting the wave after that. She argues FOMO functions as a skill — 'It is FOMO investing, but it's also not. It actually makes you think on your feet, think really fast and react to the market' — and that broad portfolio spread means a small fraction of hits still produces significant returns. Her own stated filter is different: 'the most important thing is weeding out the missionary founders from the mercenary founders. That comes down to: Why are you building a business? Who is your customer? Why do they care?' Context: in the week to 2026-09-14 nearly a dozen AI startups each raised hundreds of millions at billion-dollar-plus valuations, spanning model providers, applications, chips, healthcare, legal and defense. This is one VC's framing of her own asset class, with an obvious interest in the thesis being correct; it is not evidence that current valuations are justified.
