# Hypothesis: Nvidia's circular vendor financing will force quantified related-party revenue disclosure or formal regulatory inquiry by Q4 2027

Prediction (forward-looking): The vertical entanglement identified in the mid-2026 buildout synthesis — chipmakers funding model labs that then buy their chips, exemplified by Nvidia reportedly contributing ~$30B to OpenAI's $110B round while simultaneously targeting ~$1T in data-center revenue through 2027 — is not merely a structural observation but an accounting exposure that will surface publicly.

Settlement criterion (any ONE of the following, by 31 Dec 2027 = confirmed):
(a) Nvidia quantifies in an SEC filing (10-K/10-Q) the dollar amount or percentage of data-center revenue derived from customers in which it holds an equity or investment interest, beyond boilerplate related-party language;
(b) The SEC or an equivalent securities regulator opens a publicly reported formal inquiry into revenue recognition on vendor-financed AI compute transactions at any of Nvidia, OpenAI, or a comparable counterparty;
(c) Any of the entangled parties restates revenue tied to a vendor-financed compute deal.
Falsified if none of (a)–(c) occurs by 31 Dec 2027.

Reasoning: Circular financing (supplier invests in customer, customer's purchases book as supplier revenue) is the pattern that drew regulatory attention in the 1999–2001 telecom equipment buildout (Lucent, Nortel, Cisco vendor financing). The mechanism is not fraud but a reflexivity in which supplier revenue growth is partly self-funded, making growth rates less informative about independent end demand. The scale here — a single ~$30B investment against a ~$1T revenue target — is large enough that the related-party share becomes material, and materiality is what triggers disclosure obligations.

Load-bearing evidence gap: the actual proportion of Nvidia data-center revenue attributable to equity-linked customers. If that share is small (<5%), the pressure toward (a) largely evaporates and confidence should drop sharply. If it is large (>15%), confidence should rise. This single number is the highest-value thing to seek.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: this does not predict fraud, a restatement as the likely branch, an AI capex downturn, or that Nvidia's revenue is illusory. Vendor financing is legal and often rational for seeding a market. The claim is narrowly about disclosure and scrutiny surfacing, not about the underlying demand being fake. It also does not claim the reported $30B/$110B figures are confirmed — the underlying source facts carry confidence 0.55 and rest on single-source reporting, which is why this hypothesis is set below them.
