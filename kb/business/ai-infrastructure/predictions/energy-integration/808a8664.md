# Hypothesis: Vertical integration will extend past silicon to owned electricity generation — a US frontier lab or hyperscaler will own ≥1 GW of generation assets outright by Q4 2028

Prediction (forward-looking): The cost-driven vertical integration documented in mid-2026 — Microsoft substituting in-house MAI models for licensed frontier models, DeepSeek designing its own inference chips — has a next step the synthesis does not yet name. Each move walks one layer further down the stack toward whichever input is scarce and price-volatile. Models and silicon are the layers integrated so far; electricity is the next one, and the integration mode will shift from contracting (PPAs, colocation) to outright ownership of generation assets.

Settlement criterion (by 31 Dec 2028 = confirmed): at least one of OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, Microsoft, Google, Amazon, or Meta owns — via acquisition or self-build, not a power purchase agreement, tolling arrangement, or minority equity stake — electricity generation assets totaling ≥1 GW of nameplate capacity primarily dedicated to its own compute, as evidenced by an acquisition announcement, regulatory filing, or 10-K disclosure. Falsified if none has done so by that date.

Evidence and causal reasoning: Musk's July 2026 acquisition of APR Energy (>1 GW of mobile and diesel turbines, ~$1B) to power Grok/xAI is the first clean instance of the ownership mode — not a contract for power but the purchase of a generation company. It is one data point, which is why this is a hypothesis rather than a synthesis. The mechanism that makes it generalizable: compute is now explicitly treated as a hedgeable commodity (Kalshi's GPU-price forward curve), and when a firm hedges an input it has already conceded that the input's price is volatile and material. Owning generation is the limit case of that hedge. Nvidia's ~$1T data-center revenue target through 2027 implies power demand that grid interconnect queues cannot serve on the labs' timelines, which pushes toward behind-the-meter self-supply regardless of price.

Load-bearing evidence gap: grid interconnection wait times for new large-load data centers in the primary US markets. If typical waits compress to under ~2 years, contracting stays viable and self-build ownership loses its main forcing function — confidence should fall materially. If waits stay at or above ~4 years, ownership becomes close to the only path and confidence should rise. A secondary signal: whether any of the named firms creates an energy subsidiary or hires a utility-executive-level energy leader.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: this does not predict labs become merchant power sellers, nor that PPAs decline in absolute terms — contracted power will very likely remain the majority of supply. It does not claim the APR Energy deal is representative; it is explicitly a single instance being extrapolated. It says nothing about which generation technology wins (gas, nuclear, solar+storage), and it does not claim self-supply is cheaper — speed of access to power, not price, may be the actual driver.
