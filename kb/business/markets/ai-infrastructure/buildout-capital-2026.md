# Mid-2026 AI compute buildout drew hundred-billion-dollar commitments across labs, chipmakers and fabs, with chipmakers financing the labs that buy their chips

As of mid-to-late July 2026, a series of large capital commitments pointed to a coordinated surge in AI compute infrastructure, concentrated among a few players and structurally entangled across layers.

MODEL LABS.
- OpenAI raised planned infrastructure spending through 2030 to $750 billion, starting with a $20B, 3.2 GW Georgia data-centre campus.
- OpenAI's reported $110B funding round was backed by SoftBank, Nvidia, and Amazon.

CHIPMAKERS AND FABS.
- AMD and Anthropic signed a deal for tens of billions in AI servers: Anthropic buying up to 2 GW of AMD Instinct MI450 chips from H1 2027, with AMD investing up to $5B in Anthropic on milestones.
- Nvidia targeted roughly $1 trillion in data-centre revenue through 2027.
- TSMC committed an additional $100B to its Arizona fabs (total US pipeline $265B), explicitly citing a multi-year AI demand 'megatrend'.
- SambaNova raised $1B at an $11B valuation positioning as an Nvidia challenger.
- Apple committed $30B to US-made Broadcom chips.

THE COMMON THREAD — VERTICAL ENTANGLEMENT. Two things recur across these announcements: gigawatt-scale capacity planning, and financial entanglement running in both directions between model developers, chipmakers, and data-centre siting. The sharpest form is chipmakers funding model labs that then buy those chipmakers' chips — Nvidia in OpenAI's round, AMD's milestone-linked investment in Anthropic.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: this is a synthesis of individually-sourced announcements from newsletter reporting (The Deep View, TLDR AI); see the source facts for exact figures and per-deal caveats. Announced and planned spending are not audited commitments, and several figures are reported rather than confirmed. Nothing here establishes that the capacity is built, financed to completion, or that end demand independently supports it — the circularity of the financing is precisely what makes the headline growth figures less informative about independent demand.
