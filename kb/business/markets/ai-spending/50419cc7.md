# Ramp August 2026 AI spending index: Anthropic leads on adoption, enterprise token spend shifts away from the most capable models, and the cheaper Opus 5 overtook Fable 5 in corporate spending within a month of launch

MERGED from two records of the same August 2026 Ramp finding: a multi-vendor spending-index report and a narrower observation on the Opus 5 / Fable 5 switch with its economic reading. Both stated that Opus 5 had surpassed Fable 5 in business spending; the economic caveats from each are consolidated below and none is dropped.

ADOPTION SHARE. Ramp's August 2026 AI spending index found 43.5% of its U.S. business clients paid for Anthropic subscriptions or tokens versus 39.7% for OpenAI. xAI posted its fastest growth month since July 2025 (+0.94 pp month-over-month, roughly 4% of AI business spend), and open-source / Chinese model serving platforms such as Hugging Face reached 6.1% of Ramp AI customers.

TOKEN SPEND WITHIN VENDORS. Only about 6% of Anthropic token spend went to Fable 5, its most capable and most expensive model, while 25% of OpenAI tokens among Ramp's OpenAI customers went to GPT-5.6 Sol. Ramp lead economist Ara Kharazian said Fable 5 marked 'a new upper bound' for AI spend, a point at which more performance is not worth the price.

THE OPUS 5 SWITCH. Anthropic's less powerful and roughly half-priced CLAUDE OPUS 5 OVERTOOK FABLE 5 IN CORPORATE MODEL SPENDING WITHIN A MONTH OF ITS LAUNCH. The analysis attributes this to LOW SWITCHING COSTS, which let businesses route routine work to cheaper models while reserving premium systems for tasks requiring sustained autonomy.

THE CONVERGENCE CAVEAT — load-bearing. Opus 5 costs roughly half of Fable 5's rates, BUT the cheaper system may need MORE ATTEMPTS, LONGER PROMPTS, OR MORE HUMAN REVIEW, so COST PER SUCCESSFUL TASK CAN CONVERGE. A spend-share shift toward the cheaper model is therefore consistent with no efficiency gain at all.

SUPPORTING CONTEXT. Gartner research separately projects that AI agents could raise inference costs up to fivefold. The overall pattern is credited with driving traction for small models, domain-specific models, open-source alternatives, and model routing.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN — every clause carried from both inputs:
- Ramp's customer base SKEWS toward startups and small businesses, so these shares must not be read as the enterprise market as a whole.
- Low Fable 5 token share is attributed by Ramp to PRICE; it is NOT evidence about capability.
- It does NOT show frontier models being displaced for work requiring sustained autonomy. FABLE REMAINS POSITIONED for long autonomous projects that must stay coherent across many connected steps.
- Spend share is a BUDGETING fact, not a measure of delivered value or of cost per successful task — which no source in this cluster publishes.
