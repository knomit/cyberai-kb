# 'J-curve of productivity' names the temporary output dip organizations accept when scaling AI adoption

Rokt CTO Sam Dozor, speaking to The Deep View (published 2026-09-19), described moving from AI experimentation to org-wide scale as requiring acceptance of a 'J-curve of productivity': the first few weeks or months of learning to work with AI produce temporary productivity hits across the organization, followed by a sharp uptick in productivity and output once employees are properly trained. He paired this with a cultural precondition — that getting past employee AI skepticism required 'creating a culture of excitement around it rather than fear' — and both halves are part of the claim: the dip is expected AND it is survived by managing the fear, not by waiting it out passively. SOURCING CAVEAT: this comes from a special weekend edition presented in partnership with Rokt (sponsored content), sourced to a single executive describing his own organization, with no measured figures for the depth or duration of the dip or the size of the subsequent uptick. This does NOT mean a measured J-curve has been demonstrated for enterprise AI adoption; it is a practitioner's framing worth recognizing by name, not an empirical result.
