# A pilot, an option and a five-year-old standing perk all read as current decisive moves once reporting strips their stage and vintage — three OpenAI records each carry a corrective clause saying so

MECHANISM: an arrangement's STAGE (undisclosed pilot, preserved option, closed commitment) and its VINTAGE (how long it has been in place) determine what it can evidence, and both are stripped when the arrangement is reported as a corporate move — so a reader takes a trial, an unexercised option, or a long-standing feature as a current, decisive decision, and builds an argument on a premise the record does not support.

Three records about the same company each carry an explicit corrective clause, and the three failure modes are distinct:

- STAGE — TRIAL, NOT POLICY. An outcome-based pricing arrangement in which the customer pays only when the AI completes the job is being tested with a LIMITED NUMBER OF SELECT MAJOR ACCOUNTS. Terms, customers and prices are undisclosed and the company has not announced the test publicly. The recorded driver is that token-based pricing makes cost accounting difficult for buyers.
- STAGE — OPTION, NOT COMMITMENT. A confidential draft registration filing with named lead banks was publicly confirmed, then within weeks reporting had the listing leaning to the following year. The company's own position was that it has not decided timing, that some things are easier as a private company, and that the filing preserves the OPTION to list sooner. The recorded epistemic note: a confidential filing is an option, not a commitment; reading it as evidence of an imminent listing was contradicted within about two weeks; FILING AND LISTING MUST BE TRACKED AS SEPARATE EVENTS.
- VINTAGE — STANDING FEATURE, NOT NEW SIGNAL. Preferential early model access for the venture arm's portfolio companies has been a standing perk since the fund's founding five years earlier, alongside capital, strategic support and cloud credits. The load-bearing dating note: ANY ANALYSIS TREATING IT AS A NEW SIGNAL OF COMMERCIAL CAPTURE IS CITING A FIVE-YEAR-OLD ARRANGEMENT AS EVIDENCE OF A RECENT SHIFT. A second qualifier holds independently: the fund reportedly does NOT require portfolio companies to build on the company's APIs, so early access is a conventional corporate-VC perk rather than an exclusivity or channel-capture clause.

WHAT FOLLOWS: record and cite a corporate arrangement with its stage and its start date attached, and treat the outcome it is presumed to lead to as a separate item to be evidenced separately. The vintage failure is the harder of the two to notice, because nothing in a report of a long-standing arrangement signals that it is long-standing.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: none of the three is claimed to be false or misleading, and none of the presumed outcomes is claimed not to occur — pilots become policy, filings are followed by listings, and standing perks can still matter. The claim is narrowly that the step is not the outcome and its age is not its news value. The three arrangements are unrelated to each other beyond the shape, and the pilot record rests on a single source. Provenance — the amounts, dates, valuations, banks, named portfolio companies and investment counts — stays in the member facts cited below.
