# OpenAI's 2030 infrastructure-spend figure was revised upward at least twice inside 2026, each time described as an increase over an earlier estimate — so any single figure is a point on a rising series

MECHANISM: a forward capital-commitment figure is restated upward repeatedly within a short reporting window, each restatement framed as an increase over the prior estimate rather than as a new plan, so the figure behaves as a running total of commitments made to date rather than as a bounded forecast — and any single value quoted without its date misrepresents it.

The corpus holds successive 2026 snapshots of OpenAI's projected AI-infrastructure spending through the same 2030 horizon. The mid-year figure was reported as a double-digit percentage increase over earlier estimates; the later figure was higher again, with the earlier baseline named explicitly in that report. The revisions are separated by months, not years, and each was reported by a different outlet, so the escalation is visible across independent reporting rather than inside one source's revisions.

Two load-bearing conditions carried from the members. First, in the later report the spend figure ROSE while projected cash burn over the same horizon FELL — both moved, in opposite directions, and the combination is the finding; it must never be compressed to 'costs went up' or 'burn improved' alone. Those two movements are only reconcilable through higher projected revenue or a shift in how the spend is financed — leases, partner-funded capacity, vendor financing. Second, all of these are PROJECTIONS, not reported actuals.

WHAT FOLLOWS: cite this figure with its date or not at all, and treat the series slope — not the level — as the informative quantity. A figure rising this fast while burn falls means the financing structure is absorbing the increase, so the number is as much a statement about counterparties and lease terms as about compute.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: a rising series is not evidence the spending will be incurred, nor that the trend continues — the members record revisions, not outcomes, and one of them notes a large previously-announced infrastructure programme was reportedly stalled amid partner disputes, so announced commitments and realised spend are not the same thing. Provenance — the specific dollar figures, dates, outlets, named projects, sites and capacities — stays in the member facts cited below.
