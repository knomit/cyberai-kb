# Everything the corpus holds on OpenAI's 2030 position is leaked internal projection, and the headline measures move in opposite directions — so no single figure characterizes it

MECHANISM: a subject's forward financial picture is known only through figures attributed to sources rather than published or audited by the subject, and the separately-reported measures move in opposite directions over the same horizon, so any one figure quoted alone supports a conclusion the full set does not.

The corpus holds two September-2026 reports on OpenAI's position through 2030, from different outlets, neither audited nor company-published:

- Projected compute and infrastructure spending through 2030 ROSE substantially, while projected cash burn over the same horizon FELL. Both moved, in opposite directions, and the combination is the finding. This must never be compressed to 'OpenAI's costs went up' or 'OpenAI's burn improved' alone. The two movements are only reconcilable through higher projected revenue or a shift in how the spend is financed — leases, partner-funded capacity, vendor financing.
- Cumulative free cash flow through 2030 is expected to be deeply negative, indicating a decade-scale cash-burn position financed by capital raises rather than operations.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: neither figure states OpenAI expects a net-income loss of the cited magnitude, nor that the company expects to be unprofitable in every year through 2030 — free cash flow and profitability are distinct measures, and heavy capex can drive deeply negative FCF alongside improving operating margins. Nothing here is reported actuals; all of it is projection, and the projections themselves were revised within the reporting window, which is the point: a revision that large on one measure while another moved the other way means the underlying financing assumptions, not the operating business, are doing most of the work. A consumer citing 'OpenAI's 2030 outlook' from one of these numbers is citing one side of a set whose members disagree in direction.
