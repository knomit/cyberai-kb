# OpenAI deferred its IPO on safety grounds in the same week SoftBank levered up $11.9B to keep funding it — the capital need did not pause, it moved onto a backer's balance sheet

Two reports from the week of 2026-09-12 describe the two halves of one financing situation.

- On 2026-09-12, Sam Altman said OpenAI would not go public in 2026, arguing that current AI safety concerns made an IPO ill-advised. The company had previously filed confidentially and was reportedly considering a 2027 listing instead.
- In September 2026 SoftBank borrowed nearly $12 billion from roughly 20 banks to keep funding OpenAI, exceeding the $10 billion it originally sought. Masayoshi Son was still targeting close to $65 billion into OpenAI by October 2026 even after Altman shelved a 2026 IPO. SoftBank shares fell as much as 13% on Monday 2026-09-14 on concern over the debt pile.

THE MECHANISM: deferring a public listing removes a route to raise capital from public equity markets, but does not reduce the capital the company consumes. The requirement is therefore met from private sources, and here that meant a backer borrowing against its own balance sheet at upsized scale. The effect is that the risk is not reduced by the deferral — it is relocated, from dispersed public shareholders onto one concentrated, levered holder, which is what the 13% share-price move prices.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN:
- The sources do NOT establish that the loan was caused by the IPO deferral. The upsizing and the Son target are reported alongside it and the reporting explicitly notes the loan continued 'even after' the IPO was shelved; that is sequence and juxtaposition, not a demonstrated causal link, and the loan was already being sought at $10 billion.
- Altman's stated reason is safety; nothing here independently verifies that reason or rules out others.
- The $65 billion by October 2026 is a reported target, not a completed commitment, and the 2027 listing is reported as under consideration only.
- This is two reports from a single news cycle, not a financing pattern established across companies.
