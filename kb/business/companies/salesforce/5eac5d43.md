# Salesforce's Dreamforce 2026 strategy pairs an owned domain model with a deliberately openable product surface — the model is where it holds value, the interface is where it gives ground

Mechanism: an incumbent platform facing substitution by general-purpose agents keeps the layer competitors cannot replicate and concedes the layer they can, so that being reached through someone else's interface stops being a loss.

At one event on one day, Salesforce announced both halves. The owned half is a domain-specific reasoning model, built by post-training an open-weight model from a chip vendor on decades of the company's own business data, kept private to customer data, and reported by the vendor to match or exceed leading model performance on the relevant task class with substantially fewer errors on a benchmark the vendor itself created. The conceded half is a product slate that makes the company's own front end optional: a conversational interface replacing the traditional UI, a set of prebuilt skills that let a third-party assistant act as the front end, and a headless mode exposing every platform element through APIs and agent-facing protocols so customers can reach their data without visiting the company's site or using its tools at all. The CEO's framing was that the disruption is not the end of software but the end of software that makes humans do all the work.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: the performance comparison is vendor-reported on a vendor-created benchmark, without independent replication. This also does not claim the strategy has worked — both halves were announcements, with no adoption or outcome data attached.
