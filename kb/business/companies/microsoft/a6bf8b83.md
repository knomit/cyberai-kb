# The April 2026 Microsoft–OpenAI amendment is recorded as OpenAI winning independence, but it is also what freed Microsoft to replace OpenAI models with its own

The amendment to the OpenAI–Microsoft partnership is filed in this corpus from one side only. Read from the other, it explains a sequence of Microsoft moves that otherwise look unrelated.

WHAT THE AMENDMENT DID (as recorded): OpenAI can now offer products on any cloud provider, though Microsoft remains primary partner and products ship first on Azure unless Microsoft declines; Microsoft's IP license for OpenAI models and products became NON-EXCLUSIVE through 2032; Microsoft no longer pays OpenAI a revenue share, while OpenAI continues paying Microsoft at the same percentage through 2030 subject to a total cap; and the AGI clause was removed, under which Microsoft had retained exclusivity until OpenAI created AGI as verified by an independent expert panel. The recorded framing is that this granted OPENAI greater independence and cleared its IPO path by demonstrating diversified revenue — OpenAI's revenue chief cited an internal memo saying the Microsoft partnership 'limited our ability to meet enterprises where they are', including on AWS Bedrock.

WHAT MICROSOFT DID NEXT, ON THE SAME CLOCK:
- MAI-Thinking-1 — Microsoft's first reasoning model NOT distilled or fine-tuned from another developer's model — is recorded as following 'the April 2026 amendment making Microsoft's license to OpenAI models non-exclusive'. The source fact makes that link explicitly.
- By early July 2026 Microsoft announced it was REPLACING OpenAI and Anthropic models with in-house MAI models in products like Excel and Outlook, with tens of thousands of prompts a week already handled by MAI across reasoning, image, voice and coding. The stated motivation is cost; AI chief Mustafa Suleyman had signaled intent to wean off Anthropic models.

THE READING. An exclusivity arrangement binds both parties. Ending it let OpenAI sell on rival clouds AND let Microsoft stop paying for models it could build. The revenue-share termination is the tell: Microsoft's obligation ended while OpenAI's continued through 2030. Recording this as OpenAI gaining independence captures half of a mutual release.

THE THESIS IT TESTS, AND THE FIRST EVIDENCE. Microsoft's substitution rests on a 'good-enough AI' bet: that enterprises locked into Office 365 will accept models 80–90% as capable as frontier models if they are cheaper and seen as safer. The first published numbers are split rather than confirming. On Microsoft's own tests MAI-Thinking-1 leads mid-tier peers at mathematics — AIME 2025 97.0%, above Claude Sonnet 4.6 (95.6%) and DeepSeek V3.2 (93.1%) — but TRAILS Claude Opus 4.6 (99.8%), and trails peers outright on graduate-level science and on agentic coding.

WIDER CONTEXT. The same margin logic appears elsewhere in the stack: DeepSeek is designing its own data-centre inference chips to reduce reliance on Nvidia and Huawei under US export controls, and Chinese labs (DeepSeek, Alibaba, Z.ai) are pushing cheaper customizable open-weight models into enterprises using coding as the entry point — with trust, not capability, cited as the counterweight, since OpenAI, Anthropic and Google have accused those providers of model distillation and the models carry weaker guardrails and jailbreak risk.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN — load-bearing:
- It does NOT claim the amendment was designed for this purpose, or that Microsoft negotiated it in order to build MAI. The records establish sequence and an explicitly stated link, not intent.
- It does NOT claim Microsoft has left OpenAI. Microsoft remains primary partner, products still ship first on Azure unless Microsoft declines, and OpenAI's payments to Microsoft continue through 2030.
- The benchmark figures are MICROSOFT'S OWN TESTS with no independent evaluations published at time of reporting, so 'good enough' is a thesis under test, not a demonstrated result — and it is currently NOT demonstrated for science or agentic coding, which is where enterprise agent workloads sit.
- The substitution announcement is per Bloomberg and its source record carries no independent sources; the amendment record likewise carries none despite high stated confidence.
- Cost is the STATED motivation. No independent cost figures appear in these records.
