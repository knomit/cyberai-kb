# Anthropic's most capable model drew ~6% of its own token spend, and three separate frictions attached to it — unmeasurable by evaluators, refused by one enterprise over retention, and undercut on price by its cheaper sibling

Four records each hold one part of this and none of them connects the parts. Assembled, they describe a flagship that was the least-used model in its own vendor's line, with three independent frictions on it.

THE UPTAKE FIGURE. Ramp's August 2026 AI spending index found ONLY ABOUT 6% of Anthropic token spend went to Fable 5, its most capable and most expensive model — against 25% of OpenAI tokens going to GPT-5.6 Sol. Ramp's lead economist Ara Kharazian called Fable 5 'a new upper bound' for AI spend, where more performance is not worth the price.

FRICTION 1 — IT COULD NOT BE INDEPENDENTLY MEASURED. Classifiers screen every Fable 5 prompt for cybersecurity, biology/chemistry, or AI-model-engineering content; flagged prompts are refused via API or SILENTLY ROUTED to the weaker Claude Opus 4.8 in Anthropic's own apps. Artificial Analysis observed ~8% fallback to Opus 4.8 and still ranked Fable 5 first on its Intelligence Index at 64.9 WITH those fallbacks counted. Vals AI reported ~100% refusals on biology and cybersecurity, and on GPQA Diamond Fable 5 fell from 93.18% (2nd place) to 55.56% (94th) once refusals were scored as failures; it refused ~35% of Agents' Last Exam tasks. ARC Prize DECLINED TO TEST IT AT ALL rather than accept the 30-day retention requirement.

FRICTION 2 — THE RETENTION TERMS COST AT LEAST ONE ENTERPRISE CUSTOMER. Anthropic moved from Zero Data Retention to MANDATORY 30-DAY RETENTION for Fable, Mythos and all future models of similar or higher capability, across first- and third-party services, stating it would not train on the data and would use it only to defend against 'complex and novel attacks.' Microsoft subsequently cut internal access to Fable 5, reportedly over data-retention concerns.

FRICTION 3 — ITS CHEAPER SIBLING TOOK THE WORK. Claude Opus 5, at roughly half Fable 5's rates, OVERTOOK FABLE 5 IN CORPORATE MODEL SPENDING WITHIN A MONTH of launch, attributed to low switching costs letting businesses route routine work cheaply while reserving premium systems for sustained-autonomy tasks.

WHY THIS IS WORTH RECORDING. A vendor's safety architecture is normally assessed on whether it prevents harm. Here the corpus contains the other half of the ledger for one model: what the safeguards and their accompanying terms cost in measurability, in at least one named customer, and — separately — what the price point cost in uptake. All three land on the same model at the same time.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN — load-bearing:
- THE CAUSAL LINK IS NOT ESTABLISHED. Ramp's own explanation for low Fable 5 uptake is PRICE, not safeguards or retention. This record does NOT claim the classifiers or the retention policy caused the 6% figure; it records that three frictions and low uptake co-occurred on one model, which is weaker and is all the sources support.
- It does NOT claim the safeguards were wrong or excessive. Fable 5 is the safeguarded public form of Mythos 5, a model Anthropic describes as the strongest cybersecurity model in the world; routing flagged requests down to Opus 4.8 is the mechanism that made public release possible at all.
- It does NOT claim Anthropic lost commercially. The same Ramp index has Anthropic AHEAD overall — 43.5% of its US business clients paying for Anthropic versus 39.7% for OpenAI. The finding is about one model inside a winning line.
- SOURCE QUALITY IS UNEVEN and the weakest sources carry the sharpest claims: the benchmarking record is confidence 0.6 with ZERO recorded sources and explicitly carries The Batch's editorial framing; the Microsoft withdrawal is 0.65 with zero sources and 'reportedly'; the retention-policy record is 0.55, single-source newsletter reporting. Only the Ramp index (2 sources) is firmly sourced.
- Ramp's customer base SKEWS toward startups and small businesses, so its spend shares should not be read as the enterprise market.
- Artificial Analysis ranked Fable 5 FIRST on its Intelligence Index even with fallbacks counted. 'Could not be independently measured' means evaluators disagreed sharply on how to score refusals — not that the model underperformed.
