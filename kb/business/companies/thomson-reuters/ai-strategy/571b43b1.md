# Thomson Reuters launched an in-house domain-specific LLM for about $40 million

On Monday 2026-08-24 Thomson Reuters launched Thomson, its first proprietary large language model. It was built in-house by taking an open-source frontier model as a foundation and continuing training on decades of proprietary content including Westlaw, Practical Law, Checkpoint and Reuters. CTO Joel Hron said starting from open-source frontier models lets the company train highly capable systems at a fraction of the cost of training from scratch; the effort cost roughly $40 million in talent and compute. Only about 10% of Thomson Reuters' content has been used so far, and the stated goal is to convert more content into targeted training data rather than simply add volume. Early evaluations put Thomson on par with the latest frontier models across a range of tasks, and comparisons of GPT-5.4, Sonnet 5 and Thomson 1.0 Large on web content versus Thomson Reuters content showed improvement in factuality or completeness in every instance. At launch it is available in CoCounsel Legal, built on the company's Fiduciary-Grade AI standard for regulated industries.
