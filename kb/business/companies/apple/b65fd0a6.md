# By August 2026 OpenAI was building hardware out of Apple's staff, shipping into Apple's messaging app, and outpacing Apple on agents — while Apple's own answer ran on foundation models it co-developed with Google

Seven separately-filed 2026 records describe one competitive situation from different angles. Assembled, they show Apple under pressure from OpenAI on three fronts at once, with its counter dependent on a third party.

FRONT 1 — TALENT AND IP OUTFLOW, NOW IN LITIGATION.
On 10 July 2026 Apple sued OpenAI in US District Court, alleging former Apple employees systematically took trade secrets to build OpenAI's hardware devices, which Jony Ive designed. The suit names OpenAI's chief hardware engineer (24 years at Apple) and Chang Liu (8 years at Apple, joined OpenAI's hardware team in January 2026), and states OpenAI has hired OVER 400 former Apple employees. Apple requested a jury trial and sent legal letters to dozens of those former employees instructing them to preserve documents. Separately, Apple Vision Pro executive Paul Meade reportedly left for OpenAI's hardware team. In early August Apple escalated, filing for a preliminary injunction to bar OpenAI and two former employees from accessing alleged confidential Apple information, plus expedited discovery; OpenAI responded publicly the next day, posting text messages and emails it presented as rebutting the accusations.

FRONT 2 — ENCROACHMENT INSIDE APPLE'S OWN SURFACE.
On 20 August 2026 OpenAI launched an Apple Messages plugin letting ChatGPT find specific messages, summarize conversations, and compose and send them, working with iMessage, SMS and RCS. It landed AHEAD of Apple's iOS 27 Siri overhaul, which was expected around the September iPhone event and promises comparable capabilities across the whole Apple device ecosystem rather than Mac only.

FRONT 3 — A CAPABILITY GAP APPLE HAS NOT CLOSED.
While OpenAI (Codex), Anthropic (Claude Cowork) and Perplexity (Personal Computer) all shipped agents that autonomously pursue multi-step goals, Apple's closest WWDC 2026 equivalent was a Shortcuts upgrade generating automations from a natural-language prompt — a goal-to-automation compiler, not a goal-pursuing agent.

THE DEPENDENCY THAT TIES THEM TOGETHER — CORRECTED. An earlier version of this record stated that Apple's Siri reboot 'uses GOOGLE GEMINI models under the hood', and said so in its title. THAT WAS WRONG, and the error is recorded here rather than erased because the wrong version may already have been read.

What the better-sourced records actually say: kb/business/companies/apple/ai-strategy/e1379d89.md (post-WWDC, confidence 0.93) states the rebuilt Siri is based on APPLE FOUNDATION MODELS CO-DEVELOPED WITH GOOGLE, running both on-device and via Apple's Private Cloud Compute servers. A further record establishes that AFM 3 is Apple's OWN ~20B mixture-of-experts model, co-developed with Google and DISTILLED FROM Gemini.

So the dependency is real but is NOT model-serving: Apple runs its own weights on its own silicon and its own Private Cloud Compute, and Google's contribution is co-development plus being the teacher model in a distillation. 'Apple's answer runs on Google's models' and 'Apple's answer runs on models Apple distilled from Google's, with Google's help' are materially different claims — the first implies Google could withdraw inference and break Siri, which does not follow from the second. The source of the original error was kb/technology/ai/products/consumer/mobile/597c3ffd.md, a PRE-WWDC PREVIEW at confidence 0.55, and the earlier justification that 'a second independent record corroborates it' was itself mistaken: 7e6d4a7c corroborates only that an iOS 27 Siri overhaul exists, not what powers it.

The broader point the front-3 framing was making survives the correction and is arguably sharpened: the platform defending against one AI company's encroachment built its defence on a second AI company's research collaboration and teacher model.

MEANWHILE, THE PRODUCT AT ISSUE. OpenAI's first hardware product is reported (Bloomberg, 15 July 2026, citing people familiar) as a mobile, screen-free smart speaker positioned as a new type of home computer — powered by ChatGPT and GPT-Live, portable with a rechargeable battery, with a camera and sensors for environmental awareness, designed with help from Ive's studio LoveFrom, and with a 'human-like' personality reportedly its most defining feature.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN — load-bearing:
- Apple's Siri does NOT run on Google-served Gemini inference. See the corrected dependency section above. Do not cite this record for that claim.
- OpenAI DENIED Apple's allegations, and the litigation is unresolved. Nothing here establishes that trade secrets were taken. Hiring 400 people from a company is not itself misappropriation, and the record does not distinguish ordinary competitive hiring from the conduct Apple alleges.
- The hardware device is REPORTED, not announced: it is one of five products OpenAI is working on, possibly unveiled in 2026 ahead of a 2027 release, and its roadmap is explicitly complicated by Apple's suit. The Paul Meade move is 'reportedly' and its source record carries no independent sources.
- Sources DISAGREE on the Messages plugin's distribution: The Deep View reported it limited to ChatGPT on Mac via ChatGPT Work and Codex, while 9to5Mac reported it available across all plans in the macOS desktop app. The plugin grants ChatGPT broad access to message history, carrying privacy and control risk particularly around persistent approval.
- The agents-gap assessment is a Deep View editorial judgement, not a measured comparison, and Apple's iOS 27 work was still unshipped at the time of these records.
- This record deliberately does NOT rely on the claim that Apple would launch an 'AI Extensions' App Store section turning Siri into an AI superapp. That prediction appears in the same pre-WWDC preview whose account of the keynote is contradicted by the post-event record, and the two also disagree on the keynote's date.
