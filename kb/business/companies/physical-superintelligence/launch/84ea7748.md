# Physical Superintelligence launched with $58M seed and an AI-planned Alpha Centauri trajectory

On September 1, 2026, AI-native physics research lab Physical Superintelligence (PSI) emerged from stealth with $58 million in seed funding led by Breakthrough Energy. It launched with two proofs of concept. The first is Emmy — named for Amalie Emmy Noether — which pairs PSI's reasoning engine (sovereign pre-trained and post-trained models) with a large curated inventory of simulations; CEO Matt Pines describes it as running research campaigns that decompose a problem, generate candidate approaches, and test them against simulation, live measurement, or machine-checked proof, with nothing counting as a result until it survives a check outside the model. A subset of Emmy is initially aimed at optimizing terrestrial and orbital AI data centers and factories, with signed commercial agreements and live pilots. The second is PSI's role as founding technical partner on the Fermi Explorer Mission, a nonprofit effort at the first privately funded interstellar mission, targeting Alpha Centauri (~4.37 light-years, roughly 80,000 years at current spacecraft speeds). PSI says it validated the mission physics and identified a substantially more efficient trajectory within mass and budget constraints; Fermi's technical team, out of Starcloud, verified the analysis, and the report was written to be rerunnable. PSI was founded by Matt Pines, Alex Klokus, and Dr. Alexander D. Wissner-Gross.
