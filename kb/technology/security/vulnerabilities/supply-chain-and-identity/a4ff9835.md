# Hacktron chained CVE-2026-32882 in libheif to OpenAI SSO to take over OpenAI staff ChatGPT/Codex accounts

In July 2026 three researchers at AI-assisted security firm Hacktron chained two separate weaknesses to take over the ChatGPT and Codex accounts of several OpenAI employees and reach an internal OpenAI code repository, reported by The Hacker News on 2026-09-19. The chain required BOTH components, and neither alone was sufficient: (1) a memory-corruption bug in the libheif image library, reached because Discourse (which runs OpenAI's public help forum) passes uploaded HEIC/HEIF images to ImageMagick, which uses libheif — tracked as CVE-2026-32882, rated 8.8 by Discourse's advisory as remote code execution, though libheif's own advisory and national vulnerability databases classify CVE-2026-32882 more narrowly as an out-of-bounds read that crashes or leaks adjacent memory rather than a direct code-execution bug; and (2) OpenAI's own single sign-on, because the forum offered a 'Sign in with OpenAI' option using the same SSO staff use elsewhere, so control of the forum server converted into takeover of any OpenAI-employee forum member's account with no victim interaction. Hacktron characterized step (2) as an OpenAI identity problem rather than a Discourse flaw: any first- or third-party service on that same sign-on could have granted equivalent access. This was coordinated security research, not a real-world attack — the team proved access with a single harmless pull request, read no source code, merged and shipped nothing, and touched no customer data. Time from first look to internal access was under 72 hours; OpenAI confirmed a fix roughly 14 hours after the report and paid a $6,500 bounty on 2026-09-01, stating the award 'recognizes the OpenAI-side finding, not the actions against Discourse' because testing the forum itself fell outside its bug bounty program. Operational consequence: SSO breadth is the amplifier here — an application-layer RCE on any low-value property federated into a corporate identity provider should be treated as a potential path to employee accounts, not as an isolated forum compromise. This does NOT mean Discourse or libheif alone granted access to OpenAI staff accounts.
