# Three 2026 admin-takeover chains share a two-stage shape — obtain an identity primitive without credentials, then promote it — and in all three the patch alone is not the whole remediation

Three separately-disclosed 2026 vulnerabilities produce full administrative control through the same two-stage mechanism rather than through a single flaw: stage one hands an unauthenticated caller some identity primitive (a token, a directory object, a session) that the system does not treat as privileged; stage two exploits a second defect to promote that primitive to administrator. In each case NEITHER FLAW IS SUFFICIENT ALONE.

CASE 1 — FreeIPA, CVE-2026-76578 (CVSS 9.8). A never-authenticated client creates a Kerberos identity of its own choosing in the directory and ends up in the administrators group. THE CHAIN REQUIRES BOTH: FreeIPA's overly permissive self-service OTP token access-control rule, AND CVE-2026-76560 (CVSS 7.5) in 389 Directory Server, where an anonymous client can satisfy an ownership check when the owner field is empty. Researchers reproduced it against a DEFAULT deployment and obtained reusable administrator Kerberos credentials; in SID-enabled environments PAC-bearing tickets may also be obtainable, expanding access to HTTP and Dogtag services. RHEL 7, 8, 9 and 10 IPA/IdM components affected; FreeIPA fixed its side in 4.13.4.

CASE 2 — JFrog Artifactory. CVE-2026-42018 makes Artifactory hand an internal anonymous-user token to a caller who has not logged in, EVEN WHEN ANONYMOUS ACCESS IS TURNED OFF. CVE-2026-42016 then lets that low-privilege token be exchanged for one with administrator scope, because Artifactory validates a token's signature and issuer but not the scope the token is actually allowed. Wiz observed exploitation between August 15 and September 8, 2026; attackers created persistent administrator accounts, installed malicious Groovy plugins for host code execution, and in multiple cases dropped a custom Rust backdoor. Because Artifactory is what build pipelines pull from, this is a build-supply-chain compromise.

CASE 3 — MikroTik RouterOS, 'MikroTrick'. CVE-2026-67276 and CVE-2026-86060 (both CVSS 9.2), two of six flaws CERT Polska disclosed on September 5, 2026, chain to bypass SSH authentication and obtain full administrative control with no credentials — ONLY on devices whose SSH service is reachable from the internet. Shadowserver counted more than 122,500 MikroTik devices with SSH exposed. CERT Polska dates successful attacks to at least September 2, 2026. Patched in RouterOS 7.25beta3, 7.24.2, 7.23.4, 7.23.5 and 6.49.21.

THE OPERATIONAL POINT THAT GENERALIZES — in all three, 'apply the patch' is an incomplete remediation, for a different reason each time:
- FreeIPA: the 389 Directory Server side is a SEPARATE fix, so patching FreeIPA alone does not address CVE-2026-76560.
- Artifactory: JFrog had already patched both flaws BEFORE the attacks began, so exposure came entirely from servers that had not been updated — and the attackers left administrator accounts and plugins behind, which updating does not remove.
- MikroTik: CERT Polska advises updating AND THEN auditing for unknown users and scripts, since patching does not evict an attacker who already has admin.
The common residue is that a chain ending in persistent administrative identity survives the patch that closed the path to it.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: three cases are three cases — this says nothing about what fraction of 2026 admin-takeover vulnerabilities were chains rather than single flaws, and no share or prevalence measurement supports such a claim. Single-flaw authentication bypasses of the same severity also occurred in this period and are NOT instances of this pattern; they are a different mechanism and should not be cited under it. Each case's preconditions above are load-bearing and must travel with any citation — in particular MikroTrick applies only to internet-exposed SSH, and the FreeIPA chain depends on the 389-ds empty-owner-field defect, not on FreeIPA alone.
