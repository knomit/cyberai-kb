# WeWorm: a zero-click WeChat VoIP flaw allowing account takeover and self-propagation via unanswered calls

Security firm Calif built and demonstrated WeWorm, which it describes as the first zero-click worm able to spread through WeChat calls on both iOS and Android. The underlying flaw is a MEMORY CORRUPTION BUG IN WECHAT'S VoIP STACK. Exploitation takes seconds and yields full control of the victim's WeChat account — reading and sending messages, making calls, and acting as the owner — after which the compromised account calls another contact and repeats, which is what makes it a worm.

THREE CONDITIONS BOUND THE THREAT AND MUST BE STATED TOGETHER. (1) The target does NOT have to answer or touch the phone: the exploit runs while the phone is RINGING. (2) DECLINING the call stops the infection; answering it OR letting it ring allows spread. (3) The caller must ALREADY BE ONE OF THE TARGET'S WECHAT CONTACTS (on the victim's friends list) — this is simultaneously what makes worm propagation possible along the contact graph and what bounds it to that graph. Dropping condition (3) turns a contact-graph worm into a false claim of arbitrary remote compromise.

DEMONSTRATED CHAIN: an Android phone called an iPhone and took over its WeChat; the compromised iPhone then called a second Android phone and took it over the same way — cross-platform propagation in both directions.

DISCOVERY EFFORT: Calif says AI assistance let them find the flaw and build an RCE exploit in about two days, then the worm in roughly another week.

DISCLOSURE AND FIX: Calif reported the flaw to Tencent in July 2026. Tencent shipped fixes on 2026-08-21 (Android 8.0.77, iOS 8.0.76), and Calif says Tencent has since blocked the exploit for all users.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: this is a RESEARCHER-BUILT PROOF OF CONCEPT. No real-world attacks using the flaw have been reported and there is no evidence of exploitation in the wild — it must not be cited as an observed campaign.
