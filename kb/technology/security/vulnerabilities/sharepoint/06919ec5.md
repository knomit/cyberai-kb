# Self-hosted SharePoint Server flaws in 2026 were repeatedly exploited after patches shipped, often once a public exploit appeared

MECHANISM: once a fix is released and a working exploit becomes public (or the flaw's true impact is clarified), the installed base that has not yet applied the fix becomes the target, so exploitation follows the patch rather than preceding it.

Three separate on-premises SharePoint Server flaws from the July and August 2026 update cycles fit this shape: two were exploited in the wild after researchers published proof-of-concept exploits for already-patched bugs, and a third, originally published as a low-impact spoofing issue, was confirmed as an actively exploited authenticated RCE weeks after its patch. Reporting describes these as part of a run of SharePoint flaws exploited during 2026.

OPERATIONAL CONSEQUENCE: for self-hosted SharePoint, the risk window is the interval between patch release and patch application; a vendor's initial severity label or the absence of known exploitation at release time is not a reliable signal to defer patching.

LOAD-BEARING QUALIFIERS carried from the members: the RCE flaws cited require an authenticated attacker (at least Site Owner for one), and the impersonation flaw allows disclosure and data modification but not availability impact. One flaw's advisory severity (spoofing, CVSS 6.5) understated its real impact (authenticated RCE).

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: this is not a zero-day pattern — these flaws were exploited after fixes existed, unlike campaigns in which a flaw is exploited before any advisory is published. It does not establish exploitation counts or which organizations were hit. A further SharePoint RCE added to CISA KEV in July 2026 is in the same product line, but its exploitation timing relative to its patch is not recorded, so it is not counted as an instance here.
