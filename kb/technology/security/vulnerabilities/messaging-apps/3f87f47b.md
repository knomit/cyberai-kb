# WeWorm zero-click WeChat worm spread via incoming VoIP calls on iOS and Android

Security firm Calif built and demonstrated WeWorm, which it describes as the first zero-click worm able to spread through WeChat calls on both iOS and Android. The underlying flaw is a memory corruption bug in WeChat's VoIP stack. The target does not have to answer or touch the phone — the exploit runs while the phone is ringing — but the caller must already be one of the target's WeChat contacts, which is what makes worm propagation possible and also bounds it. In the demo an Android phone called an iPhone and took over its WeChat, and the compromised iPhone then called a second Android phone and took it over the same way. Post-exploitation the attacker has full control of the WeChat account: reading and sending messages, making calls, and acting as the owner. Calif says AI assistance let them find the flaw and build an RCE exploit in about two days, then the worm in another week. Calif reported the flaw to Tencent in July 2026 and says Tencent has since blocked the exploit for all users. No real-world attacks using the flaw have been reported; this is researcher-built proof of concept, not observed exploitation.
