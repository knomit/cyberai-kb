# V8 is the repeatedly-exploited surface behind Chrome's 2026 zero-day run, and the ordinals show the cadence accelerating through the year

Three separately-recorded Chrome zero-days in 2026 were all actively exploited and all in V8, Chrome's JavaScript and WebAssembly engine. Read together, their ordinal positions in the year also trace an accelerating cadence.

THE THREE V8 CASES:
- CVE-2026-11645 (CVSS 8.8), patched June 9, 2026 in a 74-vulnerability release; recorded as the SECOND Chrome zero-day in active exploitation in 2026.
- CVE-2026-85046 (CVSS 8.8, type confusion), already under active exploitation when Google shipped a stable-channel update on September 3, 2026 fixing 12 vulnerabilities. Reported by Salvatore Gulizia (Serotav) on August 4, 2026; fixed in 152.0.7977.82/.83. CISA added it to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog within hours, with a September 18, 2026 remediation deadline for federal civilian agencies. Recorded as the SIXTH Chrome zero-day Google fixed in 2026.
- CVE-2026-87491 (out-of-bounds write, CWE-787), reported by Jihyeon Jeong of Seoul National University, fixed in Chrome 153 on September 8, 2026 (153.0.8010.36 Linux; 153.0.8010.36/.37 Windows and macOS). Google classified it medium severity while NVD-style scoring puts it at CVSS 8.8. Recorded as the SEVENTH actively exploited Chrome zero-day of 2026 and the SECOND IN UNDER FIVE DAYS.

THE CADENCE: going from the second such zero-day on June 9 to the seventh on September 8 puts roughly five in three months, with two inside a single five-day window — the interval between incidents is shortening within the year, on these records' own ordinals.

NOT CHROME-SPECIFIC AS A CLASS: Mozilla's Firefox 152.0.6 fixed CVE-2026-15718, an invalid pointer in the JavaScript/WebAssembly component, with exploit code PUBLICLY PUBLISHED though NO IN-THE-WILD ATTACKS were known at disclosure. The same engine class draws the same bug class in a different browser.

SCOPE LIMITS THAT MUST TRAVEL WITH THIS:
- A V8 flaw of this kind gives arbitrary code execution INSIDE the browser sandbox via a crafted HTML page. CVE-2026-87491 explicitly does NOT by itself break out of the sandbox; a full compromise still needs a separate escape.
- The ordinal counts come from three differently-sourced records using slightly different phrasings ('actively exploited' vs 'Chrome zero-day Google fixed'), and the CVE-2026-11645 record carries zero corroborating sources. Treat the cadence as indicative, not as an audited tally.
- This does NOT claim all seven 2026 Chrome zero-days were in V8 — engine attribution is established here for three of them only. Other Chrome fixes in the same period were elsewhere entirely: CVE-2026-15764 and CVE-2026-15765 were use-after-free bugs in Ozone, Chrome's display-server abstraction layer for Linux, ChromeOS and Fuchsia.
