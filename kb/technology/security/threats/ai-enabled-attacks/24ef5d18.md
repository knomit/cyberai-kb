# Four independently-reported 2026 cases show AI collapsing the time and skill cost of producing working offensive capability — an existence finding, not a prevalence one

Four separately-sourced 2026 reports each record AI being used to produce working offensive capability far faster or with less expertise than the same work previously required. The common mechanism is that the scarce input in offensive security — skilled human hours spent finding a bug, writing a reliable exploit, or driving a campaign — is the input AI substitutes for.

THE FOUR CASES, with their own conditions intact:
1. Google Threat Intelligence Group's Q3 2026 report found threat actors used multi-agent AI frameworks to autonomously execute a mass credential-harvesting attack in under six hours AFTER already compromising a cloud environment. The compression is of post-compromise campaign execution, not of initial access.
2. A suspected Russian-speaking actor used AI to build exploits chaining PaperCut CVE-2026-81578 (auth bypass) and CVE-2026-82078 (RCE), compromising 440+ instances across ten countries, mainly in education, from IP 45.142.193[.]132.
3. Security firm Calif says AI assistance let them find a memory-corruption flaw in WeChat's VoIP stack and build an RCE exploit in about two days, then the WeWorm zero-click worm in another week. This is researcher-built proof of concept reported to Tencent in July 2026 and since blocked — NOT observed in-the-wild exploitation. Propagation also required the caller to already be one of the target's WeChat contacts.
4. A MATS researcher turned a synthetic-transcript-generation prompt built FOR SAFETY RESEARCH into a universal jailbreak template achieving 84-100% attack success on the nine most vulnerable of 23 models tested; only recent Anthropic models and Meta's Muse Spark 1.1 were never fully broken.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN — the scope limit is load-bearing. These four cases establish that AI-assisted capability production EXISTS, is reported by independent parties, and carries specific measured time figures in two of the cases. They establish NOTHING about prevalence: what share of exploitation in 2026 was AI-assisted, whether AI-assisted attacks succeed more often, or whether defenders' costs moved in proportion. No share or flow measurement appears in any of the four. Per kb/meta/reasoning/bae0b862.md, a structural claim about dominance or displacement is settled by share or flow measurements, and observing that a thing has been attempted settles only that it was attempted. The honest record here is 'the capability is demonstrated and in use', not 'AI now drives offensive security'.

Also note case 4 is a different mechanism from 1-3: a dual-use research artifact generalizing into an attack, rather than an attacker directing a model. It is included because it exhibits the same cost collapse, not because the causal path is the same.
