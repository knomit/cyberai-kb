# LLM-assisted malware development now documented across both low and high sophistication threat actors

Two independent threat intelligence reports in May 2026 document adversarial use of LLMs to accelerate malware development at opposite ends of the sophistication spectrum. (1) GREYVIBE (low-to-moderate, Russian-linked): WithSecure assessed the group 'uses GenAI and LLMs to supercharge its operations' and 'augment malware development' — a low-sophistication actor offsetting capability gaps with AI tooling. (2) Kimsuky (highly sophisticated, DPRK state-sponsored): Kaspersky assessed HelloDoor, a Rust-based PebbleDash variant first seen August 2025, as 'likely developed using an LLM.' The pattern: LLMs are lowering the floor for threat actors who previously lacked the capability to write novel malware families in systems languages (Rust), and are giving already-capable actors a faster iteration loop. This represents a structural shift — AI-assisted development is no longer a differentiator for sophisticated actors but is becoming a baseline expectation across the threat landscape.
