# The most detailed campaign records in this corpus exist because the operators exposed their own working directories, so detail tracks attacker carelessness rather than attacker importance

Two campaigns documented by the same threat-intelligence firm were not discovered through victim-side detection. Both were reconstructed from infrastructure the ATTACKERS THEMSELVES LEFT OPEN.

CASE ONE. An unknown attacker installed 'Hermes', an autonomous AI agent, on a rented server and ran it in unattended 'YOLO' mode — disabling the approval prompts required before running risky commands — to target Thailand's Ministry of Finance for autonomous post-exploitation, using purpose-built scripts against MOF Hadoop infrastructure. The activity was UNCOVERED VIA THREE OPEN DIRECTORIES hosted on AS132883 (TOPIDC).

CASE TWO. 'Operation CameraSwarm' compromised more than 14,530 Dahua devices between June 17 and July 22, 2026, by three attack paths (credential attacks across 12,324 unique IPs and 13,229 campaign records; authentication bypass via CVE-2021-33044 and CVE-2021-33045 against 1,923 cameras; and P2P relay reaching 283 cameras identified by serial number, including devices behind NAT). It was RECONSTRUCTED FROM A 407 MB EXPOSED WORKING DIRECTORY holding 2,616 files across 234 subdirectories — tooling, logs, shell history and campaign records.

THE CONSEQUENCE NEITHER RECORD STATES. The unusual precision of the second record — exact device counts, per-path breakdowns, the operators' own campaign logs — is available PRECISELY BECAUSE the operators exposed it. A campaign run with disciplined operational security yields no such directory, and therefore no such record. So within this corpus, THE DEPTH OF DETAIL IN A CAMPAIGN RECORD IS EVIDENCE ABOUT THE OPERATOR'S CARELESSNESS, NOT ABOUT THE CAMPAIGN'S SCALE OR IMPORTANCE. Rich records are a sample of the operators who slipped.

WHY THIS IS LOAD-BEARING. This corpus holds several syntheses characterising 2026 attacker tradecraft, each assembled from records of this kind. Any claim of the form 'attackers increasingly do X' inherits this selection effect: it describes the tradecraft of operators observable through their own mistakes. The bias runs in a specific direction — operators who leave working directories open are unlikely to be a random sample of operators on any axis, including sophistication, and the corpus separately records at least one further case of this discovery channel (a misconfigured server exposing three Evilginx phishing operations targeting Microsoft 365), indexed under the motif `convenience-defaults-expose-operation`.

SCOPE AND CAVEATS: two seed cases from one firm, plus the third noted above; this is not a measured share of threat intelligence, and no count of victim-detected versus operator-exposed campaigns exists in this corpus. Both seed records are single- or double-sourced. The Hermes case's discovery channel is one clause of its record and was not the reporter's emphasis.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: this does NOT claim victim-side detection contributes little — the corpus holds many vendor-telemetry records and this fact does not weigh the two channels. It does NOT claim the operators in these two cases were unsophisticated; running an autonomous agent for post-exploitation and compromising 14,530 devices are not the marks of incapable actors, and leaving a directory open is a discrete failure, not a general one. It does NOT claim the tradecraft syntheses in this corpus are wrong, only that their sampling frame is narrower than 'attackers'. And it does not generalise beyond this corpus to the threat-intelligence field.

Falsification: a record showing that campaigns discovered by victim telemetry and campaigns discovered by exposed attacker infrastructure carry comparable levels of documented detail would remove the asymmetry this fact rests on.
