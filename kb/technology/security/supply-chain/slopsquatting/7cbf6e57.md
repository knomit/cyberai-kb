# Squatting on package names that AI coding assistants hallucinate — reported under two names, 'slopsquatting' and 'HalluSquatting' — with Socket finding 53 of 127 hallucinated names still registrable

MERGED from two 2026 records of the same attack class, published under DIFFERENT NAMES by different parties. BOTH NAMES ARE PRESERVED because each appears in the literature and a reader may encounter either.

THE MECHANISM. AI coding assistants frequently hallucinate plausible-sounding but NON-EXISTENT package or tool names. An attacker can PRE-REGISTER those names in a public registry, so that when an assistant later recommends the name, the developer installs attacker-controlled code.

NAME 1 — 'SLOPSQUATTING' (Socket research). Socket tested frontier models — Claude Sonnet 4.6, Claude Haiku 4.5, GPT-5.4-mini, Gemini 2.5 Pro and DeepSeek V3.2 — and found they hallucinate non-existent package names. Of 127 NAMES IDENTIFIED, 53 WERE STILL AVAILABLE FOR REGISTRATION AS OF APRIL 2026 — 41 on PyPI and 12 on npm. The load-bearing amplifier: BECAUSE MULTIPLE MODELS INVENT THE SAME NAMES, ONE MALICIOUS REGISTRATION COULD TARGET USERS ACROSS PROVIDERS.

NAME 2 — 'HALLUSQUATTING' (reported by The Hacker News, 2026-07-09). Researchers disclosed the same pre-registration technique, with the reported payload being attacker-controlled BOTNET MALWARE delivered when the assistant installs the squatted name.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: the 53-of-127 figure is Socket's measurement as of APRIL 2026 and is a snapshot of registrability, NOT a count of names actually registered maliciously or of victims. The cross-provider targeting claim follows from models converging on the same invented names, which Socket observed in its own sample and is not established as a general rate. The HalluSquatting record carried ZERO recorded sources in this corpus and gave no measurements; the botnet payload is as-reported. Treating the two names as two independent corroborations would be a mistake — they are two reports of one attack class.
