# One SaaS vendor was compromised twice in a month at two different layers, each reaching a different downstream population — so an all-clear at one layer is not an all-clear for the vendor

MECHANISM: a customer's exposure to a service provider is not a single relationship but one per layer at which that provider holds a credential or a trust relationship on the customer's behalf; the layers fail independently, reach different downstream populations, and remediating one says nothing about the others.

Within one month, one email and customer-engagement provider suffered two separate compromises at two distinct layers:

- IDENTITY LAYER. A flaw in how the provider handled SAML single sign-on let attackers reach a set of customer accounts. The consequences narrowed in stages across that set — accounts accessed, a smaller number with contacts exported, a smaller number still used to send phishing to those contacts. Those three figures are distinct and must travel together: collapsing them to the largest overstates the harm, collapsing to the smallest understates the data loss. Downstream victims were the customers' own contacts.

- EDGE / CONTENT-DELIVERY LAYER. A compromised CDN API key let an attacker deploy edge code on the provider's account for a matter of hours, injecting a malicious script into the provider's own pages and into JavaScript files that customers embed on their own websites. Reach extended to the provider's site, to every customer site embedding that JavaScript, and into links inside campaign email — including the unsubscribe link. A vendor compromise scoped to 'their infrastructure' is not scoped to their infrastructure when customers embed the vendor's JavaScript.

THE DISTINCTION IS THE POINT, not a caveat on it: the identity-layer takeover hit a different set of victims than the edge-layer injection, so remediating one says nothing about the other. Do not compress this into 'the vendor was breached' — that phrasing implies one event, one victim set, and one fix, and each of those is wrong here.

WHAT FOLLOWS: when assessing a third-party provider, enumerate the layers at which it can act for you — identity federation, embedded client-side code, outbound messaging, stored data — and treat each as a separate exposure with its own detection and its own remediation. A vendor incident notice that names one layer leaves the others unaddressed.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: the two incidents are not asserted to share an attacker, a root cause, or a common entry point; the corpus records them as separate, and nothing here claims one enabled the other. Provenance — the provider and vendor names, dates, exact counts, affected domains, and the researchers' characterisations — stays in the member facts cited below.
