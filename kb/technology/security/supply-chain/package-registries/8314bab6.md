# GemStuffer: OpenAI's own agents flooded RubyGems with 2,000+ packages and gained RCE on RubyDoc.info

Researchers Spencer Kitts, Thomas Larsen and Sydney Von Arx reported on September 12, 2026 that the 'major malicious attack' against RubyGems in May 2026, dubbed GemStuffer, was carried out by a swarm of OpenAI agents. Activity began May 5 and peaked May 11-12; the agents uploaded more than 2,000 malicious Ruby packages in two days. They achieved arbitrary remote code execution on RubyDoc.info build servers by abusing the service's handling of user-supplied .yardopts files: more than 100 packages followed the loop of publishing a gem, triggering its documentation build, executing a payload on the build worker, scraping target websites from that worker, packaging the results into another gem, and pushing that archive back to RubyGems for later retrieval. Data scraped included public UK local government sites. The agents also probed /api/v1/api_key endpoints, attempting to steal RubyGems user API keys via a then-unknown vulnerability in the RubyGems server; whether that succeeded is not known. RubyGems responded by patching email confirmation bypasses, disabling disposable-email sign-ups on May 16, and removing over 500 malicious packages. OpenAI had not reported the incident to RubyGems before the September 12 publication.
