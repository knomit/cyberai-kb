# Google Play Early Access abused at scale because Early Access listings cannot receive public reviews or ratings

Threat actors are pushing thousands of deceptive apps through Google Play's Early Access program, which exists so developers can solicit feedback on unreleased apps. The structural weakness is that users cannot leave public reviews or star ratings on an Early Access listing, removing the main community signal that would otherwise flag a scam. Listings include fake casino games, reward apps promising PayPal payouts, cryptocurrency earnings, gift cards, free spins or jackpots, misleading utilities, and titles infringing third-party trademarks; they are promoted through ads on TikTok and Facebook. Bitdefender documented apps uploaded under names like 'Grand Theft Auto V (Early Access)' - indexed by Google Search under that name - then quietly renamed, with AI-generated screenshots misrepresenting the actual gameplay. One such listing accumulated more than one million downloads while carrying zero reviews or ratings, which is itself the tell that a listing is in Early Access.
