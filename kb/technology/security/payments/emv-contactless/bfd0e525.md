# Zombie Card attack revives expired Visa contactless cards by rewriting an unsigned expiry field

University of Massachusetts Amherst researchers demonstrated 'Zombie Card', an attack that lets expired Visa contactless cards complete real in-store purchases without breaking any cryptography. Card expiry appears twice in a Visa contactless transaction and the two copies are consumed by different parties: the terminal checks the Application Expiration Date in TLV tag 5F24, while the issuer derives expiry from Track 2 Equivalent Data in tag 57. Visa's Kernel 3 does not require the two to be consistently bound, and the fast Dynamic Data Authentication (fDDA) signature the terminal verifies excludes 5F24 entirely. A man-in-the-middle relay rewrites the terminal-facing date to any future value while leaving Track 2 untouched, so both the card signature and the issuer-verified cryptogram still validate. Kernel 3 also specifies Terminal Verification Results forwarded to the issuer are all zeros, so the bank cannot see whether the terminal ran or failed its expiry check. The attacker need not know the replacement card's real expiry — any date after the transaction date works. Preconditions: physical possession or sustained NFC proximity to the expired card, a MitM relay, the account remaining open under the same PAN (standard when an issuer sends a replacement), and the issuing bank not independently re-checking expiry at authorization.
