# "Zombie Card" attack revives expired Visa contactless cards by rewriting the expiry date over NFC

Researchers at the University of Massachusetts Amherst, led by Raja Hasnain Anwar, demonstrated an attack they call "Zombie Card" that lets expired Visa contactless credit cards complete real in-store purchases.

The attack rewrites the expiration date that a point-of-sale terminal reads over NFC. It does not break any of the card's cryptography. Preconditions:
- physical possession of the expired card, or sustained NFC proximity to it
- a man-in-the-middle relay positioned between the card and the terminal
- the account must remain open under the same primary account number (PAN) — standard practice when an issuer sends a replacement card
- the issuing bank must not independently re-check the expiry during authorization

The paper's preliminary study spans five major US banks and tests general tampering with EMV (Europay, Mastercard, Visa) transactions.
