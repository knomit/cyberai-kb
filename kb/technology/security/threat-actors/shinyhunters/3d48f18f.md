# ShinyHunters is credited with both a zero-day exploitation track and a no-vulnerability access-abuse track in the same period, so an actor profile built from either one prescribes the wrong defence

Two 2026 records attribute activity to the same extortion crew by methods that are opposites, and a defender who has read only one of them will prioritise the wrong control.

TRACK A — EXPLOITATION OF AN UNPATCHED FLAW. ShinyHunters, tracked by Google Mandiant as UNC6240, exploited CVE-2026-35273 in Oracle PeopleSoft Enterprise PeopleTools (CVSS 9.8, unauthenticated RCE) between 27 May and 9 June 2026. Oracle did not publish its advisory until 10 June, so it was a zero-day for the whole campaign. The bug needs no login and no user interaction, only HTTP network access, and exposure comes from an internet-reachable Environment Management Hub. Universities were hit hardest.

TRACK B — NO FLAW EXPLOITED AT ALL. Microsoft mapped three Salesforce attack paths tied to a year of ShinyHunters activity in which attackers 'walked into corporate Salesforce environments without exploiting any flaw in the platform itself, relying instead on social engineering and abuse of legitimate access.'

WHY THIS MATTERS: the two tracks call for disjoint defences. Track A is answered by inventorying internet-exposed enterprise back-office endpoints and shortening the patch cycle — and, because it ran as a zero-day, by reducing exposure of such hubs rather than by patching speed alone. Track B is answered by connected-app inventory, grant scope reduction, revocation speed, and social-engineering resistance; no patching programme touches it. A team that characterises this actor from one track and sizes its programme accordingly leaves the other track uncovered.

SCOPE AND CAVEATS: the Track B attribution is explicitly to 'ShinyHunters (AND LOOKALIKES)' — Microsoft's mapping does not isolate this crew, so the two tracks may not share operators, and this record should NOT be read as establishing that one team ran both. The PeopleSoft record carries sources: 0 in this corpus and the Salesforce record likewise; both are thin on provenance. Track A is a dated ~two-week campaign; Track B is a year-long body of activity, so the periods overlap but are not co-extensive. WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: this does not claim the actor has abandoned either method, that the two tracks were coordinated, or that method diversity is unusual for extortion crews — it claims only that this corpus attributes both to this name, and that a defence sized from one is blind to the other.
