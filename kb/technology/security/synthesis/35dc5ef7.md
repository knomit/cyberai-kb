# AI is simultaneously a cybercrime accelerant and a national-security export-control concern (2026)

By mid-2026, AI sits on both sides of the security ledger. Offensively, criminals weaponize AI for scale: Google sued operators who abused Gemini to mass-generate phishing pages and run smishing via a cheap ($88/week) phishing-as-a-service kit, paralleling persistent PhaaS operations like Sniper Dz targeting MENA users. Defensively/strategically, frontier model capability itself became a regulated concern when the US Commerce Department imposed export controls on Anthropic's Fable 5 and Mythos 5 models over fears they let even low-skill attackers breach enterprise defenses. The convergence: the same capability that lowers the barrier to cyberattacks is now driving both law-enforcement action against AI-enabled crime and government restriction of advanced models.
