# SNMPv3 leaks pre-authentication signals that narrow brute-force attacks

Malanta research found that SNMPv3 — widely regarded as the secure replacement for earlier SNMP versions for managing routers, switches and firewalls — leaks pre-authentication signals letting an unauthenticated remote actor identify a device's vendor, confirm which usernames are valid, and narrow the device's likely encryption settings before testing a single credential. The behaviors are standards-compliant, not a vendor bug, so upgrading to SNMPv3 as CISA advisory AA26-194A urges does not remove them. The findings were validated across approximately 470,000 internet-exposed endpoints. The effect is to collapse a multi-dimensional brute-force search into a focused password-guessing exercise; the mitigation implied is limiting internet exposure of the management plane rather than relying on the protocol version alone.
