# Dropcatch domains are ~1 in 5 new daily registrations, and one actor tracked as Sable Squirrel spent close to $7 million buying them — the market size and the case study are both from Infoblox

MERGED from two Infoblox findings that were carried as separate records with no cross-reference: the ecosystem-wide volume statistics and the single-actor case study that gives them meaning. Neither number reads correctly alone — a $7 million domain spend is only interpretable against the size of the market it was spent in.

THE CONCEPT. Infoblox coined the term 'DROPCATCH DOMAINS' for expired domains that are RE-REGISTERED BY A NEW PARTY, inheriting the prior owner's traffic and reputation.

THE VOLUME. In the first half of 2026, about 50,400 dropcatch domains were re-registered DAILY in generic TLDs like .com alone, rising to roughly 65,000 PER DAY including country-code TLDs — NEARLY 20% of all daily gTLD and ccTLD registrations, i.e. ONE IN FIVE newly registered domains.

WHERE. At the TLD level .net and .xyz lead dropcatch activity, AHEAD OF .com IN THIRD PLACE; other prominent TLDs include .org, .vip, .online, .store, .site, .app and .shop. Median daily dropcatch volumes by registrar: GoDaddy 5,246, Namecheap 4,385, DropCatch.com 3,568.

THE ACTOR CASE. Infoblox assesses that a threat actor it tracks as SABLE SQUIRREL has spent CLOSE TO $7 MILLION on expired (dropcatch) domains, building a criminal enterprise spanning ILLEGAL SPORTS STREAMING, ONLINE GAMBLING PROMOTION, AND MALWARE INFRASTRUCTURE.

WHAT THE MONEY BUYS. Infoblox notes the spend buys AGED REGISTRATION HISTORY, BACKLINKS, RESIDUAL TRAFFIC, AND REPUTATION SIGNALS that many defenses still treat as indicators of trustworthiness. Beyond inherited reputation, dropped domains also carry LINGERING CONNECTIONS: email intended for the original holder, cached search results, inherited web traffic, lingering DNS records, and in some cases A READY-MADE PLATFORM FOR CODE INJECTION on already-compromised sites.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: both findings come from Infoblox and are single-sourced in this corpus. The one-in-five figure counts dropcatch registrations generally, the overwhelming majority of which are ordinary commercial re-registration, NOT abuse — it is a measure of how large the reputation-inheriting supply is, not of how much of it is criminal. The Sable Squirrel spend figure is Infoblox's assessment, not an audited number, and one actor's spend is not a rate for the market.
