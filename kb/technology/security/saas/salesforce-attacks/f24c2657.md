# Both 2026 Salesforce data-loss tracks ran on granted access, with neither side claiming a platform vulnerability

Two separately reported 2026 tracks of Salesforce customer-data loss agree on the point that determines which controls would have helped: no flaw in the platform was exploited in either.

(1) THIRD-PARTY GRANT. On 11 June 2026 Salesforce disabled the Klue Battlecards app integration after detecting unusual activity that may have led to unauthorized access to a subset of customer data via the app's connection to Salesforce. The extortion group 'Icarus' compromised and exfiltrated data from Klue customers — including the cybersecurity firm Huntress — with copied data including business contacts and price quotes. The path in was the app's own authorized connection.

(2) LEGITIMATE ACCESS. Microsoft mapped three Salesforce attack paths tied to a year of ShinyHunters activity (and lookalikes) in which the attackers 'walked into corporate Salesforce environments without exploiting any flaw in the platform itself, relying instead on social engineering and abuse of legitimate access.'

WHAT FOLLOWS: for this platform, over this period, the surface that produced the losses was the set of standing grants — third-party OAuth connections and users' own authenticated sessions — not the platform's code. A defender whose SaaS programme consists of tracking the vendor's CVEs and applying its patches would have had no coverage of either track. The controls with purchase are inventory of connected apps and what each was granted, scope reduction on those grants, revocation speed once an integration is suspect, and social-engineering resistance for the humans holding legitimate access.

SCOPE AND CAVEATS: 'no platform vulnerability' in track (1) is SALESFORCE'S OWN characterization of an incident in its own product, which is a self-interested attribution and should not be treated as an independent finding; track (2) is Microsoft's mapping as reported by The Hacker News. Both source records carry sources: 0 in this corpus — thin provenance on both sides. This covers two reported tracks in 2026, not a census of how Salesforce environments are compromised. WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: this does not claim Salesforce has no exploitable vulnerabilities, that platform patching is unnecessary, or that the pattern generalizes to other SaaS platforms without their own evidence. It also does not claim the two tracks are related, share actors, or share tooling — Icarus and ShinyHunters are distinct attributions.
