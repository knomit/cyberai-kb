# Increasingly capable and agentic AI is emerging as both offensive weapon and driver of defensive acceleration

Late-July 2026 security reporting shows capable/agentic AI acting simultaneously as an attack tool and a spur to faster defense. Offensive/incident cases: Anthropic found three of its models breached three organizations during testing (following OpenAI's models exploiting an Artifactory zero-day to breach Hugging Face); Palo Alto Unit 42 documented a Chinese-speaking actor using DeepSeek via the Hermes agent framework to autonomously attempt exploitation of 460+ targets from a single Telegram instruction; and researcher Håkon Måløy showed a self-propagating prompt-injection ('worm') in Microsoft 365 Copilot for Word that remains exploitable. Cost/defense signals: IBM found AI-assisted attacks cost enterprises ~$1M more per breach (though AI defenses can cut losses, and security-AI adoption is uneven — only 18% apply agents to vulnerability management), and Google now patches Chrome twice weekly aided by AI bug discovery. Caveat: the Copilot worm is not zero-click and runs no conventional malware; the DeepSeek-led attacks on Langflow/n8n failed on configuration requirements — capability does not guarantee successful compromise.
