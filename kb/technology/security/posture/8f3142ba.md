# In two 2026 unauthenticated-root cases, internet exposure — not the CVSS number — was what separated a listed flaw from an exploited one

Two independently-documented 2026 vulnerability episodes share a mechanism: a network service that performs authentication is reachable from the internet, an unauthenticated attacker reaches root through it, and BOTH the recorded severity and the observed exploitation track the exposure of the listening service rather than the defect itself. In both cases the source records independently reach the same operational conclusion — restricting reachability was the higher-value control than patch urgency ranking.

CASE 1 — PAN-OS CVE-2026-0300, a buffer overflow in the User-ID Authentication Portal (Captive Portal service) of PA-Series and VM-Series firewalls, giving unauthenticated root code execution via crafted packets. SEVERITY IS EXPLICITLY CONFIGURATION-DEPENDENT: CVSS 9.3 if the Authentication Portal is internet-accessible, 8.7 if restricted to trusted internal IPs. Palo Alto Networks observed 'limited exploitation' SPECIFICALLY TARGETING instances where the portal is publicly accessible. Exploitation attempts began as early as 9 April 2026 and fixes shipped starting 13 May 2026 — roughly five weeks of in-the-wild exploitation against an unpatched internet-facing RCE, during which the only available control WAS exposure restriction. Censys detected roughly 263,000 internet-exposed PAN-OS hosts; not all run the vulnerable portal configuration, but the figure bounds the target population.

CASE 2 — macOS Screen Sharing CVE-2026-65400 (CVSS 9.8), an authentication issue letting an attacker ALREADY ON THE NETWORK authenticate to the built-in remote desktop service without valid credentials, fixed in an emergency update on 6 August 2026 (macOS Tahoe 26.6.1, Sequoia 15.7.9, Sonoma 14.8.9). NCSC-NL reported abuse ACROSS MULTIPLE SYSTEMS WITH PORT 5900 EXPOSED TO THE INTERNET, where attackers obtained root and installed a Monero cryptocurrency miner. The source record's own conclusion: an internet-exposed port 5900 is the single highest-value control point, because it is the precondition shared by the observed exploitation.

THE GENERALIZABLE READING: for an authentication-facing network service, reachability is a severity input, not merely a mitigating factor — and where a vendor scores the same defect differently by exposure, that difference is the actionable number.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN — the caveats from both sources travel with this:
- PAN-OS CVE-2026-0300 HAS SINCE BEEN PATCHED; the five-week window is history, not current status.
- In the macOS cluster, ONLY CVE-2026-65400 has reported in-the-wild exploitation. The companion Tahoe 26.6 bugs, including the post-authentication root-RCE path CVE-2026-43760, are NOT documented as exploited, and CVE-2026-43760 requires ALL of: Screen Sharing or Remote Management enabled, the 'VNC viewers may control screen with password' option configured, AND the attacker already possessing that VNC password. It must not be cited as an exposure-only risk.
- No details are available on the timing or scale of the macOS activity, whether CVE-2026-65400 was a zero-day, or whether the observed activity went beyond cryptomining.
- Two cases are two cases: this establishes the mechanism, not how often exposure rather than patch state is the deciding factor across vulnerabilities generally.
