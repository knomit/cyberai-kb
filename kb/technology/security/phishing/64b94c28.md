# RecruitTrap shows scheduling-tool impersonation as a scalable phishing pattern: one SaaS brand, shared kit, MFA relay

CTM360's RecruitTrap campaign illustrates a phishing model organized around impersonating a single low-suspicion SaaS workflow rather than a target's own employer. The lure layer and the infrastructure layer reinforce each other.

Lure: victims receive an unsolicited recruiter email or meeting invite referencing their professional background, impersonating real recruiters and recruitment processes at more than 50 organizations across 14 sectors. They land on either a counterfeit Calendly-style scheduling page or a brand-specific recruitment portal; both funnel to 'Continue with Google/Facebook', where a Browser-in-the-Browser technique renders a fake authentication popup with spoofed address bar and padlock (a full-screen counterfeit login page on mobile). In advanced cases the kit relays MFA prompts in real time, capturing the victim's code, yielding an authenticated session, then redirecting the victim to a legitimate Calendly page to reduce suspicion. Marketing professionals were the majority of observed targets — likely deliberate, since compromised marketing accounts grant access to advertising platforms, corporate social media, customer data and email.

Infrastructure: 3,000+ phishing URLs over two months, about 96% Calendly-themed, many fronted by Cloudflare to conceal the real servers. The brand-specific portal variant used 116 unique observed hosts, 93.1% dedicated or registered hosts and 50.9% on AWS EC2 IP addresses and ranges. Across 813 deduplicated registered domains, .cfd led at 40%, then .com (25.1%), .info (15.1%), .works (10.5%) and .work (6.3%). Repeated hostnames and reused infrastructure indicate deployment through a shared setup.

The pattern worth carrying forward: impersonating a widely-used neutral scheduling intermediary lets one kit target employees of 50+ organizations without per-employer customization — the impersonated brand is decoupled from the victim's employer, so scale comes from the SaaS brand's ubiquity rather than from reconnaissance depth. Correspondingly, the highest-yield hunting signals are infrastructure-side (the .cfd/.works/.work TLD skew, EC2 hosting concentration, repeated hostnames) rather than content-side, and real-time MFA relay means one-time codes provide no protection here — only phishing-resistant authentication such as passkeys/FIDO2 would. WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: the MFA-relay behavior is described for advanced cases, not all 3,000+ URLs; the 96% Calendly-theme figure covers observed phishing pages while the 116-host and 813-domain breakdowns describe the brand-specific portal variant and deduplicated registered domains respectively, so the percentages are not interchangeable across those populations. Calendly, Cloudflare and AWS are impersonated or abused here, not complicit.
