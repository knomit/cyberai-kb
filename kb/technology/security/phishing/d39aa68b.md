# Phishing in 2026 increasingly runs inside genuine vendor channels, so 'is this page/message real?' stops being a useful user check

Two 2026 campaigns targeting Microsoft ecosystems share a structural move: the attacker does not counterfeit the trusted surface, they route the attack through the real one. The Kali365 phishing kit, targeting US organizations, abuses Microsoft device-code authentication — the victim approves an attacker-controlled device code on Microsoft's genuine authentication page, so a legitimate Microsoft login becomes the gateway to corporate data. Separately, eSentire reports a significant increase in Microsoft Teams-based phishing since early 2026, where actors impersonate IT support and help desk personnel inside Teams itself: email bombing, then a Teams message from fake IT support offering to resolve the 'issue', then remote access granted by the user, then data exfiltration and/or ransomware deployment.

The implication for defense: user-facing authenticity heuristics — checking the URL, looking for the padlock, confirming the message arrived in a sanctioned corporate tool — fail against both, because in each case the page or platform genuinely is Microsoft's. What the victim is actually authorizing is the thing to scrutinize: an unfamiliar device code, or a remote-access session requested by someone claiming to be IT. Controls that hold are therefore procedural and policy-side — restricting or monitoring device-code authentication flows, external/federated messaging in Teams, and out-of-band verification for any unsolicited IT support contact — rather than anything that depends on the user spotting a fake.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: these are two separate campaigns with no reported link to one another; the connection is a shared technique class, not shared attribution or infrastructure. Microsoft's authentication service and Teams are being abused as designed-legitimate channels here, not compromised or breached. The Teams pattern is eSentire's reported trend since early 2026, and the Kali365 device-code targeting is reported against US organizations — neither is characterized as universal.
