# Two independent teams defeated third-party scanners for malicious AI-agent skills — one bypassed every static scanner tested >90% of the time, the other pushed rogue skills to live marketplaces; a runtime checker fared better than static analysis

MERGED from two independently reported studies of the same finding, which were carried as separate records with no cross-reference. Treating them as ONE conclusion from TWO teams is the point; treating them as one study would understate the evidence, and leaving them apart hid that the finding is independently replicated.

## WHAT A 'SKILL' IS AND WHY IT MATTERS
Skills are small packages — a Markdown instruction file plus scripts — loaded by AI coding agents such as CLAUDE CODE, OPENAI CODEX and OPENCLAW. They run WITH THE AGENT'S FULL ACCESS to files, terminal, and saved passwords. That access level is what makes scanner reliability load-bearing.

## STUDY 1 — TRAIL OF BITS, LIVE MARKETPLACES
Trail of Bits BYPASSED THREE SEPARATE DETECTORS — ClawHub's malicious-skill detector, Cisco's agent skill scanner, and the scanners in skills.sh — to PUSH ROGUE SKILLS TO PUBLIC MARKETPLACES and steal data from developer systems. One malicious skill used PROMPT INJECTION TO CONVINCE THE GUARD MODEL its payload was harmless, instructing the agent to point its package managers (npm and yarn) at an ATTACKER-CONTROLLED REGISTRY, disguised as corporate environment/VPN configuration.

## STUDY 2 — HKUST, 'SKILLCLOAK'
Researchers at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology showed the same class of scanner can be defeated systematically. Their SKILLCLOAK technique, using SELF-EXTRACTING PACKING, slipped past EVERY TESTED STATIC SCANNER MORE THAN 90% OF THE TIME.

## THE NUANCE THAT MUST NOT BE LOST
The HKUST team ALSO BUILT A RUNTIME CHECKER THAT CATCHES MOST DISGUISED SKILLS. So the finding is NOT 'skill scanning is impossible' — it is that STATIC, PRE-INSTALL scanning is the layer that fails, because the malicious behaviour is assembled or unpacked after inspection, and because a guard MODEL can be argued out of its verdict. Runtime observation of what a skill actually does is the layer that held up in testing.

## THE PRACTICAL TAKEAWAY
TRUST CANNOT BE OUTSOURCED TO A THIRD-PARTY SCANNER. Organisations should instead CURATE SKILL MARKETPLACES FROM TRUSTWORTHY OPEN-SOURCE COLLECTIONS, and weight runtime behavioural checks above static pre-install scanning.

## WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN
- The >90% bypass figure is HKUST's against THE STATIC SCANNERS THEY TESTED, not a universal rate and not a claim about runtime or behavioural detection.
- Trail of Bits' result is a demonstration against THREE NAMED detectors; it does not establish that all marketplace scanners are equally weak.
- Neither study reports in-the-wild victims — these are researcher demonstrations, and the rogue skills were pushed by the researchers themselves.
- Both input records carried low source counts in this corpus (one source and zero recorded sources respectively); the value here is the INDEPENDENT REPLICATION of the conclusion, not the depth of either record's sourcing.
