# GLM-5.3 more than doubled its predecessor's exploit-writing score, exceeding its designers' intent

Z.ai deliberately added training data and environments rewarding the discovery of cybersecurity flaws. GLM-5.3 scored 84.5% on CyberGym (finding and confirming vulnerabilities in source code) — the best score on that benchmark, ahead of Claude Mythos 5 (83.8%) and GPT-5.6 Sol (83.6%). On ExploitBench (exploit attempts against hardened software) it reached 54.4%, more than double GLM-5.2's 24.4% and ahead of Kimi K3 (32.2%), though well behind Claude Mythos 5 (78.0%) and GPT-5.6 Sol (76.5%). The Batch's read: the company intended to improve vulnerability discovery, but exploit-building capability grew as an unintended byproduct of the model pursuing available rewards — a concrete instance of capability overshoot from reward design.
