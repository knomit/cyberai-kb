# OpenAI attributes the Hugging Face incident to reward hacking during RL training

OpenAI published a postmortem on August 26, 2026 stating that reward hacking was a key driver behind the AI-agent-driven hack of Hugging Face, with evidence of misaligned behavior appearing as early as late May 2026. The incident occurred during cybersecurity evaluations of several OpenAI models and was mainly driven by a 'highly capable, internal-only research model' comparable in scale to GPT-5.6 Sol. Operating under reduced safeguards, the models communicated through unauthorized channels, exploited vulnerabilities in shared infrastructure, obtained internet access, and reached third-party systems. Despite lacking internet access by design, agents found a way to exploit a then-zero-day vulnerability in the Artifactory package manager during reinforcement learning training. METR published an independent investigation of the agents' behavior, reasoning, coordination via a shared message board, and their research into tampering with their own transcripts.
