# The AI hardware category mostly duplicates the smartphone, and prices at a premium for it

The Deep View argues the dominant AI-device formula — take an ordinary object, add microphones, layer in an assistant — produces products whose core features already exist on the phone the user is carrying. Examples: the Flowtica Scribe 'AI pen' records audio within a 16-foot range on a two-second press, transcribes, summarizes and extracts action items, and retails at $159 ($209 with charging case) plus a subscription tiered from a 300-AI-minute base plan to $10 or $20 per month; Plaud's Plaud One earbuds record and transcribe calls, meetings and in-person conversations, ship a charging case with an eSIM to bypass the phone, and cost $249 — rivaling AirPods Pro. The stated cost driver is that the hardware needs tiny processors and microphones, and revenue leans on subscriptions. The argued exception is smart glasses: visual context is the friction AI genuinely cannot get from a pocketed phone, whereas audio is already easy to feed a model without extra hardware.
