# OpenAI's first published Jalapeño results: 1.5–1.9x more AI work per watt and 1.7–3.6x lower latency than NVIDIA GB200/GB300 comparators

In August 2026 OpenAI released the first test results for Jalapeño, its first custom inference accelerator, produced in partnership with Broadcom — reporting promising latency and throughput when running both OpenAI's own and open-weights models.

THE MEASURED RESULTS (published 2026-08-25). Tested on SemiAnalysis's public InferenceX benchmark across GPT-OSS 120B, DeepSeek R1 670B, and Kimi K2.5 1T, Jalapeño delivered 1.5–1.9x more AI work per watt at peak throughput and 1.7–3.6x lower end-to-end latency than the commercially available comparison systems (NVIDIA GB200 for GPT-OSS, GB300 for the two larger models). For highly interactive workloads the advantage was 2.1–4.1x.

POWER AND NORMALIZATION. Jalapeño is rated at 700 W package TDP but measured sustained power stayed at or below 550 W on the tested workloads; the GB200/GB300 comparators are rated 1,200 W and 1,400 W respectively. OpenAI normalized results by published chip power rating and argues performance per unit of power is the more useful standard than performance per chip.

THE CAVEAT THAT MUST TRAVEL WITH THE NUMBERS. The Batch notes chipmakers must balance raw speed against total concurrent users and power draw, both of which affect inference cost and reliability — so a per-watt throughput advantage on a benchmark is not by itself a claim about serving economics at fleet scale. The results are OpenAI-published and OpenAI-normalized, not independently run.
