# Reallocating RL compute from easy to hard problems substantially improves LLM performance on the hardest tasks

A research writeup on 'never giving up' in RL for LLMs argues that standard reinforcement learning produces disproportionately poor performance on the hardest problems, and that simple scalar values may be insufficient for accurate evaluation of large language models. The proposed solution reduces compute spent on easy problems and reallocates it toward harder ones, reportedly yielding substantial improvements on difficult tasks. The general shape: uniform compute allocation across a difficulty distribution systematically underinvests in the tail that matters most.
