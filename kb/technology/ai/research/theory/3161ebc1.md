# Yann LeCun proposes Superhuman Adaptable Intelligence (SAI) as alternative to AGI goalpost

In a research paper published March 2026, Yann LeCun (Meta AI godfather) and a team of researchers challenged the concept of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) — arguing there is no consensus definition of AGI in industry or academia, and that humans themselves are not generalized. Proposed alternative goalpost: Superhuman Adaptable Intelligence (SAI) — emphasizes how quickly a model can adapt to new tasks and the range of tasks it can learn, rather than requiring a single model that can do everything. Key arguments: (1) Generality is not a requirement for intelligence to be extremely useful; (2) Humans are specialized but capable of quickly learning a wide range of things; (3) Two training mechanisms proposed: self-supervised learning (acquire generic knowledge) + world models (visual AI reflecting the world for planning). Direct opposition to: Sam Altman (AGI before 2030 via LLMs) and Dario Amodei (Claude showing signs of anxiety, questioning consciousness). LeCun's SAI framing redirects research from single-model omniscience to adaptability and learning speed.
