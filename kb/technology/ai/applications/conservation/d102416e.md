# WhaleSpotter uses thermal AI to prevent ship-whale collisions

WhaleSpotter is an AI system that detects whales in real time from their heat signatures using thermal cameras (mountable on land or vessels) and relays validated alerts to ship captains to avoid collisions, working through glare, darkness, and light fog. Whales' blows and exposed surfaces run at least ~2°C warmer than ocean water; thermal cameras detect them up to 4 nautical miles away. A neural network trained on hundreds of thousands of thermal images (with negative examples like birds, waves, boats) runs on local hardware to avoid transmission delays; a human-in-the-loop validates within ~30 seconds, yielding 99% accuracy. Newly deployed in San Francisco Bay (first install with both stationary and moving cameras), it logged 6,600 whales within ~1.5 weeks. The technology stems from over a decade of research at Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution (WHOI), commercialized in 2024; over 70 systems are now deployed. Ships strike and kill ~20,000 whales of all kinds per year (Ocean Wise); ~40% of whales that die in SF Bay are struck by vessels.
