# Google's AI-for-science programme: Gemini for Science's four components, framed by Google Research as amplifying rather than replacing scientists

THE STATED VISION. Yossi Matias, head of Google Research, described Google's approach as building AI systems to AMPLIFY, NOT REPLACE, scientists — searching papers, generating hypotheses, and accelerating experiments — in partnership with universities including Stanford and Imperial College.

THE PRODUCT. Google launched Gemini for Science at Google I/O 2026, in four components:
1. CO-SCIENTIST — a multi-agent system built on Gemini, with agents for idea generation, critique, ranking, improvement and review, coordinated by a lead 'manager' agent. Published in Nature. Stanford used it to find a drug reducing liver scarring in lab-grown tissue; other users include Calico Life Sciences (aging), the University of Edinburgh (liver disease) and the University of Cambridge (viral spillover, flu/COVID). Imperial College researchers had spent YEARS on a bacterial hypothesis that Co-Scientist reached in DAYS.
2. COMPUTATIONAL DISCOVERY — built on AlphaEvolve and ERA (Empirical Research Assistant); generates and scores 200,000+ candidate models in parallel, applied to solar forecasting and epidemiology.
3. LITERATURE INSIGHTS — NotebookLM-powered; structures findings across papers into searchable tables, reports, slides and audio.
4. SCIENCE SKILLS — 30+ life-science databases (UniProt, AlphaFold, AlphaGenome API) plugged into agentic tools such as Antigravity.

PARTNERS AND USERS. Enterprise partners include BASF, Klarna, Bayer Crop Science, and US National Labs (DOE Genesis Mission).

HOW IT FEELS IN USE, per Lizzie Dorfman (science product lead): 'I input some ideas, and then I went to sleep, and then I woke up and I had all these cool results.'

COMPETITION. OpenAI's GPT-Rosalind, a life-sciences frontier reasoning model, launched around April 2026.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: the 'amplify not replace' framing is Google's own positioning, not an observed outcome. The Imperial College years-to-days comparison is a single anecdote reported by Google, not a controlled measurement, and none of the component claims here is independently audited. The vision-statement source carries no recorded sources.
