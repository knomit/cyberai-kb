# Extropic's Z1 samples an Ising model in sub-threshold CMOS at under 1 watt

Extropic published a technical overview of Z1T on 2026-09-05, a family of transformer-like models co-designed for its Z1 probabilistic chip. Z1 is built from p-bits — bits deliberately allowed to fluctuate under thermal noise — fabricated on standard CMOS at room temperature, packing 269,568 p-bits with 16 tunable neighbor connections each, sampling a programmable Ising model at 50 MHz while drawing under 1 watt. Z1 chips pair with FPGAs in a disaggregated pipeline: the thermodynamic accelerator handles compatible operations, the FPGA handles the rest. Extropic estimates the Z1-assigned operations are ~3 orders of magnitude more energy-efficient than GPU equivalents, yielding ~2 orders of magnitude overall gain for Z1T inference (some coverage cites ~140x). These are company-reported co-design estimates, not independent benchmarks, and Z1 is a coprocessor alongside GPUs, not a GPU replacement.
