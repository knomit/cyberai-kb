# The 2026 'AI competition moved to cost-efficiency' claim is assembled from four non-comparable metrics, and the one that would settle it — cost per successful task — is published by no one

This corpus asserts in several places that AI competition shifted from raw capability to cost-efficiency during 2026. The claim is well-evidenced in DIRECTION. This record is about the STRUCTURE of that evidence, because the supporting facts measure four different quantities that are routinely compressed into one.

THE FOUR QUANTITIES ACTUALLY MEASURED.
1. SPEND SHARE — Ramp's August 2026 index: 43.5% of its US business clients paid for Anthropic vs 39.7% for OpenAI; only ~6% of Anthropic token spend went to Fable 5 vs 25% of OpenAI tokens to GPT-5.6 Sol. Anthropic's cheaper Opus 5 overtook Fable 5 in corporate model spending within a month of launch.
2. TOKEN SHARE — open-source models rose from 28% to 62% of token share at Vercel over roughly two months in mid-2026.
3. PRICE PER TOKEN — OpenAI cut GPT-5.6 Luna 80% and Terra 20%; a separate GPT-5.6 Sol API cut of more than 20% was explicitly temporary at three months; Google's Gemini 3.6 Flash held 50 Intelligence Index points while cost per task fell from $0.59 to $0.50.
4. TASK-SPECIFIC HIT RATE — NextLM's ~30B Savant 3.5 placed 24.6% of eventual buyers in its top decile vs 22.8% for GPT-5.6 Sol, at $0.003–$0.011 per 1,000 prospects scored vs $0.26–$5.11 for frontier APIs.

WHY THEY ARE NOT INTERCHANGEABLE. Spend share is a budgeting fact, token share a routing fact, price per token a list-price fact, and hit rate a single-task quality fact. A model can gain token share while losing delivered value, or cut list price while costing more per completed job.

THE DECISIVE METRIC IS MISSING. Two source facts name the same unresolved variable: COST PER SUCCESSFUL TASK. The concrete trap is stated in the sources: Opus 5 costs roughly half of Fable 5's rates, BUT the cheaper system may need more attempts, longer prompts, or more human review, so cost per successful task can converge — meaning a spend-share shift toward the cheaper model is consistent with no efficiency gain at all. No source in this cluster publishes cost per successful task. The measurement that would settle the claim is the one nobody produces, so the claim rests entirely on proxies.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN — carried from the sources and load-bearing:
- This does NOT claim the cost shift is false or that the figures are wrong. Direction is supported by multiple independent vendors and platforms; it is precision and causal reading that are not established.
- It does NOT show frontier models being displaced for work requiring sustained autonomy or breadth. EVERY source that addresses that question says the opposite: Fable remains positioned for long autonomous projects that must stay coherent across many connected steps, DeepSeek V4-Flash-Vision-Exp 'nearly matches' rather than beats Opus 4.8, and NextLM's own CEO acknowledged general models retain advantages in breadth and ease of use.
- Scope limits on individual figures must survive any citation: Ramp's customers skew toward startups and small businesses; the Vercel token-share figure is one platform's reported number, not an industry measurement, and is held at low confidence; the NextLM result is ONE niche sales-prospecting task and the research is published by the vendor; benchmark parity claims are scoped to Artificial Analysis' Intelligence Index at stated reasoning levels, where absolute capability leadership still sat with larger models.
- Falling API prices are moving OPPOSITE to hardware costs: Nvidia planned to pass rising HBM costs through, raising AI server prices by more than 15% for systems shipping the following year. Cheaper tokens do not imply cheaper compute.
- The temporary/permanent distinction matters and is frequently dropped: the GPT-5.6 Sol cut was stated as lasting three months, while Anthropic made Claude Sonnet 5 pricing permanent at $2/$10 per M tokens.
