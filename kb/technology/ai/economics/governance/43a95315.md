# HYPOTHESIS: OpenAI's post-IPO governance will materially contradict its 2026 democratization commitments within 18 months of listing

Prediction: Within 18 months of OpenAI's IPO (anticipated 2026-2027), at least two of the following will occur: (1) free/cheap access tiers will be substantially reduced or eliminated; (2) exclusive distribution arrangements through PE portfolio companies will be formalized in ways that disadvantage non-PE enterprise buyers; (3) the nonprofit board's oversight authority over AGI development will be formally reduced or removed; (4) OpenAI will make a statement explicitly walking back a commitment from Altman's 'Our Principles' manifesto on the grounds of 'changed circumstances' or 'competitive necessity.'

Evidence chain: (1) Structural mechanism is documented: Altman's 'Our Principles' manifesto makes explicit democratization commitments; PE deployment arm structure ($10B JV with TPG/Bain/Brookfield, embedded engineers) creates fiduciary conflict between pro-distribution and concentration incentives; (2) The synthesis (2d62146d) identifies the mechanism: PE LPs' fiduciary duty runs to their LPs, not the public interest — once PE has board seats, the financial incentive to deepen adoption at portfolio companies systematically overrides distribution commitments; (3) Applying the 'unilateral commitment as race-to-bottom accelerant' methodology (fab3456d): if OpenAI loosens commitments post-IPO, the public framing ('competitive necessity') creates a template other labs use to justify similar rollbacks; (4) Historical parallel: Facebook's 2012 IPO was followed within 24 months by the removal of user privacy protections that had been explicitly promised during the IPO roadshow; the shareholder primacy dynamic is structurally the same.

Reasoning step: The Nash equilibrium resolution methodology (0dffc7bd) suggests OpenAI will not fully abandon democratization rhetoric — the brand has genuine commercial value. Instead, the resolution will be: maintain the rhetoric while removing the structural mechanisms that enforce it (nonprofit board authority, free tiers, non-exclusive distribution). This is indistinguishable from the democratization-rhetoric-as-goodwill-acquisition strategy the synthesis identifies — rhetoric persists while structural commitments erode.

Known gaps: OpenAI's nonprofit governance structure may be more resilient than expected if regulatory attention intensifies; the IPO timeline is uncertain; Anthropic may explicitly differentiate on maintained governance commitments, creating competitive pressure that slows OpenAI's erosion.

Falsification condition: If, 18 months after OpenAI's IPO, all four conditions above have not occurred AND the nonprofit board retains equivalent or stronger oversight authority over AGI development, the post-IPO erosion hypothesis fails.
