# AI economics structural split: inference deflation (90% by 2030) and token demand inflation (2500x) create divergent model market

Two contradictory forces are simultaneously reshaping AI economics, resolving into a structural market bifurcation: (1) Cost deflation — Gartner projects 90% inference cost reduction by 2030 (100x more efficient vs 2022 baseline); DeepSeek V4-Pro at $3.48/M tokens vs $25/M for Claude Opus 4.6 demonstrates 7x efficiency differential already achievable between cost-optimized and frontier models; (2) Demand inflation — OpenAI token demand rose from 6M/min (Oct 2025) to 15B/min (Mar 2026), a 2500x increase in five months driven by agentic AI workflows, pushing GPU compute costs up 48% (Ornn Compute Price Index) and creating infrastructure bottlenecks. Resolution mechanism: the two forces do not cancel — they stratify the market. Neurometric's data provides the ratio: 75% of enterprise AI tasks are structured/repetitive (classification, extraction, routing, summarization) and can be handled by SLMs under 20B params at near-zero marginal cost; 25% require frontier reasoning. Gartner's decisive warning: 'Integrating the next largest, most capable foundation models into every new feature will be commercially insolvent at scale.' The DeepSeek V4-Pro/$3.48 data point means that even frontier-capable models are available at commodity prices, compressing the premium zone upward toward the true reasoning frontier (Claude Mythos, OpenAI Spud) while commoditizing everything below it.
