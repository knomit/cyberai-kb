# Steering on token count failed in both directions in 2026: pushed up as a productivity proxy it preceded a budget overrun, pushed down as a cost proxy it sometimes raised spend ~50%, and a >20% per-token price cut arrived with roughly double the tokens per session

Three 2026 records show organisations and vendors using TOKEN COUNT as the control variable for something else — productivity, cost, or price — and the intermediate quantity failing to track the quantity of interest. The three legs point in different directions, which is the point: the failure is not that tokens are too high or too low, but that they are the wrong unit to steer on.

## LEG 1 — TOKENS UP, AS A PRODUCTIVITY PROXY
Uber incentivized employee AI use with INTERNAL LEADERBOARDS RANKING WORKERS BY AI TOKENS CONSUMED, and reportedly BURNED THROUGH ITS ANNUAL AI BUDGET IN THE FIRST FOUR MONTHS OF 2026. The source presents this as an example of the 'tokenmaxxing' trend and the subsequent cost backlash. This is the one leg that is a straightforward Goodhart case: a measure adopted as a target, then optimised.

## LEG 2 — TOKENS DOWN, AS A COST PROXY
PointFive ran THE SAME PROMPT across 2,908 CLAUDE CODE SESSIONS with and without token-reduction tooling, and found that IN SOME CASES TOKEN COMPRESSION INCREASED COSTS BY NEARLY 50% — undermining the common vendor claim that cutting tokens cuts spend. Note this is a controlled comparison (same prompt, tooling as the variable), which makes it the strongest of the three legs methodologically. THE SOURCE GIVES NO MECHANISM for why compression raised cost, and none should be inferred here.

## LEG 3 — PER-TOKEN PRICE DOWN, PER-SESSION SPEND NOT NECESSARILY DOWN
From outside this cluster, kb/business/companies/openai/pricing/gpt-5-6-sol-price-cut.md records that OpenAI cut GPT-5.6 Sol developer API prices by MORE THAN 20% — and states directly that a headline per-token cut does not predict what a user pays, because GPT-5.6 Sol AT ITS HIGHEST REASONING SETTING CONSUMES ROUGHLY TWICE THE TOKENS PER SESSION of GPT-5.5 in Codex workflows and ADDS A CACHE-WRITE CHARGE GPT-5.5 did not have. That cut was also STATED AS LASTING THREE MONTHS — a promotion, not a permanent list-price change.

## WHAT THE THREE JOINTLY SUPPORT
Only this: token count is an intermediate quantity that, on the evidence recorded here, tracked neither delivered value (leg 1) nor total spend (legs 2 and 3) reliably enough to steer on. Each leg fails differently — one by being gamed, two by simply not tracking — so this is not one mechanism but one shared consequence for anyone using tokens as a dial.

WHAT WOULD BE THE RIGHT UNIT IS RECORDED ELSEWHERE AND IS NOT AVAILABLE. kb/technology/ai/economics/553272ed.md establishes that COST PER SUCCESSFUL TASK is the metric that would settle these questions and that NO SOURCE IN THAT CLUSTER PUBLISHES IT — and gives the concrete trap: a cheaper model may need more attempts, longer prompts, or more human review, so cost per successful task can converge even as per-token price halves. The organisational context is in kb/technology/ai/enterprise/inference-economics/26fd1883.md ('token panic'; Databricks CEO Ali Ghodsi calling it 'completely unsustainable'; responses being model routing and hybrid/local compute) and kb/technology/ai/enterprise/cost-efficiency/e5fea5f6.md (practitioners advising that every prompt and tool call adds cost, and that implementations should start from a problem statement including whether a task needs AI at all).

## WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN — load-bearing

- THE EVIDENCE BASE IS WEAK AND THE CONFIDENCE REFLECTS IT. Both primary legs carry confidence 0.6 in this corpus; the Uber record has ZERO recorded sources and its budget claim is 'reportedly', relayed by The Deep View citing Fortune. Three records is not a measured rate across organisations.
- IT DOES NOT ESTABLISH THAT UBER'S LEADERBOARD CAUSED THE BUDGET OVERRUN. The source records both facts and presents the pairing as illustrative of a trend; it does not measure the contribution of the incentive scheme, and neither does this record.
- IT DOES NOT CLAIM TOKEN-REDUCTION TOOLING IS USELESS OR HARMFUL IN GENERAL. PointFive's finding is that compression raised costs IN SOME CASES within one product (Claude Code) and one test design; the record does not report the distribution, how often it helped, or by how much.
- IT DOES NOT CLAIM PRICE CUTS ARE ILLUSORY. A >20% per-token reduction is real. The claim is only that per-token price and per-session spend are different quantities and moved independently in the recorded case — and the temporary/permanent distinction on that cut must survive citation.
- IT DOES NOT SUPPLY A REPLACEMENT METRIC. Cost per successful task is named as the missing measurement, not offered as an available one.
- SEPARATE AND NOT MERGED HERE: the Gartner projections in this cluster (inference cost per agentic workflow rising more than fivefold through 2028; 90% inference cost reduction by 2030 with savings not automatically reaching enterprises) describe an 'inference paradox' of efficiency absorbed by demand. That is a claim about market-level cost trajectories, already synthesised at kb/technology/ai/economics/model-market/a2d4a30c.md, and is a different claim from this one about metric selection. Do not fold them together.
