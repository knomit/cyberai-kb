# Hypothesis: Open-Source 'Own Your AI' Positioning Will Win ≥3 Fortune 1000 Defections from PE Deployment Arms by Q2 2027

Prediction: By end of Q2 2027, at least 3 publicly announced Fortune 1000-scale enterprise AI deployments will explicitly cite open-source foundation models (Mistral, Meta LLaMA, or equivalents) over PE-backed deployment arms, citing vendor lock-in and data sovereignty concerns.

Evidence base:
- Mistral Forge (Mar 2026) launched explicitly with anti-lock-in positioning: 'AI is changing a lot of things, but customizing models and making sure that you are able to own that AI is what's going to get you the differentiation' — directly targeting the PE channel's structural weakness
- PE methodology (9949ae2d): embedded engineer lock-in is less durable than technical integration lock-in; can be unwound faster than software integrations
- Open-weights models (GLM-5.1, Qwen3.5) are already leading on some benchmarks — model quality moat is compressing faster than embedded engineers can be deployed at scale
- PE fiduciary duty (17.5% return expectations cited by OpenAI) creates client-vs-LP tension that sophisticated procurement teams will exploit as a negotiating lever
- India sovereign hosting push (5555c22f) signals a broader government/regulated-sector preference for models that can run on-premises or regionally — structurally incompatible with PE embedded-engineer models

Falsification: Hypothesis FAILS if fewer than 3 such public announcements occur by June 30, 2027 — or if PE deployment arms demonstrate lock-in durability through contract renewals exceeding 90% retention after the first embedded-engineer year.

Load-bearing gap: Whether enterprise procurement teams have the sophistication and vendor leverage to resist embedded-engineer lock-in within the first 12 months of deployment — the window before switching costs compound quarterly (per synthesis).
