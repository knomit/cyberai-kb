# AI labor impact: executive reassurance contrasts with entry-level erosion in data

As of mid-2026 a tension is visible between corporate-leadership messaging and empirical labor data on AI's job impact. Executives such as IBM CHRO Nickle LaMoreaux argue the threat is overstated - that AI eliminates few roles net while productivity gains create new ones, and that soft skills (communication, leadership, judgment, conflict management) stay human. Meanwhile Stanford Digital Economy Lab research ('Canaries in the Coal Mine', Nov 2025) finds entry-level roles most exposed to AI (customer service, data entry, coding) already in 'substantial decline.' The reconcilable reading: aggregate/long-run optimism coexists with concentrated near-term harm to early-career, routine-exposed workers, raising the relative value of human soft skills.
