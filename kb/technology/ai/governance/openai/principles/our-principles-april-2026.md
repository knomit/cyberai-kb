# OpenAI's April 2026 principles rewrite made Democratization the first principle with an explicit anti-power-concentration commitment

On April 26, 2026, Sam Altman published 'Our principles' (openai.com/index/our-principles/) — OpenAI's first major update to its guiding principles since the 2018 charter. Five principles: Democratization, Empowerment, Universal prosperity, Resilience, Adaptability.

Principle 1 verbatim: 'Democratization. We will resist the potential of this technology to consolidate power in the hands of the few.' Elaborated: 'in addition to giving everyone access to AI, we need to ensure that key decisions about AI are made via democratic processes and with egalitarian principles, and not just made by AI labs.' The framing document also states: 'Power in the future can either be held by a small handful of companies using and controlling superintelligence, or it can be held in a decentralized way by people. We believe the latter is much better, and our goal is to put truly general AI in the hands of as many people as possible.'

The document followed an earlier policy document from OpenAI researchers with 'ideas to keep people first'. Greg Brockman (OpenAI president) and Andrej Karpathy (former Tesla AI lead, OpenAI cofounder) also voiced democratization positions around the same time.

DIRECTION OF CHANGE — note carefully: this STRENGTHENED rather than weakened democratization language. It is retained in first person ('We will resist'), elevated to principle #1, and given an explicit anti-concentration framing. Predictions anticipating a walk-back or qualification of democratization language are contradicted by this text.

NUANCES THAT CUT THE OTHER WAY: (a) third-party analyses note the document overall uses fewer direct commitments than the 2018 charter and shifts some claims to ecosystem recommendations — visible in Principle 3, 'our governments may need to consider new economic models' — though NOT in the democratization principle itself; (b) Principle 5 (Adaptability) builds in a revision mechanism: 'we can imagine periods in the future where we have to trade off some empowerment for more resilience,' with a promise to 'be transparent about when, how, and why our operating principles change'; (c) it places less emphasis on AGI than the 2018 charter; (d) separately from this document, reporting indicates OpenAI's mission statement has changed six times in nine years, with references to 'safely' removed in the FY2024 Form 990 filed November 2025 — that concerns safety language and the mission statement, not the democratization principle; (e) Deep View editor Jason Hiner noted that once OpenAI goes public, all financial incentives will favor centralization, making the pledge difficult to maintain.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: strengthened language is a statement of intent, not evidence of conduct. This fact records what the document says, not whether OpenAI acts accordingly.
