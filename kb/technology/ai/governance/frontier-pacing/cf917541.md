# Dario Amodei's three-part 'We Must Pace the Frontier' framework

On Saturday 2026-09-12, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published an essay titled 'We Must Pace the Frontier', arguing the AI industry must slow the rate at which it improves model capabilities so that risk prevention has time to keep up. He cites recursive self-improvement, rogue-agent incidents such as the OpenAI–Hugging Face breach, and a general lack of understanding of these systems. The framework is intended to make labs 'race to the top' on safety rather than to the bottom on risk.

The three steps:
1. Embedded Evaluators — each frontier AI company gives a team of embedded third-party evaluators (such as METR) ongoing, employee-like internal access to verify adherence to safety practices and commitments, report incidents, and assess the alignment of not just completed models but training pipelines and processes.
2. Democratic Coordination — frontier AI companies within democratic countries coordinate to establish common safety standards and limits on the rate of unchecked AI progress; Amodei says some effective forms are legally challenging and need US government mediation or narrow antitrust waivers.
3. Global Coordination — the US and other democratic governments attempt to coordinate with authoritarian governments, while taking seriously the challenges of verifying compliance.

Scope of the commitment: Anthropic unilaterally committed to step 1 only, and calls on other labs and governments to match it; steps 2 and 3 are calls for industry-wide and global coordination, not Anthropic commitments. Amodei notes the steps need not be taken strictly in order.

Amodei is explicit that this is NOT a pause: 'pacing does not mean halting model training or technical progress, but ensuring companies take adequate time to align and safeguard their models, and for third party evaluators to confirm this,' and he expects 'progress will still seem fast.'

Note the reversal: Anthropic previously argued a unilateral pause by one lab achieves little because 'it would change who the front-runner is, but it would not create the wider deliberative process that is currently missing.'
