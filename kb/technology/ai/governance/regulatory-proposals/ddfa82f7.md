# Hassabis proposed a FINRA-style industry-funded standards body for frontier AI review (July 2026)

In an X post on Tuesday July 14, 2026 titled 'A Framework for Frontier AI and the Dawning of a New Age', Google DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis called for a new 'standards body' to oversee frontier model releases, explicitly modeled on FINRA (the Financial Industry Regulatory Authority) — i.e. a self-regulatory organization, backed by the U.S. government but funded by the AI industry and operated independently.

Proposed mechanics: frontier labs would initially share models with the body voluntarily for review up to 30 days before release; once the assessment protocol is 'shown to be effective and robust', formalisation could follow, under which frontier models would be REQUIRED to pass review to be deployed in the US market. Labs would also work with the body on critical post-release vulnerabilities. Hassabis envisions it staffed by open-source representatives and technical experts from industry, with lab funding needed to retain them, and possibly outsourcing evaluations to specialized AI safety groups.

Stated rationale: the proposal builds on — and is a reaction to — the ad hoc U.S. government reviews of Anthropic's Mythos and OpenAI's Sol, which drew significant criticism for lack of technical expertise and opaque decision-making about when a model could be released.

COUNTERVAILING SIGNAL IN THE SAME SOURCE: White House AI advisor and a16z general partner Sriram Krishnan recently discounted the possibility of an AI regulator within the executive branch, stating 'there will not be an FDA for AI.' AI regulation remains controversial for both the tech industry and the Trump administration. The SRO structure may be an attempt to route around that objection.

SCOPE: this is a proposal by one lab CEO posted to X, not an announced policy, a multi-lab coalition, or a government position. No other frontier lab is reported as endorsing it.
