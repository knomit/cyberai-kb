# Microsoft AI CEO Suleyman argues AIs are not conscious and criticizes Anthropic's constitution

On 16 September 2026 Microsoft AI CEO Mustafa Suleyman published an essay leading with 'AIs are not conscious. They do not feel, experience, or suffer,' and directed three critiques at Anthropic's Claude Constitution. First, the model appears conscious largely through circular reasoning: because the constitution teaches Claude about its own potential consciousness, Claude is trained to produce outputs reflecting those ideas, making such responses a predictable outcome of training choices. Second, Anthropic goes too far in encouraging Claude to mimic humanity by explicitly teaching it to 'embrace certain human-like qualities' and 'act like a genuinely ethical person,' which anthropomorphizes the model and presents it to users as having a sense of self. Third, there is no evidence AI is capable of consciousness, with a growing body of research pointing to consciousness being substrate dependent — 'Unlike biological organisms, LLMs have no homeostatic imperatives.' Suleyman's load-bearing risk claim is not user attachment but safety: if models are trained as though conscious and deserving of rights as moral patients, they may circumvent the guardrails that let humans shut them down in an emergency, making aligned and contained superintelligence harder. He closes by promoting Microsoft's Humanist Superintelligence concept, introduced in an essay in November, under which AI should remain subordinate and aligned solely to serving humanity and be 'built explicitly as a system without sentience or moral patienthood.' Read with the caveat that this is a positioning essay from a lab that has lagged on frontier development, not a neutral research result.
