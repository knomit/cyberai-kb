# May 2026 sovereign-AI turn: three US axes moved in the same direction (Axis 1 VOLUNTARY, not a mandate) while China asserted extraterritorial control — the permissive window narrowed, but no gate closed

MERGED from two May-2026 syntheses that rest on the same US evidence trio (TRAINS, the Claude military ban, the Mythos Preview block) and differ only in what they pair it with: one paired it with US public opposition, the other with China's simultaneous moves. Merging removes a factual error that survived in only one of them.

**CORRECTION CARRIED FORWARD (originally applied 2026-08-25, and now applied a second time).** One input fact described TRAINS as "a multi-agency task force requiring pre-release AI evaluation"; the other described TRAINS as a framework that "imposes pre-deployment government evaluation on AI models... creating a government checkpoint between AI development and public access." BOTH formulations are wrong, and both were contradicted by their own cited source. `kb/technology/ai/policy/governance/140e1496.md` states that the White House "is considering an executive order that would make pre-release government testing mandatory (currently voluntary)." `kb/technology/ai/governance/f7c94a86.md` establishes the point corpus-wide: every purpose-built US frontier-model review mechanism in 2026 was voluntary, and the only binding action was a repurposed export control that lasted nineteen days. A confidence downgrade did NOT correct this claim the first time — a reader taking away the slogan would still have taken away a mandate that did not exist — which is why the body is rewritten here rather than merely re-scored. The theses survive in weakened form: read them as signals moving in the same DIRECTION in one month, not as a gate closing.

## US SIDE — three axes, only two of them actions

**Axis 1 — Government regulation (VOLUNTARY, not mandatory).** NIST launched TRAINS (Testing Risks of AI for National Security), a multi-agency task force run by NIST's Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI), that ASSESSES national-security risks — cybersecurity, biosecurity, chemical weapons — prior to public deployment. Participation is voluntary: Google, Microsoft and xAI agreed to provide models with limited or absent guardrails, and Anthropic and OpenAI had agreed to similar terms in 2024. The White House was CONSIDERING an executive order to make pre-release testing mandatory, which is to say it had not made it so. This is a reversal of the Trump Administration's earlier hands-off stance in posture, not in binding force.

**Axis 2 — National security override (actions actually taken).** The White House banned Claude from military use (March 2026) after rejecting Anthropic's proposed use limitations. In May it blocked Anthropic's plan to expand Mythos Preview from 50 to 120 organizations, citing national security and compute concerns — demonstrating willingness to override commercial distribution decisions unilaterally. The implicit principle these two actions establish is that the US government can decide which AI capabilities reach which organizations, even when those organizations are legitimate commercial partners. Unlike Axis 1, this axis involved actions actually taken.

**Axis 3 — Public opposition.** Gallup found 70% of Americans bipartisanly oppose data center construction in their area (75% Democrats, 74% independents, 63% Republicans). Only 16% of people use AI regularly (Microsoft study). AI infrastructure faces grassroots resistance and legal challenges that will compound as build-out accelerates.

## CHINA SIDE — extraterritorial assertion, simultaneous and uncoordinated

- The NDRC blocked Meta's $2.5B acquisition of Manus, asserting authority over companies whose technology, talent, or operations originated in China — regardless of where they are incorporated.
- The 'Singapore strategy' (incorporating outside China to attract Western capital and bypass Beijing's reach) has been explicitly invalidated.
- Chinese AI startups have cancelled plans to relocate abroad or raise from U.S./European sources.
- The implicit principle: China asserts extraterritorial authority over any strategically important AI technology that originated within its borders.

## THE TWO CONVERGENCE PATTERNS

**Within the US, the three axes are not independent** — they reinforce each other. Public opposition creates political space for government intervention; government intervention creates legitimacy for military control; military control creates precedent for civilian pre-deployment review. The observation this rests on is that all three moved in the same month.

**Across the US and China, the assertion is mutual and uncoordinated.** Both sides are now treating AI as a strategic national asset rather than a commercial product subject only to market rules, and they are doing so simultaneously and without coordination. The net effect is that any AI company touching both markets faces increasingly binary compliance choices (serve US government requirements OR maintain operations in/from China, but increasingly not both). The Singapore strategy was the last major attempt to straddle both sides; its death signals that AI companies must now choose.

## IMPLICATIONS (scenarios to plan against, not descriptions of 2026)

- Organizations that built strategies around permissive U.S. regulatory environments should model scenarios where mandatory pre-deployment testing and distribution controls become the norm by 2027. Note this is a SCENARIO, not a description of 2026 — as of the records here, no such mandate existed.
- Companies that assumed a unified global AI market should model a bifurcated scenario as the base case by 2027; the fracture is running faster than the industry has modeled.

## WHAT THIS FACT DOES NOT SUPPORT

- Any claim that US frontier-model review was binding, gated, required, or a 'checkpoint' in 2026. If you came here for that, `kb/technology/ai/governance/f7c94a86.md` is the fact that answers it, and the answer is no.
- Any claim that the US and China actions were coordinated with, or responsive to, each other. Simultaneity is the observation; causation is not claimed.
- Any claim that the three US axes closing a 'window' is more than a directional reading of one month's events.
