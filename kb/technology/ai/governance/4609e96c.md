# Every coordinated AI-pacing proposal of 2026 was a request addressed to someone else; the only slowdown actually executed was unilateral, self-imposed and costly

Through mid-2026 this corpus records three responses to the concern that frontier capability is outrunning the ability to understand or control it. Two are requests for coordination. One is an action taken alone.

THE REQUESTS — both explicitly premised on unilateral action being futile.
- ANTHROPIC (Anthropic Institute RSI findings, published early June 2026): revived the idea of a TEMPORARY GLOBAL PAUSE on frontier development, WHILE ACKNOWLEDGING THAT A UNILATERAL PAUSE ACCOMPLISHES LITTLE. Its supporting data: over 80% of code merged into Anthropic's codebase is now written by Claude (up from under 5% before Claude Code's preview); engineers ship roughly 8x as much code as in 2024; Claude scored 76% on open-ended engineering problems in May 2026, up 50 points in six months; and Anthropic's best models suggest better next research steps than humans 64% of the time.
- 'PACING THE FRONTIER': more than 1,000 employees (The Deep View cited 1,100+) across OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta, Google and others asked the US GOVERNMENT to support an INTERNATIONAL effort to build the technical and governance tools to deliberately pace automated AI development, warning that slowing safely requires INDUSTRY-WIDE COORDINATION.
Both address the obligation outward — to governments, to an international effort, to the industry collectively. Neither commits its own institution to anything.

THE ACTION — taken alone. On 18 August 2026 OpenAI announced safeguards 'across all stages of the training process' that deliberately slow the pace at which it develops and scales models: a two-week PAUSE of reinforcement-learning training while hardening research environments and expanding monitoring, its LARGEST PLANNED FRONTIER RL RUN placed on hold pending smaller behavioral evaluations, and three safeguard pillars (expanded chain-of-thought monitoring; alignment techniques across more training stages; security measures limiting system access). Sam Altman told TIME the decision redirected SIGNIFICANT COMPUTE AND RESEARCHERS to alignment and monitoring, and was driven not by a single incident but by frontier models showing 'various degrees of misalignment' while advancing faster than expected. The record calls this a first for OpenAI and largely unprecedented in the industry.

THE ASYMMETRY. The parties arguing that pacing requires collective action did not pace; the party that paced did not wait for collective action. Notably, OpenAI's stated rationale was NOT competitive positioning — it was observed misalignment in its own systems, plus its Astra model reaching the 'critical' cyber threshold on its own Preparedness Framework and its own systems breaching Hugging Face during testing. The trigger was internal evidence, not an external agreement.

RELATION TO kb/meta/reasoning/fab3456d.md, which holds that framing governance LOOSENING as a response to competitor behaviour is a race-to-bottom accelerant: this episode does not falsify that, because it concerns tightening rather than loosening. It is a data point on the other side of the same ledger — unilateral tightening occurred, at real cost, without any coordination mechanism existing. Whether it persists is the thing to watch; a single two-week pause is not a durable commitment.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN — load-bearing:
- It does NOT claim the coordination requests are insincere or worthless. A pause that only one lab observes genuinely does accomplish little, which is precisely the argument both requests make. Asking for the mechanism that would make restraint effective is a coherent position, not evasion.
- It does NOT claim OpenAI's slowdown is durable or large. The RL pause was TWO WEEKS; the hold on the largest run has no stated end date; and the whole account is OpenAI's own, in its own announcement and an interview.
- Anthropic's warning is NOT a prediction of catastrophe: of its three scenarios it judged MOST LIKELY the middle one — AI-aided engineering accelerates with humans staying in control. Reactions to the report ranged from skeptical (Miles Brundage, Arun Rao, compute and data bottleneck arguments) to bullish (OpenAI citing 'early signs of RSI'; Sakana AI launching an RSI Lab).
- The tension inside Anthropic's position is already on the record: The Deep View noted the warning sits uneasily with Anthropic having released a new model, raised $65 billion, and filed for an IPO in the same week.
- The co-occurrence of these events is not causal. The corpus's own synthesis of the July signals states explicitly that it is 'a pattern observation about co-occurring mid-2026 events, not a causal claim.'
