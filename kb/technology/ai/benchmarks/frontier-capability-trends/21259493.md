# Mid-2026 AI benchmarks are being rebuilt for long-horizon work, and Fable 5 leads a fast-moving but still-unreliable frontier

Synthesis across three independent mid-2026 benchmark results (KernelBench-Mega, Remote Labor Index, OSWorld 2.0), all surfaced via Import AI 464 (2026-07-06).

Pattern 1 — Benchmarks are being deliberately reset to longer horizons as predecessors saturate. OSWorld 1.0 went from ~30% (July 2025) to ~75% (MiniMax M3, June 2026), prompting OSWorld 2.0, whose median task takes a human ~1.6 hours versus 2 minutes in 1.0 (~48x longer). KernelBench-Mega and RLI likewise score end-to-end, economically meaningful deliverables rather than short snippets. The measurement frontier is migrating from 'can the model emit a correct artifact' to 'can the model sustain a multi-hour, multi-tool workflow.'

Pattern 2 — A consistent capability ordering holds across unrelated task families. Fable/Fable 5 leads on both kernel optimization (18.71x vs Opus 4.8's 14.4x) and freelance-project automation (16.1% vs Opus 4.8's 8.3% and GPT-5.5's 6.3%), with Opus 4.8 second and GPT-5.5 trailing. That the same ordering appears in CUDA kernel authoring and in 3D/CAD/graphic-design freelance work suggests a shared underlying competence rather than benchmark-specific tuning.

Pattern 3 — Rapid growth coexists with low absolute reliability. RLI more than quadrupled in under eight months (2.5% Oct 2025 -> 16.1% Jul 2026), yet the best OSWorld 2.0 result is only 20.6% binary accuracy, and OSWorld's authors note performance degrades sharply with task length, specifically where agents must recover hidden state, track many items, resolve conflicting information, or adapt to changing requirements. The binding constraint on long-horizon agents appears to be state management and error recovery over time, not per-step task skill.

Pattern 4 — Qualitative wins, not just score deltas. Fable's megakernel was distinguished by exactly one cooperative kernel launch per decoded token where all other high scorers used 4-14 — a structurally different solution, not a marginally better one. JD's Oxygen AIIC independently corroborates the state-management theme from the production side: it externalizes its ontology into a separate knowledge base so it updates without retraining, and reduces the model's job to a discrimination step explicitly to cut hallucination. Both the benchmark frontier and industrial deployment converge on constraining what the model must hold and decide at once.

Caveat: single-source (one newsletter edition summarizing primary sources); scores are self-reported by benchmark authors and vendors, and cross-benchmark orderings measured at one point in time are fragile.
