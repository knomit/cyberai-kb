# OSWorld 2.0 benchmarks computer-use agents on multi-hour long-horizon tasks

OSWORLD 2.0 is a computer-use agent benchmark released by researchers from the University of Hong Kong, UC San Diego, Columbia, UC Santa Barbara, Mila, Snorkel AI, University of Wisconsin, Alibaba Qwen, Ohio State, Simular, and NeoCognition. It contains 108 long-horizon tasks including 31 self-hosted websites. The median task takes a person roughly 1.6 hours — about 48x longer than the 2-minute median in OSWORLD 1.0 — and 69.6% of tasks are estimated to take a skilled human more than one hour. Software coverage expands well beyond 1.0's LibreOffice/GIMP/VLC/Thunderbird/VS Code/Chrome to include Slack, LinkedIn, Shortcut, REAPER, MuseScore, WPS, GitLab, Overleaf, LabPlot, Zotero, and AWS, plus mock professional portals (insurance claims, visa applications, conference management). Best reported result: Claude Opus 4.8 with maximum thinking and batched tool calls reaches only 20.6% binary accuracy and 54.8% partial-score accuracy. Performance drops sharply as tasks lengthen; agents struggle to recover hidden state, track many items, resolve conflicting information, and adapt to changing requirements.
