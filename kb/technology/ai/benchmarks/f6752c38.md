# Frontier-model evaluation is squeezed by both saturation and access restrictions

As of mid-2026, credibly measuring frontier-model capability faces two converging pressures evident in The Batch issue 358. First, saturation and training-data contamination: the SWE-bench family became too easy, prompting contamination-resistant successors (DeepSWE drawing from private repos, ProgramBench, ITBench-AA) that deliberately use human-written, unpublished problems. Second, access and safeguard restrictions: Anthropic's Claude Fable 5 screens prompts via classifiers (refusing or silently rerouting flagged ones to a weaker model) and imposes a 30-day data-retention requirement, which blocked or distorted evaluations by Artificial Analysis, Vals AI, and ARC Prize. The combined implication: a published benchmark score increasingly reflects test design and provider policy as much as raw model ability, so evaluators must report what capability a developer can actually obtain in practice, not just peak scores.
