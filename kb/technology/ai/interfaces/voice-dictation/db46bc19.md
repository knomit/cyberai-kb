# AI dictation solves accuracy but strips tone, and punctuation carries social meaning

A Deep View hands-on review published 2026-09-07 by Sabrina Ortiz found that Google's Rambler voice dictation — launched weeks earlier with the Pixel 11 and integrated into Gboard — matches Wispr Flow on the old hard problem (accurately transcribing speech, removing filler words, following a train of thought) but fails on tone: despite claims that it adapts to the user over time, it did not pick up the reviewer's enthusiastic register and produced flat, terse messages that recipients who knew her read as something being wrong. This is a single reviewer's qualitative experience, not a benchmark. The underlying mechanism is documented in linguistics research: a 2016 Binghamton University study found text messages ending in a period were rated less sincere than those without; a 2025 follow-up found perceived meanness rose sharply when periods were inserted after every word, indicating readers treat punctuation as an intentional, meaningful act; and linguist Gretchen McCulloch has described the single exclamation mark as functioning as a sincerity marker rather than an intensity marker. The general lesson: a transcription system that is lexically correct can still be pragmatically wrong, because punctuation choices the system makes on the user's behalf are read as the user's social signals.
