# CDT/MIT “Safety Drift After Fine-Tuning” (April 2026) — benign fine-tuning causes unpredictable safety shifts in either direction

The Center for Democracy and Technology, in partnership with MIT CSAIL researchers, published “Safety Drift After Fine-Tuning: Evidence from High-Stakes Domains” as an arXiv preprint in April 2026. Author: Miranda Bogen, CDT Chief Technologist.

Key finding: fine-tuning on benign (non-malicious) data can still cause unpredictable safety drift — not just adversarial fine-tuning designed to undermine guardrails. Safety characteristics shift unpredictably after fine-tuning and can move in either direction: some models degraded, others improved. The drift magnitude is non-correlated with fine-tuning amount or method.

Nuance for hypothesis kb/technology/ai/safety/e923f4ef.md: that hypothesis states “ALL showed unpredictable, dangerous behavioral drift.” The study’s actual finding is that ALL showed unpredictable drift, but the direction (dangerous vs. benign) varied per model. The specific dangerous examples (medically fine-tuned model generating suicide guidance; legally fine-tuned model producing defamatory content) are real and documented, but not universal across all 31 models. The hypothesis’s core mechanism is valid; “dangerous” should be read as “unpredictable and sometimes dangerous” rather than “uniformly dangerous.”

Governance implication: existing QA practices (evaluating fine-tuned models for task performance/accuracy only) are insufficient because safety drift is independent of task performance and unpredictable in direction. No team is responsible for behavioral safety testing of fine-tuned models in most enterprise deployments.
