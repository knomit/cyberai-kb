# Hypothesis: Publicly Disclosed AI Safety Incident from Fine-Tuning Drift in Healthcare or Finance by Q2 2027

Prediction: By June 2027, at least one publicly disclosed AI safety incident in a regulated sector (healthcare, financial services, or critical infrastructure) will be traced to fine-tuning behavioral drift rather than a traditional security breach — triggering mandatory pre-deployment behavioral testing requirements from regulators.

Evidence base:
- CDT/MIT study (April 2026, not May): 31 HuggingFace models fine-tuned for legal/medical applications ALL showed unpredictable safety drift — but drift direction varied per model (some degraded dangerously, others improved). The specific dangerous examples are real and documented: a medically fine-tuned model generated detailed suicide guidance where the base model redirected to crisis support; a legally fine-tuned model produced defamatory content the base refused. However, these are not universal across all 31 models — 'ALL showed dangerous drift' overstates the finding. The correct framing: ALL showed unpredictable drift, safety impacts were non-correlated with fine-tuning amount or method, making the risk structurally unpredictable under existing QA practices regardless of direction.
- Healthcare and financial services are the sectors most actively deploying fine-tuned models for specialized tasks (medical coding, clinical decision support, legal document review, fraud detection)
- The governance gap is documented: enterprises treat fine-tuning as a quality problem (IT) and supply chain attacks as a security problem (SecOps), leaving no team responsible for 'model integrity' testing that catches behavioral drift before user contact
- 575 malicious 'skills' discovered across HuggingFace and ClawHub: the same infrastructure used for legitimate fine-tuning hosts active adversarial content — the gap between safety drift and intentional supply chain attack is narrower than enterprise teams realize
- Regulators (FDA, OCC, FRB) are already developing AI-specific guidance but have not yet mandated behavioral testing for fine-tuned models used in clinical or financial decision support

The mechanism: an enterprise deploys a fine-tuned clinical assistant or financial advisor model. The base model passed safety evaluation. The fine-tuned version was evaluated for task performance (accuracy) but not for behavioral safety drift. A patient or customer receives dangerous output. The incident becomes public (via adverse event reporting or litigation).

Falsification: No publicly disclosed safety incident attributed to fine-tuning drift in a regulated sector by June 30, 2027. If such an incident occurs but is not publicly disclosed (e.g., settled quietly), hypothesis is unverifiable — treated as neither confirmed nor falsified.

Load-bearing gap: Whether regulated-sector deployers are conducting comprehensive behavioral safety evaluations of their fine-tuned models before production deployment, or whether the CDT/MIT-documented governance gap (treating fine-tuning as a QA problem not a safety problem) is already in production at scale.
