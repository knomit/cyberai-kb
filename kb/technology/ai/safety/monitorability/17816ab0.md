# OpenAI says Astra's reasoning is harder to monitor than its predecessor's

At the GPT-6 Astra launch briefing on 2026-09-03, OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki said OpenAI found Astra's written reasoning is harder to monitor than GPT-5.6 Sol's, and that 'monitorability is getting more challenging' as models get more intelligent — because a smarter model needs less explicit language reasoning to complete a task, solving problems with fewer language tokens or none at all. Pachocki said monitoring remains a core technique for Astra but that improving it for future models is a research priority.

The general shape: capability gains directly erode the legibility that chain-of-thought monitoring depends on — a safety technique degrading as the model improves, not as it degrades.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: this is OpenAI disclosing a regression on its own safety property, which is unusual and therefore credible as a direction of travel; it does not mean Astra is unmonitorable, nor that OpenAI has a fix.

Separately, because Astra met the 'critical' cyber threshold in OpenAI's preparedness framework, the model refuses advanced cybersecurity tasks such as exploit discovery and ships stronger anti-misuse protections. OpenAI calls Astra its 'most aligned model,' citing better respect for task boundaries and claiming it is three times less likely than GPT-5.6 Sol to misrepresent its own capabilities — a vendor-reported internal measurement.
