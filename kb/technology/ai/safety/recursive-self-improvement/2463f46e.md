# Anthropic warns recursive self-improvement may arrive sooner than expected

The Anthropic Institute published findings (early June 2026) on progress toward recursive self-improvement (RSI) — models that autonomously design and develop their successors. It said RSI is not here yet nor inevitable, but 'could come sooner than most institutions are prepared for,' raising risks of humans losing control. Supporting data points: over 80% of code merged into Anthropic's codebase is now written by Claude, with engineers shipping 8x as much code as in 2024; Claude scored 76% on open-ended engineering problems in May 2026 (up 50 percentage points in six months); and Anthropic's best models suggest better next research steps than humans 64% of the time. Anthropic outlined three scenarios (stall, humans stay in control, or full RSI where human labor stops being competitive) and suggested the option to pause frontier development, while acknowledging a unilateral pause accomplishes little. The Deep View noted the warning sits uneasily with Anthropic having released a new model, raised $65 billion, and filed for an IPO in the same week.
