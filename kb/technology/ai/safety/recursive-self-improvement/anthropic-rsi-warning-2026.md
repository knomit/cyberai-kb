# Anthropic's mid-2026 RSI report: ~80% of its code written by Claude, warns RSI may arrive sooner than institutions are prepared for

In mid-2026 (published early June 2026) the Anthropic Institute released findings on progress toward recursive self-improvement (RSI) — models that autonomously design and develop their successors. Anthropic said RSI is not here yet and is not inevitable, but 'could come sooner than most institutions are prepared for,' raising risks of humans losing control.

Supporting data points cited: over 80% of code merged into Anthropic's codebase is now written by Claude (up from under 5% before Claude Code's preview); engineers ship roughly 8x as much code as in 2024 (measured as ~8x more lines per engineer in Q2 2026 versus Q1 2023); Claude scored 76% on open-ended engineering problems in May 2026, up 50 percentage points in six months; and Anthropic's best models suggest better next research steps than humans 64% of the time.

Anthropic outlined three scenarios: (1) progress stalls / AI stays below top humans; (2) AI-aided engineering accelerates with humans staying in control — which Anthropic judged most likely; (3) full RSI, in which AI systems self-improve and human labor stops being competitive. It revived the idea of a temporary global pause on frontier development while acknowledging that a unilateral pause accomplishes little.

Reactions ranged from skeptical (Miles Brundage, Arun Rao, and compute/data bottleneck arguments) to bullish (OpenAI citing 'early signs of RSI'; Sakana AI launching an RSI Lab). The Deep View noted the warning sits uneasily with Anthropic having released a new model, raised $65 billion, and filed for an IPO in the same week.
