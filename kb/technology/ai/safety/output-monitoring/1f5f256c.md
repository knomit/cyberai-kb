# A single thresholded safety score matches sequential-test LLM monitors (CRC Monitor)

LLM safety monitors that act during generation typically analyze a running series of safety scores and apply sequential hypothesis testing to decide when to raise an alarm. Mona Schirmer, Metod Jazbec, Alexander Timans, Christian Naesseth, Maja Waldron and Eric Nalisnick (University of Amsterdam, University of Wisconsin-Madison, Johns Hopkins University) show in "Online Safety Monitoring for LLMs" (arXiv 2607.02510, submitted 2026-07-02, covered in The Batch 2026-09-04) that a much simpler design is competitive: take a single verifier signal from an external model and threshold it, with the threshold calibrated by risk control.

What was measured: on mathematical reasoning and red-teaming datasets, the simple thresholded design ('CRC Monitor') achieved nearly the same performance as the more elaborate sequential monitors it was compared against.

Foreseeable misreading: this is a claim about monitor architecture complexity on the evaluated datasets — the verifier signal still comes from an external model, so it does not say monitoring is cheap or that it generalizes to agentic tool-use traces, which the paper does not evaluate.
