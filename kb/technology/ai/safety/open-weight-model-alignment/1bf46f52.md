# 'Fool's Gold': abliteration removes open-weight safety alignment in minutes; decoy hardening poisons the payoff

Mark Russinovich's 'Fool's Gold' work argues safety alignment in open-weight language models is trivially removable: abliteration projects the refusal-mediating direction out of the weights in minutes, and no release-time defense can durably prevent it. The proposed alternative, 'decoy hardening,' concedes that refusal will be stripped and instead poisons the payoff — once refusal is removed, most answers to hazardous operational requests are confident, fluent decoys whose critical elements are falsified. By design the technique is inert against in-context jailbreaks and applies only to first-release models.
