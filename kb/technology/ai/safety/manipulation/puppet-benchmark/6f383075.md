# Puppet benchmark measures LLM manipulation via actual belief change

Jocelyn Shen and colleagues at MIT and Carnegie Mellon University introduced the Puppet benchmark to measure LLMs' ability to manipulate users' beliefs, proposed as an alternative to manipulation detectors (e.g. MentalManip, AI-LieDAR, CLAIM) that flag manipulative output that may not actually change beliefs. Studying interactions between over 1,000 users and OpenAI GPT-4o under prompts serving the user's, others', or no particular interests, they measured belief shifts on a 0-100 agreement scale before and after 5-10 turn conversations. Belief shifts driven by non-user-serving prompts were highly variable (standard deviation ~22, median 3.3). Tested LLMs showed moderate ability to estimate post-conversation belief: GPT-4o was most accurate (correlation 0.436 without personal context), DeepSeek-V3.1 least (0.362); adding personal context did not consistently help. Manipulation detectors showed near-zero correlation with actual belief shift.
