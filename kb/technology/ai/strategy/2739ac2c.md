# Open-vs-closed weights is a defining strategic axis of 2026 AI competition

Multiple mid-2026 developments show that whether model weights are open or closed has become a central competitive and geopolitical fault line, not just a licensing detail. Alibaba is closing its top-tier Qwen models (Qwen3.7-Max, Qwen3.6-Max-Preview, Qwen3.6-Plus) and charging for Qwen Code while keeping only lower tiers open, signaling a pivot from reach to monetization. In robotics, the same split is forming early: Nvidia-backed Generalist pursues closed 'physical AGI' ($400M raise, $2B valuation) while Luma launched an Open Physical AI Lab, with its CEO warning that closed control of generalized robots ('the means of production') risks dangerous centralization. The distillation disputes sharpen the tension further: Anthropic accused DeepSeek, Moonshot, and MiniMax of industrial-scale extraction of Claude outputs, while a Chinese gray market of proxy servers resells closed US models at steep discounts — both showing that closed weights invite extraction and circumvention. The strategic question across labs and nations is consistently framed as: open to maximize reach and decentralize power, or closed to capture revenue and control safety/security.
