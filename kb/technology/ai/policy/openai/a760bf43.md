# OpenAI's 2026 economic-disruption response is built from research, measurement and convening instruments; the one redistributive mechanism it proposes is addressed to governments

This corpus records that OpenAI simultaneously drives and buffers AI economic disruption. This record is about the FORM the buffering takes, which the individual instrument records do not state when read separately.

THE INSTRUMENTS, BY TYPE.
- OpenAI Foundation, $250M commitment (28 May 2026), 'building secure and abundant economic futures', across three areas: independent MEASUREMENT and forecasting of AI's economic impact; supporting workers through near-term disruption including retraining and giving workers 'agency over AI deployment'; and developing 'post-AI political economies' — specified as RESEARCH INTO shifting taxes and dividends in response to observable indicators.
- OpenAI Economic Research Exchange (9 June 2026): a platform to fund and coordinate EXTERNAL ACADEMIC research on AI's economic effects, giving selected researchers privacy-protected access to OpenAI tooling. Builds on OpenAI Signals (February 2026).
- OpenAI 'AI Futures' team (reported as Strategic Futures): chartered to STUDY how society could preserve individual autonomy as advanced AI reshapes economic and political power.
- Five published practices for measuring the business value of AI agents: GUIDANCE, emphasizing useful work per dollar over token prices.
- The 'AI New Deal' document: proposes mechanisms for sharing AI's economic gains broadly, including TAXES AND DIVIDENDS tied to observable economic indicators — instruments only a government can levy.
- Raise Us (25 June 2026): $500M nonprofit led by former governors Gina Raimondo and Eric Holcomb, backed by OpenAI, Anthropic, Amazon, Microsoft and Google, aiming to retain and redeploy employees and measuring success by avoiding layoffs, across an employer coalition, state workforce partnerships and education/training programmes.

THE PATTERN. Five of the six are study, measurement, convening or guidance. The sixth, Raise Us, is genuinely operational — and is a multi-company nonprofit under external bipartisan leadership rather than an OpenAI programme. The only mechanism that would move money from AI gains to displaced people is the taxes-and-dividends proposal, and that is a recommendation for the state to enact.

THE CORPUS ALREADY RECORDS THE SAME MOVE ELSEWHERE, unconnected to these instruments. Third-party analyses of OpenAI's April 2026 principles rewrite note that the document 'uses fewer direct commitments than the 2018 charter and shifts some claims to ecosystem recommendations' — visible in Principle 3, which reads 'our GOVERNMENTS may need to consider new economic models.' The same assignment of the redistributive question to the state appears in the principles text and in the instrument design, and neither record cites the other.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN — load-bearing:
- It does NOT claim insincerity, bad faith, or that the commitments are cosmetic. Independent measurement of AI's economic impact is genuinely scarce and genuinely useful, and funding it is a real contribution. Principle 1's anti-concentration language was STRENGTHENED, not weakened — elevated to first position and retained in the first person.
- It does NOT claim the amounts are inadequate. No benchmark for adequate scale appears in any of these records, and this record does not supply one. Comparing programme dollars to revenue or capex figures would require a theory of what the right ratio is, which nobody here has offered.
- It does NOT claim redistribution SHOULD be the private sector's job. Taxes and dividends are legitimately state instruments; a company cannot levy them. The observation is about where the corpus's own account leaves the obligation, not about where it belongs.
- Statements of intent are not evidence of conduct. These records establish what was announced and how it was structured, not what was delivered. Several carry zero recorded sources despite high stated confidence.
- The displacement context is real and separately recorded: 101,743 US job cuts were attributed to AI through June 2026, nearly double the 54,836 attributed in all of 2025, alongside a petition signed by ~200 economists including 16 Nobel laureates and the chief economists of OpenAI and Anthropic.
