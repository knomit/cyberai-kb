# U.S. Government Launches TRAINS Pre-Release AI Evaluation Framework (May 2026)

In May 2026, the U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) announced that a new multi-agency task force — Testing Risks of AI for National Security (TRAINS), run by NIST's Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI) — will assess national-security risks posed by AI models prior to their public deployment. This marks a sharp reversal from the Trump Administration's earlier hands-off, laissez-faire approach to AI regulation.

Key details:
- TRAINS draws on NIST, Departments of Commerce, Defense, Energy, Homeland Security, the NSA, and the NIH
- Tests will focus on demonstrable risks to cybersecurity, biosecurity, and chemical weapons
- Google, Microsoft, and xAI agreed to provide models with limited or absent guardrails for evaluation; Anthropic and OpenAI had agreed to similar terms in 2024
- The White House is considering an executive order that would make pre-release government testing mandatory (currently voluntary)
- CAISI published an earlier comparison of DeepSeek V4 Pro against other LLMs using an aggregate of 9 public benchmarks plus PortBench (CLI tool porting between programming languages)

Context: The policy shift was triggered in part by Anthropic's announcement that Claude Mythos Preview could autonomously exploit vulnerabilities in major operating systems and applications. The Trump Administration initially removed Biden-era regulatory barriers after taking office in January 2025; Biden's 2023 executive order required notification for models trained at roughly 1 trillion parameter scale.

DeepLearning.AI's take: Standardized benchmarks would benefit industry, but the right mechanism is the free market, not government mandate; mandatory pre-release testing slows U.S. developers and creates regulatory capture risk against open-source competitors.
