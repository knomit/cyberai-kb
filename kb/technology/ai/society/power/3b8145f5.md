# HYPOTHESIS: PE Deployment Arms Become Dominant AI Enterprise Channel by 2027, Structural Barrier to Entry

Prediction: The PE deployment arm model (OpenAI TPG/Bain/Brookfield joint venture; Anthropic/Blackstone talks) will become the dominant enterprise sales channel for frontier AI by end of 2027, creating structural barriers where enterprise AI access depends on investor relationships rather than product merit, effectively foreclosing market entry for labs without PE backing.

Evidence chain: OpenAI's PE joint venture structure ($10B pre-money, PE commits $4B for equity stakes + board seats + early tool access + embedded engineers within enterprise clients) is not a distribution deal — it is a capture mechanism. PE firms control portfolio companies totaling trillions in enterprise IT spend. If both OpenAI and Anthropic (Blackstone talks) successfully close PE deployment arms, the two largest frontier labs will have direct distribution pipelines into the majority of Fortune 500 companies via existing investor relationships. Fidji Simo framed this as 'deploying engineers within enterprise clients' — the stickiness is structural: once engineers are embedded and workflows are built on top of a specific AI stack, switching costs compound quarterly. US National AI Policy Framework's regulatory preemption removes state-level countervailing pressure that might otherwise limit such concentration.

Reasoning step: This is the 'enterprise capture before commodity' play. Historical parallel: Salesforce's AppExchange + consulting partner ecosystem locked enterprises into Salesforce CRM before open alternatives matured. The key mechanic: PE firms' fiduciary duty runs to their LPs (pension funds, endowments) — once they have equity in an AI lab plus deployed relationships in portfolio companies, they have a financial incentive to deepen AI adoption at those companies regardless of quality competition. The asymmetric cost methodology (8363e53b) applies: marginal cost of expanding within PE portfolio = near-zero (existing relationships), while marginal cost of switching to a competitor = high (re-embedded engineers, retraining, workflow migration).

Known gaps: Deals have not closed; structure could change. Open-source alternatives (Mistral, LLaMA) could commoditize the model layer and make PE distribution advantage less relevant. Enterprise procurement reforms (government mandates for competitive bidding) could counter channel capture.

Falsification condition: If OpenAI and Anthropic PE joint ventures do not close by Q2 2027, or if open-source models achieve enterprise adoption benchmarks within 15% of frontier closed models by end of 2026, the channel-capture hypothesis fails.
