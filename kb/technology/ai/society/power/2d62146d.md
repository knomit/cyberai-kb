In March-April 2026, the two largest frontier AI labs simultaneously published pro-democratization manifestos and entered structural arrangements that drive consolidation. Sam Altman published 'Our Principles' pledging resistance to power concentration ('power held in a decentralized way by people'). Concurrently OpenAI closed a $110B funding round (SoftBank $30B, Nvidia $30B, Amazon $50B) and entered a PE deployment arm structure giving TPG, Bain and Brookfield equity stakes, board seats, and embedded engineers within portfolio companies. Google deepened its Anthropic investment to $40B, and Cohere acquired Aleph Alpha for $20B, consolidating European frontier AI.

The gap is not hypocrisy per se - it reflects a structural resolution mechanism: democratization rhetoric attracts regulatory goodwill, talent and public trust; consolidation structures attract capital, enterprise distribution and durable revenue. These serve different audiences on different timescales. Jason Hiner's observation about the IPO trajectory: once OpenAI goes public, fiduciary duty to shareholders will systematically override the democratization pledge, because quarterly earnings require revenue concentration, not distribution.

PE firms' fiduciary duty runs to their LPs, not the public interest. Once PE firms hold equity in a frontier lab plus deployed relationships in portfolio companies, they have a financial incentive to deepen AI adoption at those companies regardless of competitive alternatives. The OpenAI-Microsoft amendment (non-exclusive Azure relationship, AGI clause removed) alongside the AWS Bedrock partnership creates the appearance of multi-cloud competition while embedding OpenAI deeper into cloud infrastructure at every tier.

**Predictive implication (a PREDICTION embedded in a synthesis, not an observation):** by 2028, the organizations holding the most concentrated AI power will be those whose democratization rhetoric was loudest in 2026 - because the rhetoric was the mechanism for acquiring the goodwill needed to close the capital and distribution arrangements that enable consolidation. This is unsettled and carries no settlement criteria.
