# OpenAI IPO Will Confirm Fiduciary Override of Democratization Pledge by Q4 2028

Hypothesis: Within four quarters of OpenAI's IPO (predicted to occur by end of 2027), at least two of the following observable signals will emerge, confirming that fiduciary duty to shareholders has materially overridden the 'Our Principles' democratization pledge:

1. SMB/individual pricing increases >15% across any tier, prioritizing enterprise revenue concentration
2. S-1 or earnings disclosure showing top-20 accounts represent >50% of total revenue
3. PE deployment arm (TPG/Bain/Brookfield embedded engineers) active in ≥20 portfolio companies with public documentation
4. Formal amendment or public walk-back of the 'decentralized power' commitment in 'Our Principles'

Mechanism: The synthesis fact (2d62146d) establishes that the $110B capital raise and PE deployment arm structure serve different audiences than the democratization rhetoric — the former is structural consolidation, the latter attracts regulatory goodwill and talent. Post-IPO, quarterly earnings pressure creates systematic incentive to deepen revenue concentration. The PE deployment arm methodology (9949ae2d) confirms the embedded engineers clause creates human relationship lock-in within enterprise clients that deepens regardless of competitive alternatives.

Falsification: If OpenAI IPOs by end of 2027 AND none of the four signals above are observable within four quarters post-IPO, the hypothesis is disconfirmed. If the IPO is delayed past 2028, the hypothesis is suspended pending the triggering event.

Load-bearing gap: The S-1 filing revenue concentration data (pre-IPO, not yet public) is the single most decisive piece of evidence. If top-10 customers are <25% of revenue at IPO time, the consolidation trajectory is weaker than the synthesis implies and confidence should be revised down to ~0.40. Conversely, if the PE deployment arm reports embedded engineers in >30 portfolio companies before IPO, confidence rises toward 0.75.

Confidence set at 0.63 (below the synthesis's 0.87) because: the IPO timeline is uncertain; PE deployment arms can be unwound faster than software integrations (per 9949ae2d); and regulatory antitrust action against the PE structure remains a genuine wildcard.
