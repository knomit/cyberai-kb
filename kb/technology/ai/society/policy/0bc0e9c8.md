# Anthropic defies Pentagon - pre-ban stance on mass surveillance and autonomous weapons

Before being designated a 'supply chain risk,' Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei issued a statement (Feb 27) refusing the Pentagon/Department of War's demands to remove two guardrails from Claude: (1) mass surveillance of US citizens ('serious, novel risks to our fundamental liberties'), (2) fully autonomous weapons ('guardrails simply don't exist today to deploy this safely'). Amodei: 'In a narrow set of cases, we believe AI can undermine, rather than defend, democratic values. Some uses are also simply outside the bounds of what today's technology can safely and reliably do.' Context: Claude already widely deployed in defense/intelligence (classified networks, national labs, intelligence analysis, modeling/simulation, operational planning, cybersecurity operations) — guardrails had not impeded any of these. Pentagon struck a parallel deal with xAI to use Grok models in classified systems including weapons development and battlefield operations. Pete Hegseth threatened to invoke the Defense Production Act to force Anthropic to comply. Former Trump AI adviser Dean Ball called Hegseth's threats 'incoherent.' Strategic logic: Anthropic positioned the stance as winning long-term enterprise trust vs. losing short-term government revenue.
