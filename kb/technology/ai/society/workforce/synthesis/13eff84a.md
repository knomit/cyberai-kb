# The AI Workforce Paradox: Jobs Cut, Skills Atrophying, Oversight Burden Rising — Simultaneously (May 2026)

Three distinct but reinforcing AI workforce dynamics are occurring in parallel in mid-2026:

**1. Headcount reduction (contested):** Challenger, Gray & Christmas: 21,000+ layoffs in April 2026 attributed to AI — second consecutive month AI was the leading stated cause. But White House NEC Director Kevin Hassett: 'no sign in the data.' 60% of C-suite execs (Writer survey) plan to lay off employees who won't use AI. Analyst view: pandemic overhiring + 'AI-washing' (using AI as scapegoat) likely explain many cuts.

**2. Skills atrophy (silent):** ServiceNow CLO Jayney Howson: workers are outsourcing judgment to AI, degrading ability to read people, make calls under uncertainty, trust instincts. Most visible in sales where AI generates all prep materials. Three skills now required: role competency + AI proficiency + judgment of *when NOT to use AI* — the third is hardest to teach and easiest to lose. Structural risk: if AI systems fail, over-reliant workers cannot fall back on their own judgment.

**3. Management overhead (hidden):** ServiceNow data: deploying AI agents increases manager workload, not reduces it — supervisors must train agents, monitor behavior, quality-check responses. Token costs can accumulate rapidly (combined human+agent usage). AI agents are best treated as workflow components, not team members.

**Synthesis**: The assumption that AI primarily replaces low-skill work while augmenting high-skill workers is too simple. Evidence in 2026 suggests AI simultaneously eliminates some roles, degrades judgment of remaining workers, and shifts manager burden upward. The net organizational effect may be negative productivity for 1-3 years during a transition period — a fact suppressed by both corporate messaging and political framing.
