# AI democratizes propaganda capacity: state-level information warfare now accessible to individuals and non-state actors

The March 2026 US-Iran conflict demonstrated that AI video generation tools have crossed the threshold that democratizes propaganda capacity previously available only to states: individuals and non-state actors can now produce and distribute at-scale disinformation at state-level volume and quality. Key evidence: (1) Fake fighter jet footage: 70M views from what turned out to be video game footage; (2) Tel Aviv fake: 20M views, AI-generated, and Grok falsely validated it as real when users tried to verify — showing AI systems can amplify AI-generated fakes; (3) X/Twitter's monetization threat forced a structural incentive change for creators, but geopolitical incentives remain; (4) Melanie Smith, ISD: 'The volume of AI content is starting to just pollute the information environment.' Pattern: AI disinformation has moved from 'rare but sophisticated' (state-level) to 'ubiquitous and credible-looking' (individual-level). The social cost is borne by news organizations and fact-checkers who must debunk content faster than it spreads — an asymmetric burden that cannot scale.
