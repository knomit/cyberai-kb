# HYPOTHESIS: Competitive Race Logic Collapses Self-Imposed AI Safety Governance by End of 2026

Prediction: All frontier AI labs will have effectively abandoned meaningful self-imposed safety governance frameworks (RSP-equivalents) by end of 2026, with competitive race logic — not capability evidence — as the stated justification, creating a regulatory vacuum requiring external government intervention.

Evidence chain: The Anthropic RSP loosening provides the template. Jared Kaplan's exact framing — 'We didn't really feel, with the rapid advance of AI, that it made sense for us to make unilateral commitments if competitors are blazing ahead' — is structurally a race-to-the-bottom argument: any lab can cite any other lab's behavior to justify weakening its own constraints. This logic, once stated publicly, becomes reusable. OpenAI's $110B PE deployment arm adds structural pressure: PE investors prioritize deployment velocity, not safety margins. US National AI Policy Framework preempts state regulation and is designed to be 'minimally burdensome', removing the backup governance layer. Stargate 'no longer on solid ground' means the government-adjacent safety narrative is destabilizing. The social media precedent (Facebook connection → ad empire) suggests that utopian founding narratives reliably yield to monetization pressure within 5-7 years of scale.

Reasoning step: Applying the asymmetric cost analysis methodology (8363e53b): the marginal cost of each successive RSP loosening is near-zero (update a policy document), while the marginal benefit (faster deployment, competitive parity, PE investor satisfaction) is large and immediate. This asymmetry creates structural pressure toward progressive weakening — not because labs are malicious, but because the incentive gradient runs entirely in one direction. The government pressure as market segmentation methodology (2b918224) suggests the outcome: the market will bifurcate into safety-theater labs (enterprise/regulated) and genuinely unconstrained labs (defense, government, emerging market), but the 'safety theater' labs will not maintain substantive constraints.

Known gaps: External regulatory intervention (EU AI Act, NIST frameworks, state laws) could arrest the collapse; this hypothesis assumes US federal preemption dominates. Catastrophic failure event (major AI-caused incident) could reverse the race logic instantly. Some labs (e.g., DeepMind within Google) may maintain internal governance without public RSPs.

Falsification condition: If any frontier lab (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind) publicly strengthens its safety governance framework (adds constraints rather than removes them) by end of 2026, or if US Congress passes binding AI safety legislation by Q3 2026, the hypothesis is falsified.
