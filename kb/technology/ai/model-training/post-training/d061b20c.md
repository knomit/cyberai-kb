# A 2026 post-training pattern: train on the agent harness and workflow, not just domain text

Two independently reported 2026 post-training efforts converge on the same move: the object of training is the *harness and workflow the model operates inside*, not additional domain-specific text.

- TaoLive's Harness-Aware Training is a post-training recipe (explicitly not a new architecture). During supervised fine-tuning and agentic RL it deliberately varies skill names and wording, tool schemas, prompt structure, and hook behavior, so a compact 35B model learns to interpret whatever harness it currently receives instead of memorizing one fixed interface.
- Harvey post-trained a Kimi K3 base with asynchronous RL inside realistic long-horizon legal environments, then trained separate capabilities (diligence, review tables, firm knowledge) that the main system routes to as tools or subagents. Its stated claim is that a professional-services model improves by learning the workflow and harness rather than by ingesting more legal text.

The shared implication is that interface robustness and workflow competence are trainable targets distinct from domain knowledge, and that they can be pursued on comparatively small base models.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: this is a two-example convergence, not an established result. BOTH source evaluations are vendor-authored — TaoLive's is additionally concentrated in live-commerce scenarios, and Harvey's is additionally task-specific. Neither has independent replication, neither reports head-to-head comparison against domain-text-scaling baselines, and neither establishes that harness-oriented post-training generalizes beyond its own reported domain.
