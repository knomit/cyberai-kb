# SPADE co-evolves synthetic executable environments and agent capability via self-play

SPADE (Self-Play in Adaptive Synthetic Executable Environments) is a framework from a multi-university group (University of Washington, Stanford, Northeastern, Carnegie Mellon, MIT, National University of Singapore, Seoul National University, Stevens Institute of Technology, University of Chicago). An LLM alternates between two roles: an Environment Designer that writes complete long-horizon training environments as executable Python programs with a Gym-style interface, and a Reasoning Agent that learns to act in them. The Designer's reward is 'hint-based regret': the gap in Reasoning Agent return with versus without a privileged hint attached to the environment. Tested on three Qwen3 backbones (Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507, Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507), each tuned via GRPO for 400 rollouts of 25 environments. At 30B-A3B, SPADE reaches a game-suite average of 58.3 — +8.1 over base and +5.3 over the strongest fixed-environment baseline — and improves every backbone on tool-use environments. The authors note it cannot bootstrap models substantially beyond the imaginative capabilities of the base model used for environment generation.
