# The evidence that open-weight models carry most inference volume comes from two commercial routing layers, one of which was just acquired partly for that vantage

This corpus states in several places that open-weight models now carry the majority of production inference. That claim is well supported, and it is worth recording WHERE the support comes from, because the answer is narrower than the claim's generality suggests.

THE MEASUREMENTS AND THEIR SOURCES.
- 'A majority of production tokens route through open-weight models, and the five highest-volume models on OpenRouter are all open' (kb/technology/ai/ecosystem/open-source/1c545b25.md). Source: OpenRouter's own volume rankings.
- 'Over 30% of tokens used on Chinese open-weight models came from the US, surging as high as 46% in some weeks of 2026' (kb/technology/ai/open-models/adoption/7a7b1c3f.md). Source: explicitly 'an OpenRouter report'.
- 'DeepSeek took nearly 23% of the tens of trillions of tokens on Vercel's enterprise AI gateway in June 2026 versus Anthropic's 32%' (carried in kb/technology/ai/ecosystem/82fc2f27.md). Source: Vercel's gateway index.

SO THE EVIDENCE BASE IS TWO COMMERCIAL ROUTING/GATEWAY OPERATORS. This is NOT single-sourcing — OpenRouter and Vercel are independent of each other, and their figures agree in direction, which is real corroboration. But neither is a neutral measurement body, both see only the traffic that passes through them, and both have a commercial interest in the volume they intermediate looking significant. No independent or academic measurement of production inference share appears in this corpus.

WHY IT MATTERS NOW. Stripe agreed to acquire OpenRouter for more than $7 billion (kb/business/technology/ai-industry/acquisitions/fc07e5cb.md), against a valuation of roughly $1.3 billion after its May 2026 round — a jump of over 5x. A second record (kb/business/companies/stripe/acquisitions/c3d33373.md) reports OpenRouter 'manages over 10 trillion tokens per day across the model ecosystem, giving it cross-network behavioral data on model usage that no individual provider or lab possesses,' and argues the strategic rationale is that data vantage rather than payments. So one of the two vantage points from which this market is observed now has an owner with its own interests, and the price paid was substantially for the observation position itself.

CAVEATS, LOAD-BEARING.
- The acquisition rationale is ANALYSIS, not a stated rationale from Stripe; c3d33373 says 'analysis argues'. The $7B figure is reported ('reportedly agreed'), not confirmed, and both acquisition records are single-source.
- 7a7b1c3f carries confidence 0.6 and sources 0; 1c545b25 carries 0.65.
- This record does NOT claim the open-weight share figures are wrong, inflated, or motivated. Direction-agreement between two independent operators is meaningful evidence and nothing here contradicts it.
- CARRIED FORWARD FROM 82fc2f27, whose qualifier must not be dropped: the claim is about WORKLOAD VOLUME, NOT CAPABILITY LEADERSHIP. It does not mean open models match frontier closed models on capability, nor that closed-model revenue or share is declining — only that most workloads do not require the frontier.
- WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: it does not claim the Stripe acquisition will distort OpenRouter's published figures. No evidence of that exists. The point is about the STRUCTURE of the evidence base — two interested intermediaries, no neutral measurement — and about knowing that when weighting the claim.

PRACTICAL IMPLICATION: when this corpus's open-weight-share claims are used to support a further inference, check whether the new claim needs a neutral measurement or only needs direction. Direction is supported; precise share is not independently established.
