# Meta's 2026 open-weights withdrawal was tier-specific, not a exit: it closed the flagship line and returned to open weights at the local tier within four months

The corpus records both halves of a reversal without stating it. In mid-April 2026 Meta released the closed/proprietary Muse Spark, characterized as ending Meta's role as the leading US open-weights champion. On 11 August 2026 Meta released Muse Glimmer — 30B parameters, Apache 2.0, small enough to run locally on a Mac or PC with a single consumer GPU — accompanied by a ~6,500-word Mark Zuckerberg essay, 'The Future is for Everyone,' arguing for decentralized 'personal superintelligence' and a balance of power against AI centralization.

These cannot both describe a standing strategy. What they jointly entail is narrower and more useful than either: Meta's withdrawal from open weights was TIER-SPECIFIC. The flagship line went closed and stayed closed (Muse Spark, Muse Spark 1.1 via the paid Meta Model API, and the still-in-training Watermelon); the small/local tier returned to permissive licensing four months later. The same shape is recorded for Alibaba, which closed its top-tier Qwen models while keeping lower tiers open — which is why this instantiates openness-as-market-wedge rather than a change of principle.

CONSEQUENCE FOR READERS: the standing characterization 'Meta abandoned open weights' is accurate only as of April 2026 and only for the flagship tier. Any fact or synthesis carrying that clause without a date and a tier qualifier is stale as written, including the open-weights-bifurcation synthesis that carries it.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: it does not claim Meta reverted wholesale — the flagship line remains proprietary, and Muse Glimmer is a 30B model, not a frontier release. It does not endorse the proliferation-as-safety argument: Jack Clark's critique is that the essay is silent on whether a system capable of superhuman invention would in fact work solely on behalf of individuals less capable at invention than itself. It does not claim large frontier models are obsolete. Benchmark and price comparisons in the cited sources are vendor-reported and scoped to the named indices; see the source facts for exact figures and their caveats.
