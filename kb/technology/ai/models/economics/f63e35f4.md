# HYPOTHESIS: Agentic duration workloads give open-weights models a structural cost advantage that drives 20%+ enterprise coding share by end of 2027

Prediction: Open-weights AI models (GLM-5.1-class and successors) will account for 20%+ of enterprise AI coding API spending by end of 2027, captured specifically through long-duration agentic coding workflows where token volume and cost dominance over closed frontier models.

Evidence chain: (1) Z.ai GLM-5.1 leads SWE-Bench Pro at 58.4% vs GPT-5.4 at 57.7% — quality is already at parity; (2) GLM-5.1 is priced at $1.40/M input tokens vs ~$15-25/M for frontier closed models — a 10-17x cost differential; (3) 8-hour autonomous coding sessions consume enormous token volumes: at 1M tokens/hour, an 8-hour session costs ~$11.20 with GLM-5.1 vs $120-200 with frontier closed models; (4) Enterprise AI coding workloads are increasingly agentic (planning/execution/evaluation loops) where token volume scales with session duration, amplifying the cost differential; (5) The 'enterprise ROI data validates productivity hypotheses more than benchmarks' methodology (d84d0e26) predicts that procurement will shift to measuring cost-per-completed-task rather than benchmark scores — at which point open-weights wins on economics.

Reasoning step: The decisive question is whether enterprises primarily run short, high-complexity tasks (favoring frontier model quality) or long, structured-complexity tasks (favoring open-weights cost). The Neurometric 75/25 data shows 75% of enterprise tasks are structured/repetitive — exactly the type that benefits from SLM/open-weights economics. For the 25% requiring frontier reasoning, closed models retain an advantage. But agentic coding sessions specifically are long-duration structured-complexity tasks, putting them squarely in the 75% bucket where cost matters most. The MIT RLM architecture (spawning submodels) suggests the future of long-horizon task handling may use open-weights submodels for most execution loops anyway.

Known gaps: Enterprise on-premise compliance requirements (data sovereignty) may drive open-weights adoption independently of cost, accelerating the timeline; frontier labs may respond by creating cost-competitive tiers specifically for agentic workloads; GLM-5.1 may have quality gaps in non-coding domains that limit total share.

Falsification condition: If independent enterprise AI survey data by Q4 2027 shows open-weights models accounting for fewer than 15% of enterprise AI coding token volume, the hypothesis fails. Counter-signal to watch: if frontier labs introduce sub-$2/M pricing tiers for agentic coding workloads before mid-2027, the cost differential collapses.
