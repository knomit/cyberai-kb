# Typesafe's Jev: a 'System One Model' claiming ~238x cheaper input tokens, 70-500ms latency, and no hallucination

Typesafe AI — founded by Diogo Almeida, a former OpenAI researcher and co-inventor of the reinforcement learning tactic foundational to ChatGPT — emerged from stealth in mid-September 2026 with $40 million in funding and announced Jev, which it positions as a 'System One Model': a new class of frontier model built to make fast, structured decisions that software consumes directly, rather than chat responses for humans.

Typesafe's claims, all of them vendor claims from its own launch post with no independent benchmark published:
- Price: just over 4 cents per million input tokens, roughly 238x cheaper than GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.1 at $10 per million input tokens. Output tokens are free because they are 'too cheap to meter', versus $50 per million from those competitors.
- Speed: two orders of magnitude faster, with end-to-end response times of 70–500 milliseconds versus 3–329 seconds for existing LLMs.
- Intelligence: comparable to existing large language models on System One tasks.
- Hallucination: cannot hallucinate.

Mechanism as described by Typesafe: Jev is 'neither small nor an LLM' and replaces sequential generation with parallel computation. Input is a piece of text (up to 32K tokens) plus one or more questions about it (each also capped, total input up to 64K tokens); questions are evaluated in parallel and users are billed only for input tokens, not output. Output can be a yes/no probability, a choice from a list (with confidence and per-item probabilities), or a 0-10 rubric score. It is optimized with a method Typesafe calls 'Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated Decisions' (RLCD), which trains the model to assign probabilities to answers matching how often they are empirically correct (e.g. an answer given 20% probability should be correct 20% of the time), answering queries with 'epistemically honest probabilities' rather than programmatically verified outputs written to human preferences.

INDEPENDENT-ISH DATAPOINT (via The Batch, Sept 25 2026, citing TypeSafe's own internal benchmark): across four internal datasets (labeled using GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5 as ground truth), Jev achieved 67% accuracy, about the same as GPT-5.6 Terra and Claude Sonnet 5, while costing about $0.0007/example (vs ~$0.06 for Terra and ~$0.12 for Sonnet) and running a claimed 193.6x faster than the unspecified LLMs compared. This is still TypeSafe's own reported number, not third-party verified.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: the 'cannot hallucinate' claim rests on constraining outputs to a structured space and is not an independently verified property, and the price/speed gaps have not been shown to hold at equal task quality on a public, third-party benchmark. Jev is in early access ($0.042 or free per million input/output tokens), suited to AI-powered workflows and real-time applications, explicitly NOT to human-in-the-loop or chatbot tasks. Shortly after release, competing classification models emerged: Laya (multilingual focus), Bespoke Nimble (fine-tunes Qwen-3.5-9B), and Kev (also Qwen-3.5-based, in three sizes) -- none claims to have distilled Jev, and none has a public benchmark either. Platforms Vercel and Cloudflare added support for Jev for use cases like tool selection.
