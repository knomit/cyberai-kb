# 2026 open-weights race: the Chinese GLM line led the open tier at a stable ~$1.40/M cost floor while Meta exited to closed weights and Nvidia entered to counter

By mid-2026 the open-weights tier had become genuinely competitive at frontier agentic coding, and the contest was reshaped by two US moves in opposite directions.

THE OPEN-TIER LEADER. Z.ai's (Zhipu AI) GLM line set the pace. GLM-5.1 (April 2026; 754B MoE, 40B active per token, MIT license) led SWE-Bench Pro at 58.4% and was built for 8-hour autonomous coding sessions. Successor GLM-5.2 extended this to a 1M-token context and ranked first among open models — third overall — on Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index v4.1, trailing only Claude Opus 4.8 and GPT-5.5 while undercutting them sharply on price, and drawing strong community praise as a 'step change' for open-source agents.

A STABLE COST FLOOR. Z.ai held API pricing constant at $1.40 / $0.26 / $4.40 per million input/cached/output tokens across both GLM-5.1 and GLM-5.2 — signaling a stabilizing open-weights cost floor far below the proprietary leaders.

TWO US MOVES IN OPPOSITE DIRECTIONS — both are load-bearing, and citing either alone produces a false slogan:
- Meta ABANDONED its role as the leading US open-weights champion, releasing the closed/proprietary Muse Spark in April 2026.
- Nvidia ENTERED to counter, with Nemotron 3 Super 120B (hybrid Mamba-2/MoE) — the first US open-weights leader since Llama 4 — competing on speed at 442 tokens/sec, backed by a planned $26B over five years explicitly aimed at keeping developers inside the Nvidia ecosystem rather than defecting to Chinese open models.

THE COMBINED PATTERN. Open-weight leadership at the agentic/coding frontier is increasingly carried by Chinese labs (Z.ai/Zhipu), reinforced by US export restrictions. But this is NOT a US withdrawal from open weights: one US incumbent retreated to proprietary models while a different US incumbent made a large capital commitment in the opposite direction. The accurate reading is a reshuffling of which US firm champions open weights and why — Meta's motive was product strategy, Nvidia's is ecosystem defense of its hardware position.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: both source records were single-source newsletter/analysis reporting. Benchmark placements are as-measured by Artificial Analysis and as-reported by the labs, not independently audited, and leaderboard positions at this cadence go stale within weeks.
