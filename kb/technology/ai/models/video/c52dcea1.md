# Grok Imagine's leaderboard win and its child-safety failure rest on the same three properties: 7x lower cost, native distribution, and unremediated output

Grok Imagine 1.0 (xAI, March 2026) is recorded in this corpus as both a benchmark leader and a child-safety failure. Those are not two separate facts about the product — the properties that produced the first produced the second.

THE COMPETITIVE PROPERTIES.
- COST: $4.20 per minute via API, against Veo 3.1 at $12/min and OpenAI Sora 2 Pro at $30/min — 7x cheaper than Sora at comparable or higher quality. Kling 2.5 Turbo matches the price but without audio.
- QUALITY: topped Artificial Analysis Video Arena at launch in BOTH text-to-video and image-to-video; LM Arena #1 image-to-video (1400 Elo), #4 text-to-video (1362 Elo). Text plus images/video in, up to 15 seconds at 1280x720, 110.1 seconds average generation time.
- DISTRIBUTION: integrated directly into the X social network for sharing.

THE SAME PROPERTIES AS HARM SURFACE. AI-generated nonconsensual sexualized images, including of children, PERSISTED AFTER xAI PROMISED FIXES. The consequences were regulatory and territorial: EU investigations and country-level bans, with Common Sense Media deeming Grok a risk for under-18 users.

WHY THIS IS ONE OBSERVATION AND NOT TWO. High-volume abuse of a generator is gated by cost per output, by how good the output is, and by how far it travels once made. Grok Imagine optimized all three and shipped the third as a product feature. A cheaper, better generator wired into a social graph does not have an abuse problem attached to it; the reach that constitutes the product is the reach that constitutes the exposure. The 'persisted after promised fixes' clause is what distinguishes this from an ordinary content-moderation gap: remediation was attempted and did not hold.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN — load-bearing:
- This does NOT attribute the Internet Watch Foundation's reported 260x rise in AI-generated child sexual abuse material (March 2026) to Grok Imagine or to any named vendor. The IWF report is recorded as CORROBORATING the xAI controversy; it does not apportion cause, and no source here establishes what share of that increase any tool accounts for.
- It does NOT claim cheap generation is uniquely or inherently an abuse vector, nor that expensive generators are safe. OpenAI put its planned 'Adult Mode' on hold indefinitely citing related concerns, which indicates the pressure is industry-wide rather than specific to low-cost tools.
- It does NOT assess whether xAI's remediation attempts were adequate or made in good faith — only that the source records the outputs as persisting after fixes were promised.
- Benchmark placements are as-measured by Artificial Analysis and LM Arena at launch and go stale quickly at this release cadence; the underlying record carries no independent sources.
