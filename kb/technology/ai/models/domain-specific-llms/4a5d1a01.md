# Thomson Reuters launched 'Thomson', a ~$40M domain-specific LLM family continued-trained from Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (Aug 2026)

LAUNCH. On Monday 2026-08-24 Thomson Reuters launched Thomson, its first proprietary large language model family. At launch it is available in CoCounsel Legal, built on the company's Fiduciary-Grade AI standard for regulated industries. A 35B open-weights version (Thomson-1.0-Small) ships on Hugging Face for NON-COMMERCIAL use only.

HOW IT WAS BUILT. Built in-house by taking an open-source frontier model as the foundation — Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (397B parameters, 17B active) — and continuing training on decades of proprietary content including Westlaw, Practical Law, Checkpoint and Reuters. Training reportedly took about three months and roughly $40 million in talent and compute, using a 200-billion-token curated mid-training dataset drawn from a 19-trillion-token pool. CTO Joel Hron said starting from open-source frontier models lets the company train highly capable systems at a fraction of the cost of training from scratch. Only about 10% of Thomson Reuters' content has been used so far; the stated goal is to convert more content into targeted training data rather than simply add volume.

REPORTED RESULTS. Early evaluations put Thomson on par with the latest frontier models across a range of tasks. In comparisons of GPT-5.4, Claude Sonnet 5 and Thomson-1.0-Large on open-web content versus Thomson Reuters content, Thomson showed improvement in factuality or completeness in every instance; Thomson-1.0-Large NARROWLY outperformed GPT-5.4 and Claude Sonnet 5 on completeness and factuality for tax, legal and news content, and beat both by roughly 15 points in factuality on open-web content.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: the benchmark comparisons are company-reported, not independent; the overall margin is described as narrow (the ~15-point gap is specific to factuality on open-web content); and the open-weights Small model is not licensed for commercial use.
