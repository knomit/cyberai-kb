# Thomson Reuters built "Thomson," a Qwen-based domain LLM for law, tax, finance and news

Thomson Reuters launched Thomson (announced 2026-08-24, covered in The Batch 2026-09-04), a proprietary model family specializing in law, business, tax, finance, news and adjacent knowledge work, deployed first inside CoCounsel Legal (research, analysis and document drafting) and extended to other products at unspecified dates.

Specification: Thomson-1.0-Large is a mixture-of-experts transformer, 397B parameters with 17B active per token, text in up to 262,000 tokens, text out; business customers only, price undisclosed. It is built on Qwen3.5-397B-A17B, an instruction-tuned open-weight model. A 35B sibling, Thomson-1.0-Small, is built on Qwen3.6-35B and will be released as open weights on Hugging Face for academic and non-commercial use.

Method (what the company calls Continual Learning — full-weight mid-training plus fine-tuning, not adapter fine-tuning): Thomson Reuters and partner DatologyAI curated a 200-billion-token mid-training set from a 19-trillion-token candidate pool, in three roughly equal parts — curated proprietary documents (news, regulatory filings, case law, contracts), synthetic data pairs of successful professional tasks, and select general-capability material. The base was re-aligned to the company's style and values including journalistic objectivity, using direct preference optimization against a constitution that Thomson Reuters open-sourced for reuse. A final DPO round targeted accuracy and efficiency in agentic deep research, and group sequence policy optimization (GSPO) was used to manage context compaction and document caching so the model avoids overfilling context or making redundant API calls.

Reported performance: Thomson-1.0-Large narrowly beat GPT-5.4 and Claude Sonnet 5 on both completeness and factuality for tax, legal and news content, and led both by roughly 15 points on factuality for open web content; Thomson-1.0-Small similarly beat Gemma4-31B and Claude Haiku 4.5. These are vendor-reported figures.

Cost, with a discrepancy worth flagging: The Batch's newsletter reports $40 million in total training costs over three months, citing the press release. Other coverage of the same launch reports about $40 million over roughly two years covering people and compute, with economies bringing the final training run itself down to about $450,000. Treat $40M as the program-level figure and the sub-million final run as the training-compute figure; the three-month duration is the less-corroborated number.

Foreseeable misreading: CoCounsel remains a multi-model product — Thomson is used where the domain model has an advantage, with third-party frontier models retained for other tasks. The interesting part is the pipeline claim (data curation plus mid-training plus constitutional alignment) beating general-purpose frontier models on domain completeness, not a claim of general capability.
