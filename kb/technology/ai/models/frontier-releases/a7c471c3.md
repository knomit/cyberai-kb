# Claude Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1: activation probes for cyber routing, and only one of three stated fixes landed

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Mythos 5.1 on 2026-09-01 (covered in The Batch 2026-09-11). Fable and Mythos are two names for the same model, differing only in safeguards and fallbacks: anyone can use Fable 5.1, while Mythos 5.1 is restricted via Project Glasswing to U.S. cybersecurity or life-sciences organizations Anthropic selects.

Specification: text and images in (up to 1M tokens), text out (up to 128,000 tokens, 69 tokens/second). Knowledge cutoff June 2026. Reasoning always on across five levels (low, medium, high, xhigh, max; defaults to high), changeable mid-conversation without invalidating cache (beta); tool use with optional readable progress updates (beta); prompt caching; per-turn system messages (beta); statistical watermarking of generated text; and optional fallback to Claude Opus 4.8 or Opus 5 for prompts that trip bio or cyber classifiers. API $10/$0.25/$50 per million input/cached/output tokens — a 75% cut to cache reads, from $1 — with cache writes $12.50/$20 and batch $5/$25. Available via Claude.ai, the Claude API, AWS, Google Cloud and Microsoft Azure. Parameter count, architecture and specific training methods undisclosed.

Mechanism worth noting: a probe reads the model's *activations* rather than its text output and routes anything cybersecurity-related to an LLM classifier that judges whether to block; Anthropic says the probe and classifier complement each other, each catching what the other misses. Separately, Fable 5.1 can read earlier Claude models' reasoning but they cannot read its reasoning, and for accounts created on or after 2026-08-31 editing an earlier conversation turn invalidates it — Anthropic says this closes a documented reasoning-extraction technique. Unlike Fable 5, Fable 5.1 is permitted to do purely defensive cyber work such as finding vulnerabilities in human-readable source code, but not in compiled binaries; penetration testing, exploit writing and binary scanning still fall back to Opus 4.8 or Opus 5.

Independent results: ties GPT-6 Astra for first on Artificial Analysis' Intelligence Index v4.3 at max reasoning with fallback (53, $7.63 and 12.2 min/task, versus Astra's $3.26 and 8.2 min), ahead of Claude Opus 5 (51). Fallback routing produced about 4% of the output tokens the evaluator measured. It leads two of the index's hardest components — AA-Briefcase (1,662 Elo, $22.71, 70.2 min/task), which grades multi-week knowledge work projects with thousands of source files, and GDPval-AA v2 (1,764 Elo, $9.77, 60 turns/task) — plus SciCode (63.1%) and Humanity's Last Exam (59.1%). First on Vals AI's Vals Index (68.83%, $28.92, 76 min/task). It trails Astra on overall knowledge, computer use and multistep terminal tasks.

The caveat: Anthropic's published cybersecurity evaluations are all for Claude Mythos 5.1 *with safeguards off*, a configuration nearly no one can access. Anthropic says that configuration has the strongest cyber capability of any model it has shipped and sits in the lower of its Frontier Compliance Framework's two risk tiers, but close to the higher tier reserved for models capable of novel autonomous attacks.

Scorecard on Anthropic's own stated goals: it named three problems Fable 5.1 was built to fix — high cost per task, enterprise-disappointing data retention, and overly restrictive safeguards — and independent testing supports only partial progress. Cost per task rose roughly 20% over Fable 5 despite the 75% cache-read cut. The 30-day retention requirement still applies to everyone except eligible enterprise customers (ZDR now, Enterprise Frontier Safeguards later in fall 2026). Safeguards are loosened but not removed; Anthropic tells developers to expect roughly 60% fewer cyber interventions per session in Claude Code.
