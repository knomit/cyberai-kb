# GPT-5.6 Sol is 54% more token-efficient than Claude Fable 5 and roughly 2.25x LESS efficient than GPT-5.5 — and the 2026 price cuts largely missed the tier where consumption rose

Two records in this corpus measure GPT-5.6 Sol's token efficiency at its highest reasoning setting, in Codex workflows, and reach opposite-sounding results. Both can be true, and together they determine what a buyer actually pays.

THE TWO MEASUREMENTS.
- AGAINST A COMPETITOR: OpenAI reported that GPT-5.6 Sol with max reasoning OUTPERFORMS Claude Fable 5 on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index while using 54% FEWER OUTPUT TOKENS, following optimization of the agent harness, inference layer and API stack. The stated motivation is an enterprise backlash against runaway agent token costs.
- AGAINST ITS OWN PREDECESSOR: GPT-5.6 Sol xhigh uses MORE THAN TWICE as many tokens per session as GPT-5.5 xhigh in Codex workflows. At the same token price, roughly 2.25x the tokens implies about 2.25x the cost for a similar token mix — and Sol ADDS A CACHE-WRITE CHARGE that GPT-5.5 did not have.

RECONCILIATION. These are not in conflict; they use different baselines. A team switching from Claude Fable 5 sees a large saving. A team upgrading from GPT-5.5 sees costs roughly double, plus a new charge line. The same model, the same setting, opposite economics — decided by which model you came from. This is a third domain instance of the baseline-choice problem recorded at kb/technology/ai/evaluation/voice-models/voice-gains-measured-against-own-predecessor.md, and here the two baselines point in OPPOSITE directions rather than merely differing in magnitude.

WHY THE PRICE CUTS DO NOT SETTLE IT. The 2026 reductions were distributed inversely to where consumption rose:
- GPT-5.6 LUNA: cut 80%, to $0.20/$1.20 per 1M input/output tokens (from a $1/$6 list).
- GPT-5.6 TERRA: cut 20%, to $2/$12 (from $2.50/$15 list).
- GPT-5.6 SOL: cut more than 20% — but the reduction was STATED AS LASTING THREE MONTHS, against a list of $5/$0.50/$30 per 1M input/cached/output.
Sol is the tier whose per-session consumption roughly doubled and which gained a cache-write charge, and it received the smallest and the only explicitly temporary cut. A headline per-token price change therefore does not predict the change in what a Codex user pays; consumption per session and the charge structure move independently of the rate card.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN — load-bearing:
- It does NOT claim GPT-5.6 Sol is worse value than GPT-5.5. More tokens per session may buy proportionally more completed work; neither record reports task-completion rates, so cost per successful task — the variable that would settle it — is unavailable. See kb/technology/ai/economics/cost-shift-evidence-is-four-different-metrics.md.
- It does NOT claim the 54% figure is wrong. It is OpenAI's own reported result on a named third-party index, and the harness/inference/API optimizations it credits are real work.
- It does NOT claim the price cuts were insincere or ineffective. Luna's 80% cut is large and real, and Luna became ChatGPT's default free model with text-chat limits removed for Free and Go tiers.
- Source quality: the 2.25x consumption figure carries confidence 0.68 from a single newsletter report; the 54% figure is vendor-reported at 0.8; the list prices come from a 0.65-confidence record. None is independently audited.
- Scope: both measurements concern the HIGHEST reasoning setting (max / xhigh) in CODEX workflows. Neither generalizes to lower reasoning efforts or to non-agentic use.
