# Dan Klein / Scaled Cognition: super-reliability > superintelligence for enterprise AI — APT-1 model treats info as first-order objects

Dan Klein, CEO of Scaled Cognition (AI neolab) and CS professor at UC Berkeley, argues that enterprises need super-reliability more than superintelligence. Key claims: (1) Frontier scaling has stalled on accuracy — 'We've kind of consumed the most important bits' of training data; LLMs are not truth machines, optimized to produce outputs 'indistinguishable from the truth' rather than actual truth; (2) Dangerous enterprise hallucinations are subtle ones — a chatbot reporting a bank balance off by one digit causes bad transactions, not obvious errors like 'put rocks on pizza'; (3) APT-1 (Scaled Cognition's model): smaller than frontier models; treats prompted information as 'first-order objects' rather than doing next-token prediction; 'correctness properties are in the structure of the model itself'; more efficient. Quote: 'The problem is not that the models aren't intelligent enough. It's reliability. Superintelligence is going to have its place. But I think for the vast majority of things we want to do with AI, super-reliability is more important than superintelligence.'
