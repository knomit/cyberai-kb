# OpenAI's 2026 ChatGPT protections divide into opt-in security and inferred safety — the child-safety tier depends on an age estimator whose accuracy is nowhere recorded

OpenAI shipped several protective features to ChatGPT through 2026. They divide by HOW THEY ARE APPLIED, and the division does not track how consequential they are.

APPLIED BY USER REQUEST.
- Advanced Account Security (May 2026) is explicitly OPT-IN: stronger sign-in protections, tightened account recovery, reduced exposure from compromised sessions, more visibility into account activity, with YubiKey C Nano / C NFC hardware keys via a Yubico partnership plus FIDO-compliant keys and software passkeys. Described as 'designed for people at increased risk of digital attacks' — the user identifies as at-risk and turns it on.

APPLIED BY INFERENCE ABOUT THE USER.
- ChatGPT for Teens (18 Aug 2026): users who self-report ages 13–17 OR WHOSE AGE OPENAI'S ESTIMATION SYSTEM PLACES IN THAT RANGE are AUTOMATICALLY ENROLLED. Enrollment carries default protections that block romantic or sexualized roleplay, discourage emotional dependence, and prevent the model implying it has feelings or consciousness, plus linked parent accounts able to set quiet hours, manage settings and receive high-risk notifications including eating disorders.
- Within that product, homework reminders TRIGGER WHEN ChatGPT DETECTS a teen shortcutting an assignment, redirecting to Study Mode.
- Lockdown Mode (June 2026) is rolled out to ELIGIBLE personal accounts and 'targets higher-risk users' — eligibility and targeting determined by OpenAI, limiting tools capable of data exfiltration to reduce prompt-injection risk.
- The stated product direction points further this way: OpenAI's personalization lead describes the future as 'naturally adaptive' — requiring LITTLE CONFIGURATION while keeping transparency and user control.

THE LOAD-BEARING DEPENDENCY. The child-safety tier is gated on an age estimate. Whether a given user receives protections designed for minors is decided by a classifier, not by the user or a verified credential. NO ACCURACY FIGURE FOR THAT ESTIMATOR APPEARS ANYWHERE IN THIS CORPUS, nor any correction or appeal path for a user placed in the wrong band. The protection is exactly as good as the estimate, and the estimate is unmeasured in the record.

WHY THIS MATTERS BEYOND THE PRODUCT. OpenAI endorsed the Kids Online Safety Act, with Chief Global Affairs Officer Chris Lehane saying 'We can't repeat the mistakes made during the rise of social media.' KOSA-style regimes turn on correctly identifying who is a minor, so the estimator is not only a product component but the mechanism by which a compliance obligation would be discharged. Separately, ChatGPT now ingests connected Apple Health data and supported medical records for US adults — raising the cost of a user being placed in the wrong band in either direction.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN — load-bearing:
- It does NOT report any estimation failure. No source here records a misclassification, a complaint, or an error rate. This identifies an untested dependency, not a documented defect.
- It does NOT argue defaults-on protection is wrong. Opt-in safety protects only users who already know they need it, which is precisely the wrong distribution for minors; automatic application is a defensible design choice and arguably the responsible one.
- It does NOT claim OpenAI conceals the mechanism — the automatic-enrollment behaviour is stated plainly in the product description, and self-reported age is accepted alongside estimation.
- Sources vary in quality: the teen product record carries two sources at 0.8, Lockdown Mode 0.65 from a single trade report, and the APA partnership 0.6. The 'naturally adaptive' direction is one product manager's stated intent in an interview, not a shipped feature.
- The opt-in/inferred split is a reading of these eight records together; no source frames the features this way, and OpenAI has not stated a policy that security is opt-in while safety is inferred.
