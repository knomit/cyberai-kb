# OpenAI's Atlas browser was retired, and two higher-confidence facts still treat it as a load-bearing pillar of the four-surface superapp claim

STALENESS CORRECTION with named downstream consequences. Read this before citing any claim about OpenAI covering four deployment surfaces.

THE SUPERSEDING FACT. Per TLDR AI citing TechCrunch, OpenAI SHUT DOWN its Atlas browser and shifted its agentic browsing features into ChatGPT's desktop app. Atlas no longer exists as a distinct product surface.

WHAT THIS UNDERCUTS, SPECIFICALLY.
1. kb/technology/ai/products/platform/synthesis/7fc15ed0.md argues OpenAI simultaneously expanded across FOUR distinct deployment contexts in a single product cycle — mobile (Codex mobile management), voice/real-time (GPT-Realtime-2), enterprise/regulated (HIPAA-compliant Codex), and BROWSER ('Atlas, ongoing… completes the surface coverage'). It applies a multi-context substrate methodology under which simultaneous expansion into 4+ contexts is a monopoly indicator, and concludes 'no rival has simultaneously addressed voice + mobile + enterprise + browser in a single cycle.' One of the four pillars has since been withdrawn. The three surviving contexts may still support a weaker claim, but the specific four-context argument and the competitive comparison built on it no longer stand as written.
2. kb/technology/ai/products/openai/dc81a874.md describes the superapp as merging THREE desktop products — ChatGPT desktop, the Atlas web browser, and Codex — quoting Greg Brockman that 'over the next couple months, we should have shipped the complete vision.' The merge target list is now two products plus an absorbed feature set.

THE CONFIDENCE PROBLEM — LOAD-BEARING, AND THE REASON NOTHING IS RETRACTED HERE. The retirement record carries confidence 0.6 with ZERO recorded sources, sourced to a newsletter citing TechCrunch. The two records it undercuts carry 0.85 and 0.95. So the corpus's weakest-sourced fact contradicts two of its better-sourced ones.

This is the case where a naive date-ordered supersede rule fails: the later fact is not automatically the more reliable one, and retracting a 0.85-confidence synthesis on the strength of a 0.6-confidence single-line report would trade a dated claim for an under-evidenced one. The correct handling is to record the conflict and let a better-sourced observation resolve it, not to silently retire either side.

WHAT WOULD RESOLVE IT: any OpenAI product announcement, changelog, or support-page notice confirming Atlas's discontinuation date; or conversely any continued Atlas release activity, which would falsify the retirement report. Either is cheap to check and neither is present in this corpus.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN:
- It does NOT claim OpenAI abandoned agentic browsing. The source says the FEATURES moved into ChatGPT's desktop app — the capability persists, the separate surface does not. A claim about capability coverage may survive; a claim about the NUMBER OF DISTINCT SURFACES does not.
- It does NOT impugn the other three contexts in the four-surface argument. Codex mobile management, GPT-Realtime-2, and HIPAA-compliant Codex for eligible ChatGPT Enterprise workspaces are separately recorded and unaffected.
- It does NOT establish that the retirement report is correct — only that it is on the record, is in direct conflict with two live claims, and that citing any of the three without the others misleads.
