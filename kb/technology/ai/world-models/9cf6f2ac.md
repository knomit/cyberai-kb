# 2026: world models emerge as a heavily funded frontier alongside compute constraints

By mid-2026, 'world models' (AI that understands physical space and physics) became a major frontier drawing billions in capital and reshaping company strategy, per The Deep View (July 3, 2026). Startups raised large rounds (Yann LeCun's AMI Labs $1B seed at $3.5B; Fei-Fei Li's World Labs $1B at $5B; Runway $315M; Luma $900M; General Intuition $320M), and gaming firms pivoted in (Niantic sold games to Scopely for $3.5B and spun out Niantic Spatial's 'Large Geospatial Model'). Approaches diverge across predictive video (Nvidia Cosmos, Google DeepMind Genie), 3D spatial representation (World Labs Marble), simulation (Waymo, Tesla, Figure AI), and multimodal (Luma), with synthetic-data generation for robotics/self-driving the leading commercial use. All are gated by the same compute and data scarcity squeezing the whole industry — a crunch severe enough that OpenAI shut down its Sora video platform to reallocate compute to flagship models. Figures like LeCun argue language models alone cannot reach AGI without grounding in the physical world.
