# By mid-2026 world models became a heavily funded frontier pursued along four divergent technical approaches, all gated by the same compute and data scarcity

The Deep View (July 3, 2026) reported that 'world models' — AI that understands physical space and physics — had become a major frontier drawing billions in capital and reshaping company strategy by mid-2026.

FUNDING AND STRATEGIC REPOSITIONING. Startups raised large rounds: Yann LeCun's AMI Labs $1B seed at a $3.5B valuation; Fei-Fei Li's World Labs $1B at $5B; Runway $315M; Luma $900M; General Intuition $320M. Gaming firms pivoted in: Niantic sold its games business to Scopely for $3.5B and spun out Niantic Spatial around a 'Large Geospatial Model'.

FOUR DIVERGENT APPROACHES. The same survey found major players pursuing technically distinct paths rather than converging on one:
- Predictive video models — Nvidia's Cosmos; Google DeepMind's Genie / Project Genie, powered by Gemini and Genie 3; Runway's interactive-video GWM-1.
- 3D spatial-representation models — World Labs' 'Marble'.
- Simulation models for robotics and self-driving — Tesla, the Waymo World Model, Figure AI's Helix.
- Multimodal approaches — Luma.

SHARED CONSTRAINT. All four approaches face amplified compute and data constraints — the same crunch squeezing the whole industry, severe enough that OpenAI shut down its Sora video platform to reallocate compute to flagship models. Their most commercially significant current use is generating synthetic data to train robotics and self-driving systems.

ARGUMENT MOTIVATING THE FIELD. Figures like LeCun argue language models alone cannot reach AGI without grounding in the physical world.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: this is a single survey article's snapshot as of July 3, 2026, not an independently verified market analysis; the valuations and round sizes are as-reported. The four-way taxonomy is the surveyor's grouping, and companies listed under one approach may pursue others. Nothing here establishes that any approach is working — commercial traction is confined to synthetic-data generation, and no capability results are cited.
