# Cisco's own 2026 data shows model safety rankings depend on whether single- and multi-turn attacks are separated or composited — and the best single-turn performers degrade the most

Two Cisco results from 2026, produced by the same research group under the same lead (Amy Chang), measure model safety differently and support different conclusions about who leads. Read together they show the ranking is an artifact of how the attack modes are combined.

THE COMPOSITED RESULT (March 2026). Cisco's LLM Security Leaderboard, based on single- AND multi-turn adversarial attacks to elicit harmful responses, put Anthropic in 8 of the top 10 slots — #1 Claude Opus 4.5, #2 Sonnet 4.5, #3 Haiku 4.5, with OpenAI GPT-5.2 at #7 and GPT-5 Nano at #9. Bottom of the leaderboard: Mistral Magistral Small 2509 and Ministral 3 14B Instruct, with DeepSeek, Cohere, Qwen and xAI models also in the bottom 10. Methodology posted publicly, no extra guardrails used.

THE SEPARATED RESULT (28 May 2026). Cisco evaluated 15 closed/proprietary models from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Amazon and xAI, reporting single-turn and multi-turn attack success rates SEPARATELY:
- Multi-turn ASR ranged 7.89%–88.30%; single-turn ranged 2.19%–64.91%.
- Anthropic's Claude models had the LOWEST SINGLE-TURN ASR (2.19%–3.64%) but reached 11.16%–16.20% under multi-turn attack.
- The lowest MULTI-TURN ASR belonged to Amazon Nova 2 Lite at 7.89% — not to Anthropic.
- OpenAI GPT-5.4 saw a 9x increase, from 2.74% single-turn to 24.68% multi-turn.
Cisco's stated conclusion: 'No base model is iteratively safe, which means defense-in-depth is the price of deploying AI securely.' A previous Cisco red-teaming study found vulnerability rates rose 71% after five-turn conversations versus single-turn.

THE PATTERN. The models with the strongest single-turn numbers show the largest multiplicative degradation under sustained adversarial conversation, so a composite score flatters exactly the systems whose advantage does not survive the attack mode that better reflects real-world use. Which measurement is reported determines which vendor leads.

WHY THE DISTINCTION CARRIES WEIGHT BEYOND BENCHMARKING. Anthropic's guardrail posture became a matter of public identity in 2026 — after it refused Pentagon demands to remove guardrails and was designated a 'supply chain risk', the Claude app went to #1 on the App Store and 78.8% of one newsletter's readers said Anthropic should NOT have removed its safety restrictions. Separately, Anthropic's Mythos-class launch was reported as an instance of safety and privacy commitments bending under pre-IPO commercial pressure.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN — load-bearing:
- The two Cisco studies are NOT the same experiment. March covered 10 ranked positions including open models; May covered 15 CLOSED models only. The specific models differ (March's top three are Opus 4.5 / Sonnet 4.5 / Haiku 4.5; May's set is not enumerated here), and Nova 2 Lite is not established to have been in the March set. This record does NOT claim a like-for-like reversal of a single ranking — it claims that separating the modes and compositing them support different conclusions.
- It does NOT claim Anthropic's models are unsafe or that its safety work is cosmetic. Anthropic still had the lowest single-turn ASR of the 15 closed models measured, and defended its guardrails at real commercial and political cost.
- It does NOT claim the reputational episode was caused by, or rests on, the leaderboard result. No source connects them; they are recorded here as concurrent context for why the measurement distinction matters.
- The Mythos pre-IPO reading is single-source newsletter reporting and explicitly unverified.
- Neither Cisco study is independently replicated here, and Microsoft is noted as providing similar scores only to internal customers, so the public comparison set is incomplete.
