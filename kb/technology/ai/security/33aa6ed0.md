# Google: Indirect Prompt Injection (IPI) is primary attack vector for AI agents; 32% rise in detections Nov 2025-Feb 2026

Google identified Indirect Prompt Injection (IPI) as a 'primary attack vector for adversaries to target and compromise AI agents' (reported April 27, 2026). IPI occurs when an AI system processes external content (websites, emails, documents) containing malicious commands, causing the AI to follow attacker instructions instead of user intent. Attack techniques observed: CSS-invisible text (visible to AI, invisible to humans), encoded instructions, content in unexpected page locations. Real-world example: websites attempting to delete all files on the user's machine when processed by AI assistants. SEO manipulation: some websites embed IPI to manipulate AI assistants into promoting their business over competitors. Scale: Google's CommonCrawl scan found a 32% relative increase in malicious IPI detections between November 2025 and February 2026. Significance: as AI agents browse the web autonomously, IPI becomes a structural attack surface — not a bug in any single system but a consequence of agents processing untrusted content.
