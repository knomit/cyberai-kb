# Benchmark indices are now revised faster than models ship, so "ranked first" needs an index version

A mental model surfaced by a concrete week, documented in The Batch 2026-09-11. Between 2026-09-01 and 2026-09-07 the leaderboard picture changed three times without any model changing: Claude Fable 5.1 shipped 2026-09-01; GPT-6 Astra shipped 2026-09-03 scoring two points below it on Artificial Analysis' Intelligence Index; on 2026-09-04 Artificial Analysis shipped Intelligence Index v4.2, retiring GPQA-Diamond as saturated, adding two harder tests, and doubling the share scored on private tests to 40% (which it says reduces labs' ability to game evaluations), calling it an expedited interim update because the frontier was moving too fast to wait for the completed v5; on 2026-09-07 it upgraded again, moving Terminal-Bench to v4 and adding AutomationBench-AA in place of a banking test. After those changes Fable 5.1 and Astra tied for first.

The consequence to act on: a claim of the form "model X ranks first on index Y" is undated and therefore close to meaningless — record the index version and date alongside the score, and expect the component tests that produced a ranking to have been swapped by the time you rely on it. The same applies within a single benchmark across harnesses (see GPT-6 Astra's 62.7% vs 99.9% on ARC-AGI-3 depending on harness).

Foreseeable misreading: this is not a claim that benchmarks are gamed or worthless — the v4.2 change explicitly increased the private-test share to make gaming harder, and saturation retirement is the system working. The point is that the measuring instrument now has a shorter shelf life than the thing it measures, so scores are only comparable when the index version matches.
