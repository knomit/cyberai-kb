# Two independent 2026 critiques — one enterprise, one theoretical — reject static capability scores for properties that only exist over time

In 2026 two arguments arrived from unrelated directions, each rejecting the prevailing way AI capability is scored, and each proposing a replacement that can only be measured across time rather than at a point. Neither cites the other.

THE ENTERPRISE CRITIQUE — duration of autonomous work. Z.ai's GLM-5.1 was explicitly designed for 8-hour autonomous coding sessions, cycling through planning, execution, evaluation and strategy-revision loops hundreds of times, and led SWE-Bench Pro at 58.4% as an open-weights model at $1.40/M tokens; OpenAI's GPT-5.5 headlined 'better long-horizon task handling' and 'multi-step scientific workflows' as its primary improvements. The argument for replacing accuracy scores is commercial verifiability: sustained coherence over time requires planning, self-correction, state management and failure recovery — capabilities qualitatively different from those that raise single-turn scores — and, decisively, 'enterprise buyers can measure whether their AI can complete an 8-hour coding task, but they cannot independently verify what 57 on an AI Intelligence Index means for their workload.'

THE THEORETICAL CRITIQUE — speed and breadth of adaptation. In a March 2026 paper, Yann LeCun and co-authors challenged AGI as a goalpost on the grounds that no consensus definition exists in industry or academia and that humans themselves are not generalized. Their proposed replacement, Superhuman Adaptable Intelligence (SAI), measures how QUICKLY a model adapts to new tasks and the RANGE of tasks it can learn, rather than requiring one model that can do everything. Two supporting arguments: generality is not a requirement for intelligence to be extremely useful, and humans are specialized yet capable of quickly learning a wide range of things. This sets LeCun directly against Sam Altman (AGI before 2030 via LLMs) and Dario Amodei.

THE SHARED STRUCTURE. Both replace a STATIC SCALAR — a leaderboard number, a threshold called AGI — with a property that has no value at an instant. You cannot read autonomous-work duration or adaptation speed off a single forward pass; both require watching a system operate. That is the agreement, and it is structural rather than substantive: the two proposals measure DIFFERENT time-extended quantities and are not interchangeable.

ADJACENT, NOT A THIRD PILLAR: Mistral's Forge (18 March 2026) pitches customers on building models from their own proprietary data — codebases, internal documentation, compliance policies, operational processes — with the stated differentiator that an open-source foundation avoids vendor lock-in and MODEL DEPRECATION RISK, a risk made concrete by the GPT-4o retirement backlash. That is also a move away from 'pick the highest-scoring model', but toward fit-to-your-data rather than toward a temporal metric, so it belongs beside this claim rather than inside it.

RELATED RECORDS, distinct in kind: this corpus separately documents three ways EXISTING measures get misread — kb/technology/ai/economics/cost-shift-evidence-is-four-different-metrics.md (unit conflation), kb/technology/ai/security/benchmarks/single-turn-ranking-inverts-under-multi-turn.md (aggregation choice), and kb/technology/ai/evaluation/voice-models/voice-gains-measured-against-own-predecessor.md (baseline choice). Those concern misreading instruments that exist. This record concerns proposals to replace the instrument.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN:
- It does NOT claim either replacement has been adopted. No standardized agentic-duration evaluation framework exists in this corpus, and SAI is a proposal in one paper. A separate hypothesis tracking whether such a framework appears by Q2 2027 remains open and is deliberately not treated as evidence here.
- It does NOT claim the two critiques support each other's substance. LeCun's argument is about what intelligence IS; the duration argument is about what enterprises can VERIFY. They agree only that the current scalar is the wrong object.
- It does NOT claim benchmark scores are worthless. GLM-5.1's SWE-Bench Pro lead is cited approvingly in the very record arguing benchmarks are being displaced.
- Both source records are single-sourced, and the duration argument's forward-looking claim about 'the next 12–18 months' is projection, not observation.
