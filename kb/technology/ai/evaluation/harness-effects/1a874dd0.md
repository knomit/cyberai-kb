# The harness is a coupled component, not a neutral wrapper — so measurements, RL runs and distillation artifacts that do not pin it are underspecified

Five independently-sourced 2026 records, spanning four different activities that the review pipeline never clustered together, all turn on the same mechanism: the scaffolding around a model (harness, runner, grader, split) is part of the system under test, not a transparent wrapper around it. Consequences show up wherever a number or an artifact is carried from one harness to another.

IN MEASUREMENT. GPT-6 Astra scored 62.7% on ARC-AGI-3 Semi-Private under the standard provider-neutral harness and 99.9% under a provider-supplied adapter harness — same weights, two numbers. Separately, the September 2026 'two MMLU scores' analysis showed that two runs sharing provider, model family, metric identifier, unit AND benchmark name can still diverge, because runners, graders and dataset splits are not pinned by the benchmark label. That record states the operational rule directly: a benchmark name identifies a DATASET FAMILY, not a measurement procedure, so comparing two reported numbers from different sources is invalid unless harness, grader and split are also matched.

IN TRAINING AND DISTILLATION — this is the load-bearing addition, because it supplies a mechanism rather than another instance. Salesforce research (arXiv 2609.09134) on co-evolving agents and harnesses found that directly training smaller models on expert agent trajectories can DEGRADE performance once the harness has already been optimized: trajectories collected under one harness encode behaviors a better harness already handles differently, so distillation can teach the model to fight its own scaffolding. Harness improvements and model fine-tuning are therefore NOT independent additive gains. Practical consequence stated in that record: measure distillation gains against the CURRENT harness, not the one that generated the data.

IN RL POST-TRAINING. Mercor and SkyRL, post-training Qwen3.5-397B-A17B on 1,928 expert knowledge-work tasks for a 70% lift in APEX-Agents Pass@1, argue that at frontier scale robust environments, exact token accounting, asynchronous RL and harness design matter as much as the choice of RL algorithm.

WHY THE COUPLING RESULT MATTERS MOST: the measurement cases establish that harness choice moves numbers. The Salesforce result explains why that is not merely a reporting-hygiene problem — if harness and model are coupled, then gains measured under one configuration do not transfer, and a component swapped in isolation can make the system worse. It predicts the measurement discrepancies rather than sitting beside them.

RELATED, AND DELIBERATELY SEPARATE: kb/technology/ai/agents/593328d2.md records that harness-layer work (routing, RL scaffolding) is WHERE MEASURED GAINS ARE BEING REPORTED, with its own caveat that this is not evidence harness work generally outperforms model scaling. That is a claim about where improvement is currently being found. THIS fact is a claim about COUPLING — that harness and model are not separable, so a number or artifact produced under one does not carry to another. The two are complementary and must not be collapsed: one could be true without the other.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN. No source here decomposes how much of observed performance is attributable to harness versus weights, so this must NOT be compressed into 'the harness matters more than the model' — that is a share claim and nothing here measures a share. The evidence base is also uneven and should not be quoted as uniform: the Salesforce and Mercor/SkyRL records each rest on a single source, Mercor/SkyRL's is the practitioners' own write-up, and Andrew Ng's related position that driving coding agents is a top-level AI engineering skill is explicitly an argument and skills taxonomy drawn from interviews, NOT a measurement. The ARC-AGI-3 99.9% figure is the adapter-harness number; quoting it without the harness caveat is the common misreading.
