# Frontier capability claims are routinely benchmarked against a baseline only the claimant can reproduce

Mechanism all members instantiate: a capability claim is published by the party that benefits from it, measured against a baseline that party chose and that no outsider can reproduce, so the number describes a private comparison rather than a position among alternatives.

Across model, robotics, and infrastructure announcements, the headline figure is generated under conditions the vendor controls: a benchmark the vendor ran against a competitor it selected, a demonstration the vendor staged in an environment set it chose, or a speedup measured against the vendor's own prior system. Each is internally honest and externally uncheckable.

Operational consequence for a reader: treat the direction of such a claim as informative and its magnitude as unranked. A multiple reported against a vendor's own prior stack says nothing about that vendor's standing versus others; a staged demonstration without published success rates evidences that a capability transferred at all, not how reliably it transfers; a vendor-run head-to-head that is split across sub-metrics must not be compressed into a blanket claim of superiority over the named competitor.

What this does NOT mean: self-referential measurement is not evidence the claim is false, and it is not a reason to discount vendor announcements as a class. It is a reason to refuse the specific inference these numbers invite — cross-vendor ranking — while accepting the weaker claim each actually supports.
