# Expert re-grading shows leading physics benchmarks understate frontier models by 20-30 points

A September 2026 arXiv paper (2609.13009) had physics faculty and graduate researchers audit six widely used physics benchmarks, reviewing problem statements, reference solutions and model responses to separate genuine model errors from grader bugs, incorrect reference solutions, and ambiguous or underspecified questions. Most cases initially scored as incorrect turned out to be benchmarking defects rather than reasoning failures. After correcting or excluding flawed items, GPT-5.6-Sol's mean@4 rose from 47.3% to 78.7% on HLE-Physics and from 61.0% to 87.2% on CMT-Benchmark, and its corrected pass@4 reached 94.4% on the 54 retained CritPt challenges; UGPhysics, PRISM-Physics and PHYBench scores also rose substantially. Note the corrected scores are computed on the retained subsets after expert review, not the full original benchmarks. The authors conclude these closed-ended benchmarks are near saturation and that harder expert-validated evaluations are needed.
