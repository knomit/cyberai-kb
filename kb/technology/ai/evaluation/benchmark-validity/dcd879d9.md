# A reported benchmark score is a property of the whole measurement apparatus, and the apparatus fails in two independent directions — harness choice inflates, defective answer keys depress

EXTENDS kb/technology/ai/evaluation/harness-effects/1a874dd0.md, which establishes that the scaffolding around a model — harness, runner, grader, split — is part of the system under test rather than a transparent wrapper, so a benchmark name identifies a DATASET FAMILY and not a measurement procedure. This record adds a second, independent failure channel in the same apparatus, running in the opposite direction.

CHANNEL ONE — THE HARNESS MOVES THE NUMBER UP OR DOWN. GPT-6 Astra scored 62.7% on ARC-AGI-3 Semi-Private under the standard provider-neutral harness and 99.9% under a provider-supplied adapter harness: same weights, two numbers. The 'two MMLU scores' analysis showed two runs can share provider, model family, metric identifier, unit AND benchmark name and still diverge, because runners, graders and splits are not pinned by the label.

CHANNEL TWO — THE REFERENCE MATERIAL ITSELF IS WRONG, AND IT DEPRESSES. A September 2026 arXiv paper (2609.13009) had physics faculty and graduate researchers audit six widely used physics benchmarks, separating genuine model errors from grader bugs, INCORRECT REFERENCE SOLUTIONS, and ambiguous or underspecified questions. Most cases initially scored incorrect were benchmarking defects, not reasoning failures. After correcting or excluding flawed items, GPT-5.6-Sol's mean@4 rose from 47.3% to 78.7% on HLE-Physics and from 61.0% to 87.2% on CMT-Benchmark, and its corrected pass@4 reached 94.4% on the 54 retained CritPt challenges.

WHY TWO CHANNELS MATTER JOINTLY: a low score is not evidence of a model limitation and a high score is not evidence of a capability, until the apparatus is specified in BOTH directions. The harness result says the number can be inflated by favourable scaffolding; the physics result says the number can be deflated by a broken answer key. An analyst who audits only one channel will systematically misread the other, and the two corrections do not cancel — they are independent defects in the same instrument.

CARRIED CAVEATS, ALL STILL BINDING:
- The physics corrected scores are computed on the RETAINED SUBSETS after expert review, not the full original benchmarks, so they are not directly comparable to the originally published figures.
- Nothing here decomposes how much of observed performance is attributable to harness versus weights. This must NOT be compressed into 'the harness matters more than the model' — that is a share claim and nothing here measures a share.
- The ARC-AGI-3 99.9% figure is the ADAPTER-HARNESS number; quoting it without the harness caveat is the common misreading.
- The evidence base is uneven and should not be quoted as uniform: the Salesforce coupling result and the Mercor/SkyRL RL result each rest on a single source, and Mercor/SkyRL's is the practitioners' own write-up.
- The physics paper's own conclusion is that these closed-ended benchmarks are NEAR SATURATION and that harder expert-validated evaluations are needed — which is a claim about these benchmarks, not about physics reasoning in general.
