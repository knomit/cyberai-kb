# Google's two 2026 AI deployments were evaluated on opposite standards: the mammography system against human readers and still gated, AI Overviews on a disputed absolute score and already at 2 billion users

This corpus records two Google AI deployments assessed in fundamentally different ways. Set side by side, the deployment with the more rigorous evaluation is the one that has not shipped, and the one with the contested measure is the one operating at planetary scale.

THE COMPARATIVELY EVALUATED DEPLOYMENT — mammography, still gated.
Google's breast-cancer detection system was measured AGAINST THE INCUMBENT HUMAN PROCESS under real UK NHS conditions:
- Retrospective study, 116,000 mammograms across 5 hospitals, ages 50–70: AI sensitivity 0.541 versus the first human reader's 0.437 (significantly better); AI specificity 0.943 versus 0.952 (lower but statistically equivalent). AI identified 25% of cancers humans initially missed that became apparent up to 3 years later.
- The cost side was counted too: simulating replacement of the second human reader gave a ~40% reduction in human effort DESPITE sending 1,800 more cases (absolute +4pp) to arbitration panels.
- Live test, 9,250 fresh scans across 12 NHS clinics: AI median 17.7 minutes from screen to interpretation versus more than 2 days for a human first read. Patients were diagnosed normally — the AI ran parallel and blind and did not affect care. Some doctors reported distrust in AI output.
The system REMAINS UNDER REGULATORY REVIEW for deployment.

THE ABSOLUTELY EVALUATED DEPLOYMENT — AI Overviews, already universal.
An April 2026 analysis (commissioned by The New York Times from Oumi, also covered by Ars Technica) found AI Overviews accurate about 90–91% of the time across 4,326 searches on OpenAI's SimpleQA benchmark — wrong roughly 9–10% of the time. NO HUMAN OR PRIOR-SYSTEM BASELINE IS OFFERED, and Google disputed the methodology, arguing SimpleQA contains errors and that using one AI to grade another is flawed. Meanwhile AI Overviews surpassed 2 BILLION MONTHLY USERS against 5+ trillion annual searches, which the source notes implies millions of incorrect responses.

THE ASYMMETRY IS EXPLAINED BY REGULATORY REGIME, NOT BY CHOICE OF RIGOUR. A breast-cancer screening system is a regulated medical device and must be measured against standard of care; a search feature is neither. That is exactly why the comparison is worth recording: it locates where the accountability gap sits rather than attributing it to negligence. Google is simultaneously capable of the NHS-grade evaluation and not required to perform its equivalent on the product with a billion times the reach.

USER RESPONSE WHERE THE BASELINE IS ABSENT. DuckDuckGo installations jumped 30% as users pushed back against the AI Search overhaul, which the source reads as resistance to AI-integrated search imposed without opt-out. In the clinical setting the analogous scepticism is recorded too — some doctors reported distrust — but it operates inside a review process rather than through substitution.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN — load-bearing:
- TWO DEPLOYMENTS IS NOT A PATTERN. This is a contrast between two records, not evidence about Google's evaluation practice generally, and it does not claim any other Google AI product is under- or over-evaluated.
- It does NOT claim AI Overviews is inaccurate. 90–91% is the disputed figure's own number, Google's methodological objections are recorded and not adjudicated here, and no better measurement exists in this corpus.
- It does NOT claim the mammography results establish clinical superiority. Specificity was LOWER than human (0.943 vs 0.952, described as statistically equivalent, not better), the effort saving came WITH an increase in arbitration referrals, and the system was not used to diagnose any patient in the live test.
- It does NOT claim regulation is the right or wrong answer for search. The observation is that the two regimes differ and the evaluation rigour tracks the regime.
