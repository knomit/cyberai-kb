# LLMs excel at math because the math literature is almost entirely correct, not because math is verifiable

The argument: LLMs are good at judging mathematical arguments because they remain mostly powered by IMITATIVE learning from pretraining data rather than by reinforcement learning, and almost everything in the published math literature is correct. Given that clean corpus, only a small amount of curated mid-training data and/or RL is needed to hone metacognitive strategies. In many other fields the research literature mixes true and valuable information into a sea of falsehoods and confused ideas, and models trained on it produce correspondingly confused output with occasional insights.

The operational consequence: expected model reliability in a domain should be predicted from the CORRECTNESS RATE OF THAT DOMAIN'S LITERATURE, not from whether answers in the domain are machine-verifiable. This directly contradicts the common 'verifiability explains math and coding performance' account — do not compress this fact into that one. It is a hypothesis argued from observation, not an experimentally isolated result.
