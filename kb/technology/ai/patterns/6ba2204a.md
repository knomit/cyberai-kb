# Mid-2026 AI progress is concentrating in post-training and scaffolding rather than larger base models

Several August 2026 releases share a structure: the reported gain comes from what surrounds or follows the base model, not from a bigger base model.

- Z.ai states the only change in GLM-5.3 versus GLM-5.2 is the amount of post-training — more environments, more diverse tasks, more compute — yielding over 50% improvement on its in-house coding benchmark plus 'emergent' cyber capabilities including improved vulnerability discovery. Analyst commentary notes Z.ai's time-to-release is days rather than the months typical of OpenAI and Anthropic.
- DeepSeek shipped Harness v0.1, an open-source agent harness (MIT license, developer preview), alongside DeepSeek-V4-Pro — releasing the scaffold as a product in its own right and positioning against Claude Code.
- Google introduced Custom Agents in Antigravity 2.0 and the Antigravity CLI: file-based role configurations with scoped instructions, tools and constraints, aimed at keeping active context clean and minimizing token overhead. They complement rather than replace skills and dynamic subagents.
- Alibaba's Qwen-3.8 is a deliberately lightweight 27B Apache-2.0 vision-capable model claimed to beat previous generations on real-world coding and office workflows — capability at small scale.

A cost-side caveat sits alongside this: PointFive ran the same prompt across 2,908 Claude Code sessions with and without token-reduction tooling and found token compression increased costs by nearly 50% in some cases, so scaffolding that reduces token counts does not automatically reduce spend.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: this does not establish that pretraining scale has stopped mattering, and the GLM-5.3 post-training claim is Z.ai's own characterization of its release measured on its own in-house benchmark, not independently verified. The Qwen-3.8 assessment includes an independent reviewer (Simon Willison) rating it excellent but noting it defaults to wildly overthinking problems.
