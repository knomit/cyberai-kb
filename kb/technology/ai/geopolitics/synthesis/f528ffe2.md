# AI Geopolitical Bifurcation: US and China Simultaneously Assert Sovereign Control Over AI, Fracturing Global Market (May 2026)

In May 2026, the US and China simultaneously enacted sovereign control mechanisms over AI that are structurally incompatible with a unified global AI market:

**US side**:
- TRAINS framework imposes pre-deployment government evaluation on AI models for national security risks — creating a government checkpoint between AI development and public access
- White House banned Claude from military use and blocked Anthropic's Mythos expansion, asserting authority over an AI lab's commercial distribution decisions
- The implicit principle: the U.S. government can unilaterally decide which AI capabilities reach which organizations, even when those organizations are legitimate commercial partners

**China side**:
- NDRC blocked Meta's $2.5B acquisition of Manus, asserting authority over companies whose technology, talent, or operations originated in China — regardless of where they are incorporated
- The 'Singapore strategy' (incorporating outside China to attract Western capital and bypass Beijing's reach) has been explicitly invalidated
- Chinese AI startups have cancelled plans to relocate abroad or raise from U.S./European sources
- The implicit principle: China asserts extraterritorial authority over any strategically important AI technology that originated within its borders

**The bifurcation pattern**: Both sides are now asserting that AI is a strategic national asset, not a commercial product subject only to market rules. This is happening simultaneously and without coordination. The net effect is: any AI company that touches both markets faces binary compliance choices (serve US government requirements OR maintain operations in/from China, but increasingly not both). The Singapore strategy was the last major attempt to straddle both sides — its death signals that AI companies must now choose.

**Implication**: The global AI market is fracturing into at minimum two sovereign domains faster than the industry has modeled. Companies that assumed a unified global market should model a bifurcated scenario as the base case by 2027.
