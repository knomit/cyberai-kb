# China Blocks Meta-Manus $2.5B Acquisition, Kills 'Singapore Strategy' for Chinese AI Startups

In May 2026, China's National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC) blocked Meta's proposed acquisition of Manus, a Singapore-based AI agent startup founded in China by Butterfly Effect. The deal was worth as much as $2.5 billion.

Manus background: Launched in early 2025 as an invitation-only beta, Manus is a general-purpose agent completing long-running tasks with minimal user input. By end of 2025, it had $100M in annual recurring revenue growing at 20% monthly. The company had raised from major Chinese investors and Silicon Valley VCs, then relocated to Singapore in July 2025. Meta announced the acquisition in December 2025 and began integrating Manus technology into Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp.

NDRC action: In January 2026, NDRC opened a security review citing concerns over data transfers and foreign ownership. In April 2026, it said it would examine foreign investment in domestic AI companies much more closely, particularly to curb U.S. investment in China-developed technology. It then formally blocked the deal.

Chilling effect: The blocking of the Meta-Manus deal has ended the 'Singapore strategy' — the approach where Chinese AI founders incorporated in Singapore to attract Western capital while operating beyond Beijing's reach. Startups have cancelled plans to move abroad, pursue acquisitions, or raise from U.S. and European sources. Beijing is asserting authority over any strategically important company whose technology, talent, or operations originated in China, regardless of incorporation location.

Geopolitical context: This comes amid broader China-U.S. AI competition, U.S. semiconductor export controls (escalating since 2022), and planned bilateral discussions on AI governance.
