# The confidence-accuracy decoupling runs as a three-step chain: post-training rewards certainty, certainty language drives ~90% user trust, and the result is unexamined AI input to critical-infrastructure decisions

MERGED from three separately-filed records that each held ONE LINK of a single causal chain. Filed apart, none of them stated the chain; each read as an isolated observation about overconfidence. All three findings and their attributions are preserved.

## LINK 1 — WHERE THE OVERCONFIDENCE COMES FROM
Apple Research (cited by The Deep View, July 2026) identified that popular POST-TRAINING METHODS — INSTRUCTION TUNING AND RLHF — reward responses that sound HELPFUL AND CONFIDENT, incentivizing models to EXPRESS CERTAINTY EVEN WHEN GUESSING. This contributes to confidently-stated hallucinations. The mechanism is a reward-proxy problem: the training signal scores how an answer SOUNDS, not whether it is right.

## LINK 2 — WHAT CONFIDENT LANGUAGE DOES TO USERS
Research by Maarten Sap and colleagues (cited by The Deep View, July 2026) found that when AI responses used epistemic 'STRENGTHENERS' (language expressing certainty), NEARLY 90% OF USERS TRUSTED THE ANSWER; when 'WEAKENERS' (uncertainty language) were used, ~90% CHOSE TO VERIFY IT THEMSELVES. The wording alone moves verification behaviour by roughly the full range.

## LINK 3 — WHERE THAT LANDS
A May 2026 Hacker News analysis argues AI hallucinations are introducing serious security risks in CRITICAL INFRASTRUCTURE DECISION-MAKING by exploiting HUMAN OVERTRUST in AI outputs. The stated core issue: when models lack certainty they output CONFIDENT-SOUNDING BUT INCORRECT answers, and humans in HIGH-STAKES CONTEXTS FAIL TO APPLY APPROPRIATE SKEPTICISM. It is framed as a systemic risk as AI integrates into critical systems.

## WHY THE THREE BELONG TOGETHER
Each alone is an observation; together they are a mechanism with a lever. The intervention point is LINK 2: a model that expressed calibrated uncertainty would, on Sap's numbers, get verified about 90% of the time instead of trusted about 90% of the time. But LINK 1 says the training pipeline actively selects AGAINST that expression. So the critical-infrastructure risk in Link 3 is not a user-education failure that better warnings would fix — it is downstream of a reward-shaping choice made during post-training.

## WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN
- The chain is an assembly of three separate reports, NOT a single study that measured the whole path. No source here traces a specific critical-infrastructure error back to a specific post-training choice.
- TWO OF THE THREE INPUTS CARRIED ZERO RECORDED SOURCES (the Apple Research and Sap items, both reaching this corpus via a single newsletter citation); the third is a vendor-publication analysis. None is independently replicated here.
- The ~90%/~90% figures are as-reported from one study and should not be treated as a general rate across populations or task types.
- It does NOT claim models are usually wrong, only that expressed confidence does not track correctness and that users read it as though it does.
