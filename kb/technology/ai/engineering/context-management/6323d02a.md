# SelfCompact: letting the agent choose when to compact beats fixed-interval compaction at 30-70% lower cost

Long agent traces — chains of thought plus tool calls — accumulate stale content that anchors later generations and eventually overflows the context window. Standard scaffolds compact at a fixed token threshold, which ignores trajectory structure and can discard partial results mid-derivation or mid-search.

Tianjian Li and colleagues at Johns Hopkins University and Apple introduced SelfCompact (arXiv 2606.23525, covered in The Batch 2026-09-11): an inference-time scaffold with two parts — a compaction tool the model invokes itself to summarize accumulated context, and a lightweight rubric telling it when to fire (a sub-task has resolved, or the trajectory is converging) and when to suppress (mid-derivation, or when stuck).

What was measured: across six benchmarks and seven models, SelfCompact matched or exceeded fixed-interval summarization at 30-70% lower cost. No training is required — the authors' framing is that deciding *when* to compact is a capability a lightweight rubric can supply at inference time. Code is published at github.com/tianjianl/selfcompact.

Foreseeable misreading: this is not a better summarizer. The compaction summary itself is unchanged; the gain comes entirely from choosing meaningful stopping points rather than token thresholds, which is why the result is a cost reduction at matched quality rather than a quality jump.
