# 1Password shipped its own 'govern the stay' doctrine within a month — stated agent-security principles are converging on scoped, monitored, never-handed credentials

Two facts one month apart, same protagonist, trace a principle moving from stated doctrine to shipped product.

June 2026: 1Password CTO Nancy Wang and Cursor Security Lead Travis McPeak articulate the position — do not hand credentials or API keys directly to agents (they leak across contexts); govern the stay, not just the access, because an agent's blast radius is large and continuous monitoring beats one-time grants; consider a separate monitoring model with its own goals to oversee the primary agent and surface only meaningful approval requests. Agents that get stuck become 'creative in dangerous ways.'

16 July 2026: 1Password for Claude ships as a near-literal implementation. Credentials are never exposed to the model, its memory, or Anthropic (the 'don't hand them over' rule). Permissions are task- and session-scoped and do not carry over (blast-radius containment). Biometric approval per request is the 'meaningful approval request' surface. Agentic Mode locks the vault whenever an agent drives the browser, and post-autofill field analysis keeps scanning the page — that continuous scan-during-use, not merely at grant time, is precisely 'govern the stay.'

The inference worth holding: in this space the gap between a vendor's stated security philosophy and its shipped architecture is currently very short, which makes public agent-security doctrine a usable leading indicator of product roadmaps. The corollary caution: because the same firm both names the principle and sells the implementation, the doctrine is not independent validation of the product — it is marketing continuity. Treat convergent guidance from unaffiliated parties (e.g. Cursor's McPeak co-signing while not selling a vault) as the load-bearing part of the evidence.

One piece of the June doctrine is not yet in the July product: the separate intelligent monitoring model with its own goals. 1Password's field analysis is a deterministic scanner, not an adversarial overseer. That gap is worth watching as a falsifiable prediction — if the doctrine-to-product pipeline holds, a monitoring-model layer should appear next.
