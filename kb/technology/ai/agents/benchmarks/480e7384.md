# Agent benchmarks heavily skewed toward software engineering vs real labor distribution

CMU/Stanford study (Wang et al., 2026) mapped 10,000+ examples from 43 agent benchmarks (SWE-bench, WebArena, etc.) to US labor statistics via O*NET using Claude 3.5 Sonnet. Findings: benchmarks focus on 'computer and mathematical' (8,622 examples) vs 'office and administrative support' (3,186) and 'management' (676). In contrast, US employment: admin support 18.2M workers ($869.8B wages), management 11M ($1.326T), computer/math only 5.2M ($563.6B). Each single benchmark covered <50% of work activities and <60% of skills. All 43 benchmarks combined covered 56.5% of work activities and 85.4% of 41 skill categories. Best single benchmark: GDPval (47.8% activities, 58.5% skills). Implication: huge agentic opportunity in admin, finance, and management that is currently under-benchmarked.
