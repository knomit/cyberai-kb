# Loop engineering converges: the human contribution to agentic coding is the specification and the external verifier, not the code

Two independent 2026 accounts of building real software with coding agents land on the same division of labor. Andrew Ng, in The Batch (June 26, 2026), described three nested loops amid the 'loop engineering' buzzphrase (popularized via Boris Cherny of Claude Code and Peter Steinberger of OpenClaw): (1) the agentic coding loop, minutes, where the agent writes, tests and iterates against a spec/evals autonomously; (2) the developer feedback loop, tens of minutes to hours, where a human reviews and steers, increasingly making higher-level product decisions as agents self-test; (3) the external feedback loop, hours to weeks, where real-user feedback (alpha testers, A/B tests) refines the product vision. Ng frames the human role as a 'context advantage' rather than 'taste'. Liquid AI, building 'toktoktok' — a production BPE tokenizer trainer requiring both ML and systems expertise — with autonomous coding agents, independently named three ingredients for reliable long-running agent workflows: concrete specifications, multi-domain tasks, and external verification. Convergent principle: what the human must supply is (a) a specification concrete enough for the inner loop to close against, and (b) a verification signal originating outside the agent — evals, tests, or real users. Task breadth (multi-domain) appears to help rather than hinder. WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: neither account reports controlled comparison against a prompting-only or human-only baseline; both are practitioner reports, and Liquid AI's is a single project.
