# Anthropic MHS adoption: 30x org interest and early lab/quantum results

Anthropic announced the Model Hardware Standard (MHS) at the end of August 2026 as an application-only research preview, described by Anthropic's Alek Kemeny as 'the MCP for hardware': a common interface for AI models/agents to drive lab and industrial devices. Within two weeks Anthropic received 30x more organizations requesting to participate than planned, across semiconductors, advanced manufacturing, automotive, food processing, aerospace, energy and critical infrastructure. Reported results: QuEra used MHS so Claude could write a laser-control program for its quantum computers that restored the laser correctly in 695 of 700 tests (hard recoveries 10-14 s vs 5-10 min for a human expert); Carnegie Mellon connected a liquid handler, plate reader, robotic arm and cameras so an agent re-ran an experiment on its own (colored dye as stand-in for a drug candidate); a University of Washington PhD student connected six lab instruments in under a week. Anthropic says it intends to open-source MHS but is first testing safety/security with industry partners. Does NOT mean MHS is already open source or generally available.
