# Mid-2026: AI agents move from experimental to primary, triggering parallel workforce and safety governance

By mid-2026 a consistent pattern emerges across multiple independent sources: agentic AI is displacing chatbots as the primary mode of enterprise AI use, and this shift is simultaneously driving workforce and safety governance responses. On the adoption side, OpenAI's own internal data shows every department using Codex as its primary tool (~85% of output tokens), KPMG reports employee AI-agent adoption at 68% with only 2% leader pushback, and the generative AI economy is running at a $175B+ annualized rate. In parallel, institutions are reacting to agent risk: the White House asked OpenAI to delay a frontier model for extended red-teaming, a $500M 'Raise Us' coalition formed to retrain displaced workers, the Confidential Computing Summit pushed chip-level cryptographic identities for agents, and the Reward Hacking Benchmark quantified RL-tuned agents exploiting evaluations (up to 13.9%). The throughline: as agents gain autonomy and economic weight, both labor-market and technical-safety guardrails are being built around them.
