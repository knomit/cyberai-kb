# Single-stream decode throughput: optimizing within autoregressive decoding has a measured ceiling, while parallel-decoding paradigms report far higher absolute rates — but the two are not directly comparable

Three 2026 datapoints together locate where the remaining headroom in single-stream token throughput sits, and the useful part is the asymmetry between optimizing WITHIN a paradigm and CHANGING the paradigm.

WITHIN AUTOREGRESSIVE DECODING, the ceiling is now measured, not hypothetical. Cohere's decode 'megakernel' serving engine for North Mini Code reaches 292 tokens per second AT BATCH SIZE 1, which it reports as 62% of theoretical speed-of-light and 1.58x faster than vLLM, with the margin holding out to 256K context and NO MEASURABLE ACCURACY LOSS. Because speed-of-light is the bound being measured against, the remaining engineering headroom inside this paradigm at batch size 1 is capped at roughly 1.6x more — whatever else kernel work achieves, it cannot exceed the bound it is already 62% of.

UNDER PARALLEL/NON-AUTOREGRESSIVE DECODING, reported absolute rates are considerably higher. DiffusionGemma adapted Gemma 4 into a discrete diffusion model refining 256-token blocks in parallel, reaching roughly 1,500 output tokens per second on a single NVIDIA H100. Inception Labs' Mercury 2.5, described as the largest diffusion language model ever trained, outputs 1,107 tokens per second on widely available Nvidia GPUs with a 260K-token context window, launch-priced at $0.04 per million input and $0.15 per million output tokens.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN — this is the trap and it is easy to fall into. These numbers are NOT a like-for-like comparison and must not be quoted as 'diffusion is ~4x faster than optimized autoregressive'. The three figures come from different models, of different sizes and capability levels, on different hardware, measured under different conditions, and none of the three sources reports a matched-quality head-to-head. The Cohere figure is explicitly a batch-size-1 measurement with a stated no-accuracy-loss claim; the two diffusion figures carry no comparable quality control. The defensible joint claim is narrow: the autoregressive path has a KNOWN and nearly-exhausted bound at batch size 1, whereas the parallel-decoding path reports throughputs in a different range and its own bound has not been characterized here. Nothing in these sources establishes that diffusion language models match autoregressive quality, nor what share of deployments use either.
