# Latency and throughput are distinct axes of AI 'speed' with different hardware determinants

Two different measurements travel under the word 'speed' in AI serving. Latency (time-to-first-token) is the wait before a response begins; throughput is tokens per second once generation starts. They are affected by different parts of the stack, and a model can be excellent at one and poor at the other — GPT-5.6 Sol at max reasoning posts 97.2 seconds to first token (versus 14.5s for GPT-5.5 at high reasoning) while Cerebras' Ultrafast tier targets throughput specifically via on-chip SRAM weight residency. Practical thresholds: human conversation runs on turn gaps of roughly 0.3–1 second, so a voice assistant pausing more than about a second feels broken; for coding agents, multi-minute waits are long enough to induce context-switching cost; always-on monitoring agents need low latency to act within the event window.
