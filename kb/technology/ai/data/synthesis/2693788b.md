# AI training data collection creating multi-front consent crisis (2026)

Three distinct vectors of AI training data collection are simultaneously producing consent crises across different stakeholder groups in 2026: (1) Crowdsourced physical-world data — DoorDash Tasks pays gig workers to capture spatial/behavioral/audio data for robot training; Pokémon Go spatial imagery (hundreds of millions of players) has already been repurposed as Visual Positioning System data for delivery robots — with no consent from individuals incidentally captured. (2) Enterprise behavioral surveillance — Meta installing keystroke, click, and mouse-movement tracking on US employee systems explicitly for AI model training; GitHub CLI now collecting anonymous usage telemetry by default. (3) Web content extraction at scale — Reddit charging $12M+/year for API access and prohibiting AI training without license; Wikipedia/Wikimedia Foundation requiring machine-readable attribution for AI systems using its content; both cite 'content theft at scale' as primary driver. Structural pattern: all three vectors show incumbents asserting new data rights over existing relationships (gig workers, employees, content creators/readers) rather than designing new consent mechanisms. The data collection velocity for AI training is systematically outpacing consent frameworks designed for narrower, earlier data-use paradigms. Key diagnostic: regulatory pressure for AI-specific data consent frameworks is structurally predictable within 2–3 electoral cycles, with 'employee surveillance' and 'children's data' narratives most likely to drive early legislative action (per narrative cost asymmetry methodology kb/meta/reasoning/08a48cb3.md).
