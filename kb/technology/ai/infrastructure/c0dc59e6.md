# Through 2026 OpenAI moved down into silicon, networking and power while CUTTING its compute spending target — integration substituting for purchased scale, not supplementing it

Two OpenAI moves ran in opposite directions through 2026, and reading either alone gives the wrong picture.

RETRENCHMENT ON PURCHASED CAPACITY.
- OpenAI narrowed its compute spending target from $1.4 TRILLION to $600 BILLION by 2030 (stated in two separate records).
- Capacity originally built for Oracle and OpenAI Stargate in Abilene, Texas was ABANDONED after both walked away; Microsoft agreed to rent that orphaned 700 MW from Crusoe Energy instead, and separately committed $50B in server-farm leases.
- On the Ohio campus, Nvidia originally planned to commit $250B as financial backstop but LOWERED its guarantee to less than $120B to address investor concerns about its risk exposure — leaving OpenAI to decide later how to finance the remainder.
- OpenAI shut down its Sora video platform to free compute for flagship models.

SIMULTANEOUS INTEGRATION DOWN THE STACK.
- SILICON: Jalapeño, OpenAI's first-generation 'intelligence processor' for LLM inference, built with Broadcom over nine months as the first chip in a broader joint compute platform, following its October 2025 Broadcom partnership aiming at 10 GW of custom accelerators.
- NETWORKING: MRC (Multipath Reliable Connection), a protocol two years in development, already deployed in OpenAI's and Microsoft's largest training clusters and used to train GPT-5.5.
- POWER: talks to buy electricity from Helion Energy starting at ~12.5% of Helion's production, targeting 5 GW by 2030 and 50 GW by 2035.
- SELLING CERTAINTY UPWARD: Guaranteed Capacity (20 May 2026) offers enterprises 1–3 year compute access commitments with duration-based discounts. Sam Altman: 'Customers are increasingly asking us for certainty on capacity. As models get better, we expect that the world will be capacity-constrained for some time.'

THE READING. OpenAI is buying LESS capacity outright while owning MORE of what produces it, and is monetizing scarcity by selling access certainty to customers rather than only consuming it. Integration is operating as a substitute for scale of purchase. Power and grid capacity, not chips alone, are the binding constraint — Morgan Stanley analysts projected AI appetite could leave the US with a 13 GW power shortfall by 2028, and a 'ratepayer protection pledge' signed with the US government requires AI companies to bear the costs of new electricity generation.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN — load-bearing, and the first item cuts hardest against the obvious reading:
- The MRC specification was released via the Open Compute Project under an OPEN LICENCE, and OpenAI explicitly does NOT treat it as a competitive differentiator, stating it wants to move the whole industry off fragmented proprietary networking. At least one layer of this integration is deliberately NOT a moat, so the pattern must not be summarized as straightforward vertical foreclosure.
- The Jalapeño performance claim ('performance-per-watt substantially better than current state-of-the-art') is OpenAI's own, from early testing on engineering samples, not independently benchmarked.
- The Helion arrangement was IN TALKS, not closed. Altman is a Helion backer; he stepped down from its board and recused himself from deal discussions, and that conflict should be carried with any citation.
- The Ohio deal was 'close to closing', not closed, and the 10 GW figure comes from a thinner earlier report while the backstopped commitment covers only the ~5 GW first phase.
- The $1.4T-to-$600B revision and the Sora shutdown are newsletter-reported; the Sora record in particular carries low confidence and no independent sources.
- Reduced spending targets do NOT indicate slowing demand. The same sources state AI-fuelled data-centre demand is not slowing and that unclaimed power capacity is being competed for aggressively — Oracle's higher debt-to-earnings ratio is cited as limiting ITS ability to fund AI projects, which is a balance-sheet constraint rather than a demand signal.
