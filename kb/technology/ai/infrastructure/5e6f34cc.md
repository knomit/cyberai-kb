# Rising agentic inference cost is the framing vertically integrated AI clouds sell against

A recurring enterprise-AI narrative in mid-2026 pairs a cost projection with an architectural remedy, and the two should be evaluated separately. The projection: a Gartner estimate reported by The Deep View holds that inference costs for running AI agents will grow roughly fivefold by 2028, driven by agentic workflows increasing token usage. The remedy being marketed against it: vertical integration of the inference stack. Crusoe, in a sponsored August 22, 2026 edition of The Deep View, described owning every layer — energy, data centers and cloud infrastructure — to make high-performance inference cheaper and faster, and packages this as a Managed Intelligence portfolio centered on its Intelligence Foundry, comprising Serverless Inference, Tailored Deployments, Self-serve Deployments and Serverless Fine-Tuning.

The evaluative lesson is about provenance discipline. The performance figures attached to such pitches are vendor claims made in paid placements: Crusoe's Michael Yen-Chi Ho claimed its MemoryAlloy technology delivers 9.9x faster time-to-first-token and 5x greater throughput than vLLM on OpenAI's GPT-OSS-120B — not independently verified, and stated against one baseline on one model. Open-source contribution claims (a tokenizer called 'fastokens' said to be integrated into vLLM, SGLang and NVIDIA Dynamo) are checkable in a way the benchmark numbers are not, and are the better signal to verify. The Gartner figure itself reaches the reader secondhand through a newsletter rather than from a Gartner publication.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: it does not establish that vertical integration in fact lowers inference cost, that MemoryAlloy's speedups replicate outside the vendor's test, or that the fivefold projection is sound. It records that the cost-growth framing and the vertical-integration remedy travel together in 2026 enterprise AI marketing, and that a serving-provider choice should be driven by the buyer's own workload and service-level objectives rather than by vendor benchmark ratios.
