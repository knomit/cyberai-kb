# NVIDIA's NeMo Switchyard is an open-source per-step router for agentic workloads, reported to cut task cost to about one third

MERGED from two records of the same mid-August 2026 NeMo Switchyard release. One carried the release framing and NVIDIA's rationale, the other the measured cost claim and the companion model launch; both are preserved.

WHAT IT IS. NeMo Switchyard is an OPEN-SOURCE library, released by NVIDIA in mid-August 2026, for smart routing of AI agent workloads across models within popular agent tools. It sends EACH STEP of an agentic workflow to whichever available model best fits that step's speed, quality, or cost needs. It lets enterprises build routers meeting their own needs, lowering inference costs WITHOUT requiring applications to be rebuilt.

THE COST CLAIM. NVIDIA reports that routing through Switchyard cut task-completion cost to ABOUT ONE THIRD of running every step on Claude Opus 4.8 alone.

WHY IT IS NOT ONLY ABOUT COST. Kari Briski, NVIDIA's VP of generative AI software for enterprise, noted routing to domain- or task-specific models yields FASTER RESPONSES, GREATER TOKEN EFFICIENCY, and in some cases HIGHER DOMAIN ACCURACY — not just cost savings.

COMPANION RELEASE. Alongside Switchyard, NVIDIA released Nemotron 3.5 Lightning. Artificial Analysis measured ~302 output tokens/second across providers with 7.5s time to first token; NVIDIA claims up to 4x faster output than class peers and 30% faster agentic task completion.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: the one-third cost figure, the 4x output-speed claim, and the 30% task-completion claim are NVIDIA'S OWN, not independently audited; only the ~302 tokens/second and 7.5s time-to-first-token figures are attributed to Artificial Analysis. A per-token or per-task cost reduction does not establish a reduction in cost per SUCCESSFUL task — see kb/technology/ai/economics/553272ed.md.
