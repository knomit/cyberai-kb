# OpenAI MRC: Multipath Reliable Connection Protocol for GPU Clusters

OpenAI, in collaboration with researchers from Microsoft, AMD, Broadcom, Nvidia, and Intel, published a paper debuting MRC (Multipath Reliable Connection), a new compute networking protocol designed to make GPU clusters faster, more reliable, and energy-efficient. MRC addresses two core problems in AI infrastructure networks: congestion and failures.

- **Packet spraying**: Scatters data across hundreds of network paths simultaneously, preventing single-link congestion and enabling 'flatter' networks that reduce data center compute/power.
- **Microsecond failure rerouting**: Detects and reroutes around network failures in microseconds, allowing GPU training runs to continue seamlessly.
- **SRv6 pairing**: MRC pairs with SRv6 (IPv6 Segment Routing) to pre-specify data paths through the network, offloading routing work from switches and reducing their energy consumption.

The protocol has been in development for two years. It is already deployed in OpenAI and Microsoft's largest training clusters (Oracle site in Abilene, TX; Microsoft Fairwater supercomputers) and was used to train GPT-5.5 and other models.

The MRC specification is available via the Open Compute Project under an open license — OpenAI explicitly does not treat this as a competitive differentiator and wants to move the entire industry away from fragmented, proprietary networking approaches.
