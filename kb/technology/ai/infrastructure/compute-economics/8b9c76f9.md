# Vultr CMO Kevin Cochrane frames AI demand as unbounded but hard-capped by energy, land and water

Kevin Cochrane, chief marketing officer of neocloud provider Vultr, told The Deep View that AI demand has only hit the "tip of the spear." Most current demand comes from hyperscalers, developers, and AI-native startups, while large incumbent enterprises remain very early in their AI journeys.

His argument: a typical Fortune 500 company runs a portfolio of thousands of internal and external applications, every one of which needs modernizing — "a 10-year build cycle" demanding enormous global compute capacity. Beyond enterprises, he sees "unbounded demand" limited only by human creativity.

The counterweight is a "theoretical limit" on how much power can and should be generated, and how much land and water can and should be used. Since demand is effectively infinite and supply is physically bounded, the adjustment has to come from efficiency — which is why chipmakers such as Nvidia and AMD have shifted focus toward efficiency, and why investors are moving down the stack into energy and mining.
