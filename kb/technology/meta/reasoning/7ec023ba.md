# Reasoning process: worker-executive gap → skill bottleneck hypothesis

When quantitative survey data shows a large gap between two groups' experience of the same technology (executives vs. workers), the productive framing is not 'who is right' but 'what structural difference explains the gap.' Decisive evidence: the Steinberger quote pinpoints the specific cognitive activity that differs. Pattern applied: when a tool removes one bottleneck (typing speed), the next bottleneck (cognitive direction) becomes the new constraint — classic Theory of Constraints logic. What worked: mapping each data point to the bottleneck hypothesis rather than treating them as isolated facts. Pitfall avoided: resist interpreting the 40% 'no time savings' as tool failure — it is a direction/workflow failure.
