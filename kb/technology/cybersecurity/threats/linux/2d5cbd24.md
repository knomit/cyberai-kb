# Hypothesis: PamDOORa's $1,600 Price Point Drives PAM-Based SSH Credential Theft Into Top-10 Threat Pattern by Q4 2026

Prediction: By Q4 2026, at least 2 major threat intelligence reports (Mandiant, CrowdStrike, Elastic, Recorded Future, or equivalent) will identify PAM-based SSH credential theft as a distinct, named emerging attack pattern — driven by commodity availability of PamDOORa and QLNX-family tooling.

Evidence base:
- PamDOORa is openly sold for $1,600 on Rehub Russian cybercrime forum — a price point accessible to mid-tier criminal groups (well below the $10K-$50K range for nation-state tooling). This creates a commodity distribution pathway that accelerates adoption.
- QLNX (Quasar Linux RAT) embeds PAM backdoor source code directly in binary as string literals — making the technique self-documenting for derivative attackers who reverse the binary
- PAM-based access is uniquely persistent: it survives reboots, persists across legitimate sessions, and appears as a legitimate authentication module to standard process/file monitoring
- The convergence of nation-state tooling (QLNX) and commodity criminal tooling (PamDOORa) on the same attack surface historically signals the 12-18 month window before a technique becomes widespread: nation-state actors prove the value, criminal groups commoditize it
- Most Linux administrators do not audit installed PAM modules — the attack layer is under-monitored relative to network-facing and file-system vectors
- Cloud Linux infrastructure (where developer systems and CI/CD pipelines run) is the primary target for supply chain compromise, and these systems are densely concentrated in a small number of cloud providers' infrastructure

Falsification: If no major threat intelligence provider releases a report naming PAM-based credential theft as an emerging or significant attack pattern by Dec 31, 2026, hypothesis fails.

Load-bearing gap: Whether the $1,600 price point is low enough to overcome the technical barrier of correctly deploying a PAM module replacement without disrupting authentication on the target system — if deployment failure rate is high, criminal adoption stalls despite low price.
