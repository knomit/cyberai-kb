# Hypothesis: A SolarWinds-Scale Supply Chain Compromise Will Occur via an AI Developer Tool by End of 2026

Prediction: By 31 Dec 2026, the Mini Shai-Hulud / TeamPCP supply chain attack wave will be confirmed as a SolarWinds-scale compromise event.

## Criteria (REVISED 2026-07-15 — see Criteria defect below)

All three must hold:
(a) 3+ major developer infrastructure platforms compromised.
(b) Confirmed downstream victim count of **1,000+ distinct ORGANIZATIONS**, reported as organizations by a named source. Package, version, download, developer, and repository counts DO NOT satisfy this criterion.
(c) Attribution of the wave specifically to a persistent, sophisticated threat actor with ongoing campaign infrastructure — not to the now-public technique template.

### Criteria defect corrected
The prior version graded itself on two incompatible bars: criterion (b) required 'thousands of organizations' while the falsifiable signal accepted '500+'. A hypothesis carrying both a hard bar and an easy bar will in practice settle on the easy one. Pinned to a single threshold (1,000+ orgs) as of 2026-07-15.

## Status by criterion (as of 2026-07-15)

**(a) MET, and strengthened.** npm, PyPI, GitHub Actions, VS Code extension marketplace, plus expansion to RubyGems and PHP/Packagist. Registry-hopping is deliberate and demonstrated.

**(b) NOT MET — and may be unmeasurable.** Every figure the industry produces counts packages, versions, downloads, developers, or repositories: 502 unique packages / 1,055 versions campaign-wide; 639 malicious versions across 323 packages in ~1 hour (May 19, 2026); ~1,800 developers; 1,800+ repos holding stolen credentials; 2,500+ compromised GitHub repos; ~518M cumulative downloads. None of these is an organization count. The best available organization-level enumeration is the named-victim list — SAP, Lightning, Intercom, Mistral AI, UiPath, Guardrails AI, Grafana, TanStack, Trivy, LiteLLM, GitHub, European Commission — roughly a dozen, three orders of magnitude below the bar.

**(c) MET but WEAKENED.** TeamPCP has sustained infrastructure across months and platforms. However, OX Security reports copycat actors deploying Shai-Hulud clones, so wave-level attribution can no longer rest on tradecraft alone.

## Confidence: 0.85 → 0.50 (2026-07-15)

1. **The named load-bearing signal resolved against the hypothesis.** The prior version stated disconfirmation follows if 'GitHub's investigation concludes stolen repos were internal-only with no customer exposure and the attack wave ceases.' On 2026-05-20 GitHub stated exactly the first half: breach 'detected and contained,' limited to internal repositories, no customer data affected, credentials rotated same-day. Critically, this statement PREDATES the prior 0.82→0.85 raise dated 2026-06-30 — the raise was made on evidence that was already available and cut the other way. Treat the prior confidence as unreliable rather than as a considered prior. Caveats: GitHub's is a first-party statement made mid-investigation with a fuller report still pending, and the wave has NOT ceased — so the disconfirming condition is only half-satisfied.

2. **Criterion (b) remains unmet after two additional months of evidence maturity.** Absence of org-level counts is now more informative than it was in June; the reporting ecosystem has had ample time and is not producing this number.

3. **Copycats weaken criterion (c).**

Against lowering further: the campaign is vigorous and expanding (RubyGems, PHP; SAP, Intercom, European Commission), and GitHub's full report could still disclose customer exposure.

## MEASURABILITY RISK (load-bearing)
Criterion (b) may be **unmeasurable in practice**. No source is producing organization-level victim counts for this campaign and none appears forthcoming; the industry's native units are packages and downloads. If so, this hypothesis fails by default on 2026-12-31 regardless of what actually occurred — which would make it a bad hypothesis rather than a false one. Non-confirmation must therefore be read as ambiguous between 'did not reach that scale' and 'reached it, uncounted.' Evidence that would resolve this: any vendor, CERT, or CISA publication enumerating affected organizations rather than artifacts.

## SPECIFICATION CRITIQUE — 'SolarWinds-scale' may be the wrong frame
SolarWinds' defining properties were a **signed update from a trusted vendor** delivered to enterprise customers, followed by **selective hands-on-keyboard espionage** against roughly 100 high-value targets out of ~18,000 downloads. Mini Shai-Hulud is an **automated credential-stealing worm** with a **criminal extortion motive** (stolen GitHub source listed at $50,000, with a leak threat). It is plausibly LARGER in raw reach and clearly DIFFERENT IN KIND. Scale alone does not make it a SolarWinds rhyme.

The one property that genuinely does rhyme is absent from the original criteria: TeamPCP published malicious packages through TanStack's own GitHub Actions release pipeline using hijacked OIDC tokens, yielding packages bearing **valid SLSA Build Level 3 provenance attestations** — the first documented npm worm producing validly-attested malicious artifacts. That is trusted-build-pipeline subversion, i.e. the actual SolarWinds mechanism, and it is measurable. A successor hypothesis built on provenance defeat rather than victim headcount would likely be sharper than this one; retained here rather than retracted so the 2026-12-31 settlement stays on the record.

## Falsification
CONFIRMED if a named source reports 1,000+ distinct affected organizations AND (a) and (c) hold at settlement. DISCONFIRMED if no such organization count exists by 2026-12-31 — subject to the measurability caveat above, which should be recorded alongside any disconfirmation rather than treated as a clean negative.

## WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN
A disconfirmation would NOT mean the campaign was minor, contained, or unimportant. It compromised six ecosystems, defeated SLSA L3 provenance, breached GitHub itself, and reached the European Commission. It would mean only that the SolarWinds comparison was the wrong measuring stick.
