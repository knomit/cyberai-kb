# Hypothesis: A SolarWinds-Scale Supply Chain Compromise Will Occur via an AI Developer Tool by End of 2026

Prediction: By 31 Dec 2026, the Mini Shai-Hulud / TeamPCP supply chain attack wave will be confirmed as a SolarWinds-scale compromise event.

## Criteria (REVISED 2026-07-15 — see Criteria defect below)

All three must hold:
(a) 3+ major developer infrastructure platforms compromised.
(b) Confirmed downstream victim count of **1,000+ distinct ORGANIZATIONS**, reported as organizations by a named source. Package, version, download, developer, and repository counts DO NOT satisfy this criterion.
(c) Attribution of the wave specifically to a persistent, sophisticated threat actor with ongoing campaign infrastructure — not to the now-public technique template.

### Criteria defect corrected
The prior version graded itself on two incompatible bars: criterion (b) required 'thousands of organizations' while the falsifiable signal accepted '500+'. A hypothesis carrying both a hard bar and an easy bar will in practice settle on the easy one. Pinned to a single threshold (1,000+ orgs) as of 2026-07-15.

## Status by criterion (as of 2026-08-17 — supersedes the 2026-07-15 status below)

**(a) MET.** Unchanged: npm, PyPI, GitHub Actions, VS Code extension marketplace, RubyGems, PHP/Packagist.

**(b) NOW ARGUABLY MET — two named sources independently produced organization-level counts above the bar.**
- SOCRadar (2026-08-13): 'Potential exposure has been reconstructed across 2,500+ organizations and about 434,000 captured CI/CD files,' spanning six CI/CD platforms, skewing European and Latin American by primary domain. SOCRadar explicitly qualifies: 'These figures describe reconstructed exposure rather than a confirmed victim count.'
- Hudson Rock (independent analysis of the raw 153GB unredacted dataset): 'Our independent analysis of the raw, unredacted data confirms the staggering scale of this compromise, mapping 118,829 attributed pipeline runs to nearly 2,500 distinct organizations.' Named affected organizations include Orange, Boeing, and Roku.

The two sources converge on ~2,500 organizations but differ on evidentiary type. SOCRadar's is reconstructed exposure; Hudson Rock's is attribution of pipeline runs found inside the exfiltrated data, which is a stronger claim — those organizations' CI/CD secrets are demonstrably in the attacker's possession, and SOCRadar notes the harvested data is being brokered on Telegram bundled with other TeamPCP stages and tied to a ransomware affiliate program. Reading Hudson Rock's attributed-pipeline-run mapping as a confirmed victim count is defensible; reading criterion (b) strictly against SOCRadar's own disclaimer is also defensible. Graded here as met-on-balance, not cleanly met.

**(c) MET and re-strengthened.** Google now tracks TeamPCP formally as UNC6780, and SOCRadar places the LiteLLM compromise within the wider TeamPCP campaign that first compromised the Trivy scanner — a specific actor with a multi-stage, multi-month chain, not the public technique template. The ecosystem flaw is tracked as CVE-2026-33634, added to CISA's KEV catalog on 2026-03-26. The copycat concern from July stands but does not touch this particular chain, which is attributed to TeamPCP directly.

## MEASURABILITY RISK — RESOLVED (2026-08-17)
The July version flagged that criterion (b) 'may be unmeasurable in practice' because 'no source is producing organization-level victim counts for this campaign and none appears forthcoming; the industry's native units are packages and downloads,' and warned the hypothesis might 'fail by default on 2026-12-31 regardless of what actually occurred.' That risk has now resolved against itself: two named sources produced organization-level enumerations within five days of each other, one of them derived from the raw stolen data. The identified resolving evidence was 'any vendor, CERT, or CISA publication enumerating affected organizations rather than artifacts' — SOCRadar and Hudson Rock both did exactly that. A settlement on 2026-12-31 will therefore be a real grade, not an artifact of the industry's counting conventions, and the ambiguity caveat attached to non-confirmation should be dropped.

## SPECIFICATION CRITIQUE — 'SolarWinds-scale' may be the wrong frame
SolarWinds' defining properties were a **signed update from a trusted vendor** delivered to enterprise customers, followed by **selective hands-on-keyboard espionage** against roughly 100 high-value targets out of ~18,000 downloads. Mini Shai-Hulud is an **automated credential-stealing worm** with a **criminal extortion motive** (stolen GitHub source listed at $50,000, with a leak threat). It is plausibly LARGER in raw reach and clearly DIFFERENT IN KIND. Scale alone does not make it a SolarWinds rhyme. The 2026-08-17 evidence sharpens this: a 153GB dump of CI/CD secrets from ~2,500 organizations brokered on Telegram to ransomware affiliates is mass credential harvesting at industrial scale, which is not what SolarWinds was.

The one property that genuinely does rhyme is absent from the original criteria: TeamPCP published malicious packages through TanStack's own GitHub Actions release pipeline using hijacked OIDC tokens, yielding packages bearing **valid SLSA Build Level 3 provenance attestations** — the first documented npm worm producing validly-attested malicious artifacts. That is trusted-build-pipeline subversion, i.e. the actual SolarWinds mechanism, and it is measurable. A successor hypothesis built on provenance defeat rather than victim headcount would likely be sharper than this one; retained here rather than retracted so the 2026-12-31 settlement stays on the record.

## Falsification
CONFIRMED if a named source reports 1,000+ distinct affected organizations AND (a) and (c) hold at settlement. DISCONFIRMED if no such organization count exists by 2026-12-31. (The measurability caveat previously attached to this clause is withdrawn as of 2026-08-17 — org-level counts now exist.)

## WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN
A disconfirmation would NOT mean the campaign was minor, contained, or unimportant. It compromised six ecosystems, defeated SLSA L3 provenance, breached GitHub itself, and reached the European Commission. It would mean only that the SolarWinds comparison was the wrong measuring stick. Equally, the 2026-08-17 raise does NOT mean the campaign has been shown to be a SolarWinds rhyme — it means the hypothesis has become gradeable and its numeric bar is now cleared.
