# AI-accelerated exploit timelines will make manual patch cycles obsolete, forcing automated continuous remediation as baseline security hygiene

Given that LMDeploy's CVE-2026-33626 was exploited within 13 hours of disclosure, and AI systems like Claude Mythos can discover zero-days surviving decades of human review, the traditional monthly/quarterly patch cycle is structurally incompatible with AI-speed exploitation timelines. The hypothesis: organizations that cannot achieve automated, continuous vulnerability remediation (target: <6 hours from disclosure to patch deployment) will face systematically higher breach rates within 24 months as AI-generated exploit tooling becomes commoditized among criminal actors. Evidence chain: (1) 13-hour LMDeploy exploit sets empirical baseline for attacker speed; (2) PHANTOMPULSE being AI-generated confirms barrier to entry for sophisticated malware is collapsing; (3) SANS Institute's 'barrier to entry collapsing' validates attacker democratization trend; (4) Claude Mythos finding thousands of zero-days at once implies a future flood of simultaneous CVE disclosures that manual patching cannot absorb. Known gaps: automated patching introduces its own risks (broken deployments, regression); legacy/OT environments may be unpatchable; the hypothesis assumes continued open CVE disclosure norms. Falsification condition: if industry patch response times do not measurably decrease and breach rates do not correlate with patch velocity by end-2027, the hypothesis fails.
